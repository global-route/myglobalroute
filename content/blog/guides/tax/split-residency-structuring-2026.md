Split residency is one of the most discussed - and most misunderstood - strategies in the African professional community. Done correctly, with genuine substance and proper compliance, it is legal tax planning. Done incorrectly, it is tax evasion with serious criminal consequences. This post explains the framework.

## The core concept

Tax residency determines which country has the right to tax your income. Most countries use the 183-day rule as the primary threshold: spend more than 183 days in a country and you are likely tax resident there.

**Split residency** means structuring your time across multiple countries so that you are genuinely tax resident in a low-tax or no-tax jurisdiction rather than in Nigeria.

**The legitimate version:** You genuinely live in the UAE (or Mauritius, or another low-tax jurisdiction) for 183+ days per year, have a real residence permit, real economic ties, and have genuinely ceased Nigerian tax residency.

**The illegitimate version:** You obtain a residence permit in a low-tax jurisdiction but continue living in Nigeria, and claim foreign tax residency to avoid Nigerian tax obligations.

## The substance test - what tax authorities look for

Tax authorities globally are increasingly sophisticated about "paper residency." The OECD Global Forum and individual tax authorities look for substance - genuine physical presence and economic ties.

**Substance indicators:**

| Factor | Strong substance | Weak substance |
|---|---|---|
| Physical presence | 183+ days in the jurisdiction | Occasional visits |
| Accommodation | Owned or long-term leased property | Hotel stays |
| Banking | Primary bank account in jurisdiction | Account opened but rarely used |
| Employment/business | Active work or business in jurisdiction | No local economic activity |
| Family | Family living in jurisdiction | Family remains in Nigeria |
| Social ties | Local friends, clubs, community | No local social network |
| Utilities | Bills in your name | No utility accounts |
| Mobile phone | Local SIM as primary number | Nigerian number as primary |

## The three-country structure (common among African professionals)

Many African professionals with international income use a three-country structure:

**Country 1 - Nigeria (home country)**
- Spend less than 183 days per year
- Maintain family home and ties
- Receive Nigerian-source income (property, investments)
- File Nigerian tax returns on Nigerian-source income only

**Country 2 - UAE or Mauritius (tax residency)**
- Spend 183+ days per year
- Hold primary residence permit
- Maintain primary bank account
- Declare tax residency here
- No personal income tax (UAE) or low tax on foreign income (Mauritius)

**Country 3 - Philippines or other (banking)**
- Hold secondary bank account
- Receive some international income
- Philippines not fully CRS-compliant - lower automatic reporting risk

**The legal requirement:** Each country where you claim residency must have genuine substance. You cannot claim UAE tax residency while spending 200 days in Nigeria.

## The Philippines banking question

The Philippines has not fully implemented CRS automatic exchange as of 2026. This means Philippine banks do not automatically report foreign account holders' information to their home countries under CRS.

**What this means in practice:**
- A Nigerian with a Philippine bank account is less likely to have that account automatically reported to FIRS
- This makes the Philippines a "lower automatic reporting risk" jurisdiction

**What this does not mean:**
- The Philippines can still share information on request under its Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA)
- If FIRS specifically requests information about a Nigerian's Philippine accounts, the Philippines can provide it
- Using a Philippine account specifically to hide income from FIRS while remaining a Nigerian tax resident is tax evasion
- The Philippines is progressing toward full CRS implementation - this situation can change

**The legitimate use case:** A Nigerian who is genuinely tax resident in the Philippines (spending 183+ days there) has a legitimate reason for a Philippine bank account. The non-CRS status is incidental, not the purpose.

## How to structure this correctly

**Step 1 - Establish genuine residency in your chosen jurisdiction**
- Obtain a valid residence permit (UAE employment/investor/Golden Visa, Mauritius Occupation Permit, etc.)
- Spend 183+ days per year in that jurisdiction
- Establish genuine economic ties (accommodation, banking, business)

**Step 2 - Formally cease Nigerian tax residency**
- Notify FIRS of your change of tax residency
- File a final Nigerian tax return for the year you depart
- Consult a Nigerian tax adviser about your ongoing obligations on Nigerian-source income

**Step 3 - Declare correct tax residency to all banks**
- Update your tax residency declaration with every bank where you hold accounts
- Declare your new jurisdiction of tax residency, not Nigeria
- Do not provide false information - this is a criminal offence

**Step 4 - Comply with tax obligations in your new jurisdiction**
- UAE: no personal income tax - file any required returns
- Mauritius: 15% on Mauritius-source income - file annual returns
- Philippines: file returns if you have Philippine-source income

**Step 5 - Manage Nigerian-source income correctly**
- Nigerian rental income, dividends from Nigerian companies, and other Nigerian-source income may still be subject to Nigerian withholding tax
- Consult a Nigerian tax adviser about your ongoing Nigerian obligations

## The risks of getting this wrong

**Criminal penalties in Nigeria:**
- Tax evasion under the FIRS Act: fines and imprisonment
- Money laundering under the Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022: up to 7 years imprisonment
- False declaration on CRS self-certification: criminal offence in most jurisdictions

**Practical risks:**
- FIRS can request information from CRS partners and non-CRS jurisdictions under AML frameworks
- Banks can close accounts if they suspect false tax residency declarations
- Visa authorities can revoke residence permits if substance requirements are not met
- The OECD Global Forum conducts peer reviews - Nigeria's CRS enforcement is increasing

## The honest assessment

Split residency structuring is a legitimate strategy for African professionals who genuinely relocate to low-tax jurisdictions. The UAE, Mauritius, and other destinations covered in this platform's guides are real options for people who genuinely want to live there.

It is not a strategy for people who want to continue living in Nigeria while claiming foreign tax residency on paper. That is tax evasion, and the risk of enforcement is growing.

The question to ask yourself is not "can I get away with this?" but "am I genuinely living where I claim to be living?" If the answer is yes, you are doing legal tax planning. If the answer is no, you are taking a criminal risk.
