The UAE is one of the most popular second residency destinations for African professionals. No personal income tax. A strong passport. A well-developed financial centre. And - critically - a CRS reporting structure that, for genuine UAE tax residents, means your UAE bank account is reported to the UAE rather than Nigeria.

Here is exactly how this works, what makes it legitimate, and where the line between legal tax planning and tax evasion sits.

## How UAE CRS reporting works

The UAE participates in CRS. UAE banks report account information to the UAE Federal Tax Authority (FTA). The FTA then sends this information to the tax authority of the account holder's declared country of tax residence.

**The key variable is your declared tax residency:**

| Your declared tax residency | Where your UAE account is reported |
|---|---|
| Nigeria | FIRS (Nigeria) |
| UAE | UAE Federal Tax Authority |
| UK | HMRC (UK) |
| Germany | Bundeszentralamt fur Steuern (Germany) |

**If you declare UAE tax residency:** Your account is reported to the UAE FTA. The UAE has no personal income tax. The report has no tax consequence for you.

**If you declare Nigerian tax residency:** Your account is reported to FIRS. Nigeria taxes worldwide income of Nigerian tax residents. FIRS may assess you on the income in that account.

## What makes you a UAE tax resident

UAE tax residency is determined by physical presence and economic ties.

**The 183-day rule:** Spending 183+ days per year in the UAE is the primary threshold for UAE tax residency.

**Additional factors:**
- Valid UAE residence permit (employment, free zone company, investor visa, or Golden Visa)
- UAE as your primary place of business or employment
- UAE as your habitual abode (where you actually live)

**What does not make you a UAE tax resident:**
- Having a UAE residence permit but living in Nigeria
- Visiting the UAE for short periods
- Having a UAE bank account without genuine residence

**Primary source:** `https://www.tax.gov.ae/`

## The four routes to UAE residency

| Route | Minimum requirement | Validity |
|---|---|---|
| Employment visa | Job offer from UAE employer | 2-3 years |
| Free zone company | Company registration in a UAE free zone | 2-3 years |
| Investor visa | AED 750,000 property or business investment | 2-3 years |
| Golden Visa | AED 2,000,000 investment or exceptional talent | 10 years |

All four routes provide a UAE residence permit. The permit alone does not make you a UAE tax resident - you must also spend 183+ days in the UAE.

## The legitimate structure

**What genuine UAE tax residency looks like:**

1. Obtain a UAE residence permit through one of the four routes
2. Spend 183+ days per year in the UAE
3. Establish genuine economic ties in the UAE (bank account, business, accommodation)
4. Formally notify FIRS of change of tax residency
5. Declare UAE tax residency to your UAE bank
6. Your UAE bank reports your account to the UAE FTA (not FIRS)
7. UAE FTA has no personal income tax - no tax consequence

**The Nigerian obligation that remains:**
Even after establishing UAE tax residency, you may still have Nigerian tax obligations on:
- Income sourced in Nigeria (rental income from Nigerian property, Nigerian business income)
- Income earned before you ceased Nigerian tax residency

Consult a Nigerian tax adviser about your specific obligations.

## The illegitimate version - what constitutes tax evasion

**Tax evasion occurs when:**
- You obtain a UAE residence permit but continue living in Nigeria (183+ days in Nigeria)
- You declare UAE tax residency to your bank while remaining a Nigerian tax resident
- You use the UAE bank account to receive income that you do not declare to FIRS
- You structure your affairs specifically to hide income from FIRS using non-CRS jurisdictions

**The risk:** FIRS receives CRS data from 100+ countries. Even if your UAE account is reported to the UAE rather than Nigeria, FIRS can request information from the UAE under the exchange of information framework. The UAE cooperates with such requests.

## The Nigerian bank account question

Many Nigerians with UAE residency maintain Nigerian bank accounts. Nigerian banks are not CRS reporting institutions in the same way - they report to FIRS directly under Nigerian law, not under CRS.

**What this means:** Your Nigerian bank account is already visible to FIRS regardless of CRS. The CRS question is about your foreign accounts.

## The substance test

Tax authorities globally are increasingly focused on "substance" - whether your residency is genuine or just on paper.

**Signs of genuine UAE residency:**
- Utility bills, lease agreements or property ownership in the UAE
- UAE school enrolment for children
- UAE health insurance and medical records
- UAE driving licence
- Regular UAE mobile phone usage
- UAE social and professional network

**Signs of paper residency (red flags for tax authorities):**
- UAE residence permit but no evidence of actual living in the UAE
- Continued Nigerian address on all documents
- Nigerian mobile number as primary contact
- No UAE utility bills or lease agreements
- Spending less than 183 days in the UAE
