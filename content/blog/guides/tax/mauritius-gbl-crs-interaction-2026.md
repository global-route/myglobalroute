The Mauritius Global Business Licence (GBL) is the most widely used holding company structure for investments into Africa. It has a well-documented tax structure. But how it interacts with CRS automatic reporting is less well understood. This post explains the two separate reporting streams.

## Two separate CRS reporting streams

When a Nigerian sets up a Mauritius GBL company, there are two separate CRS reporting streams:

**Stream 1 - The GBL company's accounts**
The GBL company is a Mauritius-resident entity. Its bank accounts are held in Mauritius. Under CRS, Mauritius banks report these accounts to the Mauritius Revenue Authority (MRA). The MRA then exchanges information with countries that have activated exchange relationships with Mauritius.

**Stream 2 - The owner's personal accounts**
The owner's personal bank accounts (wherever they are held) are reported based on the owner's declared personal tax residency. If the owner declares Nigerian tax residency, their personal accounts in CRS-participating countries are reported to FIRS.

**The key point:** Setting up a GBL company does not change where the owner's personal accounts are reported. These are two completely separate reporting streams.

## How the GBL company's accounts are reported

The GBL company's Mauritius bank accounts are reported to the MRA as Mauritius-resident entity accounts. The MRA then sends this information to countries that have activated exchange relationships with Mauritius.

**Does Nigeria receive information about GBL companies?**
Mauritius participates in CRS and has activated exchange relationships with many countries. Whether Nigeria specifically receives information about Mauritius GBL companies depends on whether Nigeria and Mauritius have activated a bilateral exchange relationship under CRS.

**The practical implication:** If you are a Nigerian tax resident who owns a Mauritius GBL company, FIRS may receive information about that company's accounts from the MRA. This is not a reason to avoid the GBL structure - it is a reason to ensure your GBL company is properly structured and compliant.

## The beneficial ownership question

CRS requires financial institutions to identify the beneficial owners of entities. For a GBL company, the Mauritius bank will identify the ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) - typically the Nigerian individual who owns the company.

**What this means:** The GBL company's accounts are reported as Mauritius-resident entity accounts, but the beneficial owner's identity (including their country of tax residence) is included in the report. If the beneficial owner is a Nigerian tax resident, FIRS may receive information about the GBL company's accounts.

## The GBL tax structure - unchanged by CRS

CRS reporting does not change the GBL's tax structure. The GBL still benefits from:
- Approximately 3% effective corporate tax rate on foreign-sourced income
- 46 double taxation treaties
- No capital gains tax in Mauritius

CRS reporting means FIRS may know about the GBL company. It does not mean FIRS can tax the GBL company's income - that is governed by tax law, not CRS.

## The legitimate use case

A Mauritius GBL company used for its intended purpose - holding investments across multiple African or Asian markets, benefiting from the treaty network, and managing cross-border investment flows - is a legitimate structure. CRS reporting of the company's accounts to FIRS does not undermine this.

The structure becomes problematic if it is used to hide income from FIRS that should be declared. CRS makes this harder - but the GBL structure was never designed for that purpose.

## What to do

If you own or are considering a Mauritius GBL company:

1. Ensure the company has genuine substance (resident directors, board meetings in Mauritius, principal bank account in Mauritius)
2. Ensure the company's accounts are properly reported under CRS
3. Declare the company's existence and any distributions to FIRS if you are a Nigerian tax resident
4. Consult a Mauritius tax adviser about the company's CRS obligations
5. Consult a Nigerian tax adviser about your personal obligations as a beneficial owner
