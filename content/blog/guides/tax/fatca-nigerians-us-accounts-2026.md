The USA is the most significant exception to the CRS automatic exchange system. While over 100 countries automatically share financial account information with each other, the USA does not participate in CRS. Instead, it uses FATCA - a system that works very differently.

## What FATCA is

FATCA (Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act) is a US law enacted in 2010. It requires:

1. **Foreign financial institutions** (banks, brokers, etc. outside the USA) to report accounts held by US persons to the IRS
2. **US financial institutions** to report accounts held by foreign persons to the IRS (for withholding purposes)

**The key difference from CRS:** FATCA is about US persons globally. CRS is about foreign account holders in participating countries. The USA reports outward (US persons abroad) but does not receive inward reports from other countries under CRS.

**Primary source:** `https://www.irs.gov/businesses/corporations/foreign-account-tax-compliance-act-fatca`

## What this means for Nigerians with US accounts

**Scenario 1 - Nigerian with a US bank account (not a US person)**

If you are a Nigerian national who is not a US citizen, green card holder, or US tax resident, and you have a US bank account:
- Your account is not automatically reported to FIRS under FATCA
- Your account may be subject to US withholding tax on certain types of income (interest, dividends)
- You may need to complete a W-8BEN form for your US bank confirming you are not a US person

**Scenario 2 - Nigerian with a US LLC**

A US LLC owned by a non-US person:
- Is not automatically reported to FIRS under FATCA
- Is a pass-through entity for US tax purposes - profits flow to the owner
- May be subject to US withholding tax on certain distributions
- The owner may have US tax filing obligations depending on the LLC's activities

**Scenario 3 - Nigerian who is also a US person (dual citizen, green card holder)**

If you are a Nigerian who is also a US citizen or green card holder:
- You are a US person for tax purposes
- Your worldwide income is subject to US tax
- Your foreign accounts (including Nigerian accounts) must be reported to the IRS under FBAR (FinCEN 114) and FATCA Form 8938
- This is a complex situation requiring a US tax adviser

## The W-8BEN form

When you open a US bank account or investment account as a non-US person, you will be asked to complete a W-8BEN form. This form:
- Certifies that you are not a US person
- Claims any applicable tax treaty benefits
- Determines the withholding tax rate on your US-source income

**Completing this form correctly is important.** Providing false information is a criminal offence.

## FBAR - the other US reporting requirement

If you are a US person (US citizen, green card holder, or US tax resident) with foreign bank accounts exceeding USD 10,000 in aggregate at any point during the year, you must file an FBAR (FinCEN 114) with the US Treasury.

This is separate from FATCA and applies to US persons with Nigerian, UAE, or any other foreign bank accounts.

## The Nigeria-USA tax treaty

Nigeria and the USA do not have a double taxation treaty. This means:
- If you are both a Nigerian tax resident and a US person, you may face double taxation
- There is no treaty mechanism to offset Nigerian tax against US tax or vice versa
- This is a complex situation requiring advisers in both jurisdictions
