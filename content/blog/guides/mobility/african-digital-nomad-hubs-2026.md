The narrative around African mobility has historically focused on leaving Africa - European study visas, North American skilled worker routes, Australian points tests. But a parallel story is emerging: African countries competing to attract remote workers, digital entrepreneurs and mobile professionals who want to stay on the continent while earning internationally.

Three countries have moved furthest in this direction with formal visa routes: Mauritius, Cape Verde and Namibia. This guide covers what each offers, who each suits, and how to choose between them.

## Why intra-African remote work mobility matters

The AfCFTA (African Continental Free Trade Area) is creating new business corridors across the continent. Rwanda, Mauritius, Ghana and Kenya are positioning themselves as regional business hubs. The Mauritius International Financial Centre, Rwanda's Kigali International Financial Centre, and Ghana's financial services sector are all attracting African professionals who want to operate regionally rather than emigrating to Europe or North America.

For remote workers specifically, the question is not just where to get a visa - it is where to build a base that gives you access to African business networks, reasonable cost of living, reliable connectivity, and a stable legal status.

## Mauritius - the premium option

Mauritius is the most developed of the three options. It has:
- A strong passport (visa-free to EU Schengen, UK, Singapore)
- Reliable island-wide connectivity
- A well-developed financial sector
- A flat 15% income tax rate
- English and French as official languages alongside Creole

The Premium Visa allows remote workers to live in Mauritius for up to one year, renewable. There is no published minimum income threshold, but the cost of living is moderate-to-high by African standards.

Mauritius suits remote workers who prioritise connectivity, financial infrastructure and international mobility. It is more expensive than Cape Verde or Namibia but offers more in return.

## Cape Verde - the Atlantic option

Cape Verde is an archipelago off the coast of Senegal with a Euro-pegged currency, low cost of living and a growing remote worker community. The Remote Work Visa allows stays of 6 months, renewable to 2 years.

The income threshold is approximately EUR 1,500/month - lower than Mauritius or Namibia. The main islands (Sal, Santiago, São Vicente) have reliable connectivity. Portuguese is the official language but English is widely spoken in tourist areas.

Cape Verde suits remote workers who prioritise low cost of living, Atlantic time zone overlap with Europe, and a relaxed island environment. It is the most affordable of the three options.

See the full [Cape Verde Remote Work Visa playbook](/blog/playbooks/professional/cape-verde-remote-work-visa-2026/) for application details.

## Namibia - the Southern Africa option

Namibia is the largest and most sparsely populated of the three. It is English-speaking, politically stable, and has a well-developed infrastructure in Windhoek. The Digital Nomad Visa allows stays of 6 months, extendable.

The income threshold is approximately USD 2,000/month. Namibia is in the UTC+2 time zone - good overlap with European and East African business hours. It is the best option for remote workers with South African business interests or who want access to the Southern African Development Community (SADC) region.

See the full [Namibia Digital Nomad Visa playbook](/blog/playbooks/professional/namibia-digital-nomad-visa-2026/) for application details.

## What is not yet available

Rwanda, Ghana and Kenya are frequently cited as African business hubs but none currently has a dedicated remote work visa equivalent to Mauritius, Cape Verde or Namibia. Rwanda has a long-term residence permit for investors and skilled workers. Ghana has a Right of Abode for diaspora. Kenya has a Digital Nomad Visa in development as of 2026. These routes are worth monitoring but are not yet as straightforward as the three covered in this guide.
