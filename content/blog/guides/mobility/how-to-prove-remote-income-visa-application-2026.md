Proving remote income for a visa application is one of the most common challenges for African digital nomads and remote workers. Consulates are designed to process employment-based applications - they have standard checklists for payslips and employment letters. Remote workers, freelancers and business owners do not fit neatly into those checklists.

This guide covers exactly what documents work, in what combination, for different types of remote income.

## Why remote income is harder to prove

A salaried employee has a simple proof chain:
- Employment letter confirming salary and leave approval
- Payslips showing regular salary credits
- Bank statements showing those credits arriving

A remote worker or freelancer has a more complex proof chain:
- Multiple clients or a foreign employer
- Income that may be irregular
- Payments arriving through multiple channels (Payoneer, Wise, direct transfer)
- No single employer to write a letter

Consulates are not always equipped to assess this complexity. Your job is to make it as simple as possible for them.

## The document set by income type

### Type 1 - Remote employee (employed by a foreign company, working from Nigeria)

**Best document set:**
1. Employment letter from your employer on company letterhead, confirming: your position, your salary in the relevant currency, that you work remotely, and that you have approved leave for the visa period
2. 6 months of payslips showing regular salary credits
3. 6 months of bank statements showing those salary credits arriving
4. Your employment contract (if the letter is not detailed enough)

**Key point:** The employment letter must confirm you work remotely. A standard employment letter that does not mention remote work may lead the consulate to question why you are not applying from your employer's country.

### Type 2 - Freelancer (multiple clients, project-based income)

**Best document set:**
1. 12 months of bank statements showing income credits (use 12 months to show average, not just recent months)
2. 3-5 active client contracts or service agreements
3. Invoices issued to clients in the past 6 months
4. Business registration (CAC certificate if you have a registered business)
5. Cover letter explaining the nature of your income and showing your 12-month average meets the threshold

**Key point:** Bank statements are the anchor. Everything else supports them. If the money is not showing up in your bank account, contracts and invoices alone are not convincing.

### Type 3 - Business owner (running a company, taking salary or dividends)

**Best document set:**
1. Business registration documents (CAC certificate, memorandum and articles of association)
2. 12 months of business bank statements
3. 6 months of personal bank statements showing salary or dividend payments from the business
4. Most recent 2 years of tax returns (personal and/or business)
5. Accountant's letter confirming your income from the business

**Key point:** Show the money flowing from the business to you personally. Business bank statements alone do not prove your personal income.

## The bank statement - what makes it convincing

Your bank statement is the most important document. Here is what makes it convincing:

**Regular credits:** Income arriving on a predictable schedule (weekly, bi-weekly, monthly) is more convincing than irregular large deposits.

**Identifiable source:** Credits that show the sender's name or reference are more convincing than anonymous transfers. If your Payoneer or Wise transfers show as "PAYONEER INC" or "WISE PAYMENTS", that is acceptable. If they show as "MISC CREDIT", that is less convincing.

**Consistent balance:** A balance that stays above the required threshold throughout the statement period is more convincing than a balance that dips below and then recovers.

**No suspicious patterns:** Large deposits appearing just before the application, followed by large withdrawals, raise questions. Consulates look for genuine income patterns, not staged balances.

## Payment platforms - how to handle them

Many African remote workers receive income through Payoneer, Wise, or similar platforms before transferring to their Nigerian bank account.

**The two-step approach:**
1. Show your Payoneer/Wise account statements showing income arriving from clients
2. Show your Nigerian bank statements showing transfers from Payoneer/Wise

This creates a clear chain: client pays Payoneer → Payoneer transfers to your Nigerian bank → Nigerian bank statement shows the credit.

**What not to do:** Submit only Payoneer or Wise screenshots without corresponding Nigerian bank statements. Screenshots of payment platforms are the least convincing form of income proof.

## Handling irregular income

Freelance income is often irregular. Some months are high, some are low. Here is how to handle this:

**Use a 12-month average:** Calculate your average monthly income over the past 12 months. If the average meets the threshold, present it clearly in a cover letter.

**Show the trend:** If your income has been growing, show that trend. A statement that shows income growing from EUR 1,000/month to EUR 2,500/month over 12 months is more convincing than 3 months of EUR 2,500.

**Supplement with savings:** If your income is irregular, show that you have savings that cover any shortfall. A savings buffer of 3-6 months of living costs strengthens your application.

## The cover letter - your most underused tool

Most visa applicants do not include a cover letter. This is a mistake for remote workers.

A cover letter allows you to:
- Explain the nature of your income (freelance, remote employment, business)
- Present your 12-month average income clearly
- Explain any irregularities in your bank statements
- Connect your documents into a coherent picture

**Cover letter structure:**
1. Your name, passport number, and visa type you are applying for
2. Your income type and how it works
3. Your average monthly income over the past 12 months
4. A list of the documents you are submitting and what each proves
5. A statement that you meet the income threshold
