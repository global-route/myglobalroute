# How to Fund a German Blocked Account from Nigeria: The Complete 2026 Guide

The German blocked account (Sperrkonto) is the single biggest financial barrier for Nigerian students applying for a German student visa. You need to deposit €11,208 into a special account before the German embassy will issue your visa.

This guide covers how to get that money from Nigeria into a German blocked account - the providers, the transfer methods, the costs, and the timeline.

**Evidence note:** Transfer fees and exchange rates change frequently. Verify current rates directly with the provider before transferring.

---

## What is a blocked account?

A blocked account (Sperrkonto) is a special bank account that holds your living funds for Germany. The German embassy requires proof that you have €11,208 available - equivalent to €934/month for 12 months - to cover your living costs.

The account is "blocked" in the sense that you can only withdraw a fixed monthly amount (€934) after you arrive in Germany. You cannot withdraw the full amount at once.

The blocked account is **not** tuition. It is living funds proof only. Tuition at German public universities is €0 for most programmes (a semester admin fee of ~€150–€350 applies separately).

## The providers

Three providers dominate the market for international students:

| Provider | Setup fee | Monthly fee | Total first year | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fintiba | €89 | €5.90/month | ~€160 total fees | Most widely used; accepts USD transfers |
| Expatrio | €49 | €4.99/month | ~€109 total fees | Lowest fees; bundle with health insurance available |
| Deutsche Bank | ~€150 | €0 | ~€150 total fees | Traditional bank; requires more documentation |

All three hold the same €11,208 deposit. The difference is setup fees, monthly fees, and the transfer process.

**Recommendation for Nigerian applicants:** Fintiba or Expatrio - both accept international transfers and have English-language support. Deutsche Bank requires more in-person documentation that is harder to complete from Nigeria.

## How to transfer from Nigeria

You have three main options. The cheapest is Wise.

### Option 1 - Wise (recommended)

Wise (formerly TransferWise) offers the closest to mid-market exchange rate with a transparent fee.

**Process:**
1. Create a Wise account at wise.com
2. Verify your identity (Nigerian passport or NIN)
3. Set up a transfer: NGN → EUR (or NGN → USD → EUR if direct NGN/EUR is unavailable)
4. Enter the Fintiba or Expatrio account details as the recipient
5. Fund the Wise transfer from your Nigerian bank account via bank transfer or debit card

**Typical fee:** 1–2% of the transfer amount  
**Transfer time:** 1–3 business days  
**Exchange rate:** Mid-market rate with a small transparent markup

**Important:** Check whether Wise supports direct NGN → EUR transfers at the time you apply - availability varies. If not, transfer NGN → USD first, then USD → EUR in a second transfer.

### Option 2 - Domiciliary account (DOM account)

If you have a domiciliary (foreign currency) account at a Nigerian bank (GTBank, Zenith, Access, UBA), you can:

1. Convert NGN to USD at your bank
2. Transfer USD from your DOM account to Fintiba or Expatrio (they accept USD and convert to EUR)

**Typical fee:** Bank conversion spread (1–3%) + international wire fee (~$25–$50)  
**Transfer time:** 3–5 business days  
**Note:** Nigerian banks apply CBN-regulated exchange rates which may differ from the open market rate

### Option 3 - Western Union / MoneyGram

Available but typically more expensive than Wise for large transfers.

**Typical fee:** 2–4% plus exchange rate margin  
**Transfer time:** 1–2 business days  
**Note:** Check the maximum transfer limit - large amounts may require multiple transfers or additional verification

## Step-by-step: Fintiba from Nigeria

1. **Register** at fintiba.com - provide your name, nationality, university admission details
2. **Verify identity** - upload your Nigerian passport
3. **Receive your Fintiba IBAN** - this is the account number you will transfer to
4. **Transfer €11,208** (or USD equivalent) from Nigeria using Wise or your DOM account
5. **Fintiba confirms receipt** - typically within 1–3 business days of the transfer arriving
6. **Download your Finanzierungsnachweis** - the confirmation certificate you submit to the German embassy

The Finanzierungsnachweis is what the embassy actually checks. It confirms the account is funded and the funds are blocked.

## Timeline

| Step | Time required |
|---|---|
| Open Fintiba/Expatrio account | 1–2 days |
| Identity verification | 1–3 days |

| Transfer from Nigeria (DOM account) | 3–5 business days |

| Download Finanzierungsnachweis | Immediate after confirmation |
| **Total minimum** | **5–10 business days** |

Allow 2–3 weeks to be safe. Do not book your embassy appointment until you have the Finanzierungsnachweis in hand.

## What about the exchange rate risk?

The €11,208 requirement is fixed in EUR. If the NGN/EUR rate moves against you between when you start saving and when you transfer, you need more naira.

**Practical approach:**
- Monitor the rate weekly using Wise's rate tracker
- Transfer when the rate is favourable - do not wait for a "perfect" rate that may never come
- Budget a 5–10% buffer above the current naira equivalent to absorb rate movement

---

## After you arrive in Germany

Once you arrive and register your address (Anmeldung), you can access your blocked account. Fintiba and Expatrio release €934/month automatically. You will need to open a regular German bank account (N26, Deutsche Bank, Sparkasse) to receive the monthly releases.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can my parents fund the blocked account on my behalf?**
Yes - the funds can come from any source. What matters is that the account is in your name and the Finanzierungsnachweis shows the required amount is held.

**What if I cannot raise the full €11,208?**
Some universities accept a sponsor letter from a parent or employer in place of a blocked account - but this is rare and at the embassy's discretion. The blocked account is the standard and most reliable route.

**Can I use the blocked account funds for tuition?**
No. The blocked account covers living costs only. Tuition at German public universities is €0 for most programmes, so this is rarely an issue.

**What happens to the money if my visa is refused?**
Fintiba and Expatrio will return the funds to you, minus their fees. Check the refund policy of your chosen provider before opening the account.
