The African diaspora sends approximately USD 100 billion home each year in remittances. A smaller but growing number of diaspora members are making a different kind of transfer - moving themselves, or at least their business operations, back to Africa.

This is not a single decision. It is a spectrum: from maintaining a UK or US business while spending more time in Nigeria, to fully relocating and establishing a new African business. This guide covers the practical steps across that spectrum.

## The spectrum of repatriation

**Level 1 - Extended visits with remote work.** You keep your existing job or business in the UK, US or Europe. You spend 1-3 months per year in Nigeria, Ghana or another African country. You work remotely during that time. No structural changes required - just a reliable internet connection and awareness of your visa status in the African country.

**Level 2 - Dual base.** You split your time roughly equally between your diaspora country and your African home country. You may restructure your business to operate from both locations. Tax residency becomes a consideration - you need advice on where you are tax resident and what obligations that creates.

**Level 3 - African base with international clients.** You relocate your primary residence to Africa. You continue to serve international clients remotely. Your income is sourced outside Africa. This is the remote worker model - the Mauritius Premium Visa, Cape Verde Remote Work Visa and Namibia Digital Nomad Visa are designed for this.

**Level 4 - Full repatriation with African business.** You relocate to Africa and establish or join an African business. You are employed by or own an African company. This requires the most structural planning - business registration, tax registration, employment authorisation if applicable.

## Practical steps for Level 1 and 2

**Visa status in your African home country.** Most Nigerians, Ghanaians and other African nationals have the right to live in their home country without a visa. If you hold only a foreign passport (e.g., British or American), check your right of entry and residence in your country of origin. Many African countries offer right of abode or citizenship by descent for diaspora members.

**Ghana Right of Abode.** Ghana offers a Right of Abode to persons of African descent. It provides indefinite residence rights without a visa. Applications are made to the Ghana Immigration Service. This is particularly relevant for diaspora members who hold non-Ghanaian passports.

**Nigeria dual citizenship.** Nigeria does not formally recognise dual citizenship, but Nigerian-born individuals who have naturalised elsewhere can apply for a Nigerian passport and travel on it. The practical effect is that most Nigerian diaspora members can enter and reside in Nigeria on their Nigerian passport regardless of their other citizenship.

**Tax residency.** If you spend more than 183 days per year in an African country, you may become tax resident there. This does not necessarily mean you owe tax - it depends on the tax treaty between your diaspora country and your African home country, and the source of your income. Get advice before you cross the 183-day threshold.

## Setting up a business during a visit

**Nigeria:** Business registration is handled by the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC). Online registration takes 1-3 days for a private limited company. Cost is approximately NGN 10,000-50,000 depending on share capital. You can register remotely or in person.

**Ghana:** Business registration is handled by the Registrar General's Department. The process takes 5-10 days. A registered agent can handle it on your behalf.

**Kenya:** Business registration is handled by the Business Registration Service. The process takes 3-5 days online.

**Bank account:** Opening a business bank account in Nigeria, Ghana or Kenya typically requires in-person attendance at the bank. Plan to do this during your visit. Requirements vary by bank but typically include: certificate of incorporation, directors' IDs, utility bill for the registered address, and a minimum opening deposit.

## Property acquisition

**Nigeria:** Foreigners and diaspora members can acquire property in Nigeria. The key document is the Certificate of Occupancy (C of O), which is the primary evidence of land ownership. Engage a Nigerian solicitor for any property transaction - title verification is essential.

**Ghana:** Foreigners can lease land in Ghana for up to 50 years (renewable). Ghanaian citizens can hold freehold. The Land Title Registry handles registration. Engage a Ghanaian solicitor.

**Kenya:** Foreigners can hold leasehold interests in Kenya for up to 99 years. The National Land Commission handles land matters. Engage a Kenyan advocate.

## Transferring funds

For investment amounts above USD 10,000, document the source of funds carefully. Both the sending country (UK, US, EU) and the receiving country (Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya) have anti-money laundering requirements. Keep records of:
- The source of the funds (salary, business income, savings)
- The transfer documentation (bank statements, wire transfer receipts)
- The purpose of the transfer (investment, property purchase, business capital)

Fintech platforms (Wise, Remitly, Grey, Chipper Cash) are efficient for smaller amounts. For larger investment transfers, use a bank wire and notify your bank of the purpose in advance.

## Evidence note

Business registration processes, tax rates and property rules described in this post are sourced from the official government portals of Nigeria (CAC), Ghana (Registrar General's Department), and Kenya (Business Registration Service), retrieved October 2026. Rules are subject to change. Consult a qualified legal and tax adviser in both your diaspora country and your African home country before making any structural decisions.
