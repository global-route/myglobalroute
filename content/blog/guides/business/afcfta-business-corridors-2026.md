The African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) came into force in 2021. It is the world's largest free trade area by number of participating countries. For African entrepreneurs and investors, it creates a strategic question: where do you base your African operations to access the continental market most efficiently?

Four countries have emerged as the leading answers: Rwanda, Mauritius, Ghana and Kenya. Each offers a different combination of regulatory ease, tax efficiency, financial infrastructure and market access.

## Rwanda — the regulatory efficiency hub

Rwanda has transformed its business environment over the past two decades. It consistently ranks first in Africa on regulatory ease metrics.

**Business registration:** 1-2 days via the Rwanda Development Board (RDB) online portal. The process is fully digital. Cost is approximately USD 50-100.

**Tax environment:** Corporate tax rate of 30%. Special Economic Zones offer reduced rates of 15%. No capital gains tax on shares. Rwanda has double taxation agreements with several countries.

**Residency for investors:** Rwanda offers a 10-year renewable residence permit for investors who invest at least USD 250,000 in Rwanda. The Rwanda Directorate General of Immigration and Emigration administers this.

**AfCFTA positioning:** Rwanda hosts the AfCFTA Secretariat in Kigali. The government has made continental trade facilitation a strategic priority. Kigali International Financial Centre (KIFC) is positioning Rwanda as a financial hub for East and Central Africa.

**Practical note:** Rwanda is landlocked. For businesses requiring physical goods movement, logistics costs are higher than coastal hubs. For services businesses and financial services, this is less relevant.

Primary source: `https://www.rdb.rw/`

## Mauritius — the financial and holding company hub

Mauritius has a 40-year track record as Africa's premier financial centre. It is the preferred jurisdiction for holding companies investing into Africa.

**Business registration:** 1-3 days via the Mauritius Business Registration system. Fully digital.

**Tax environment:** Flat 15% corporate tax rate. Extensive double taxation treaty network (46 treaties including India, China, France, UK, South Africa). Global Business Licence (GBL) companies pay an effective rate of 3% on foreign-sourced income. No capital gains tax.

**Holding company structure:** Mauritius is the most common jurisdiction for holding companies investing into Sub-Saharan Africa, particularly for private equity and venture capital. The treaty network reduces withholding taxes on dividends and capital gains from African subsidiaries.

**Residency:** The Occupation Permit for investors requires a minimum investment of USD 50,000 and a minimum annual turnover of USD 100,000 from year 2. The Premium Visa (covered separately) is for remote workers rather than investors.

**Practical note:** Mauritius is an island. Physical presence requirements for GBL companies mean you need a registered agent and at least one resident director. Substance requirements have increased since 2019 — pure shell structures are no longer viable.

Primary source: `https://www.edbmauritius.org/`

## Ghana — the West Africa gateway

Ghana is the most stable democracy in West Africa and the natural gateway for businesses targeting the ECOWAS region (350 million people).

**Business registration:** The Registrar General's Department handles company registration. The process takes approximately 5-10 days. A registered agent can handle it remotely.

**Tax environment:** Corporate tax rate of 25%. Free Zone enterprises pay 0% for the first 10 years, then 8%. Ghana has double taxation agreements with the UK, France, Germany, South Africa and others.

**Investment incentives:** The Ghana Investment Promotion Centre (GIPC) offers incentives for foreign investors including reduced corporate tax rates in priority sectors (manufacturing, agriculture, ICT). Minimum foreign investment is USD 200,000 for joint ventures with Ghanaian partners, USD 500,000 for wholly foreign-owned enterprises.

**Residency:** The GIPC Investor Permit is available to foreign investors who meet the minimum investment threshold. It provides residence and work authorisation.

**AfCFTA positioning:** Ghana hosts the AfCFTA Secretariat's operational headquarters. Accra is positioning itself as the commercial capital of West Africa.

Primary source: `https://www.gipc.gov.gh/`

## Kenya — the East Africa tech and services hub

Kenya is East Africa's largest economy and the regional hub for technology, financial services and professional services.

**Business registration:** The Business Registration Service (BRS) handles company registration. The process takes approximately 3-5 days online.

**Tax environment:** Corporate tax rate of 30%. Special Economic Zones offer 10% for the first 10 years. Kenya has double taxation agreements with the UK, Germany, France, India, South Africa and others.

**Tech ecosystem:** Nairobi's Silicon Savannah is Africa's most developed tech startup ecosystem. M-Pesa, the world's most successful mobile money platform, originated in Kenya. The ecosystem includes accelerators, venture capital, and a large pool of technical talent.

**Residency:** The Kenya Investment Certificate from the Kenya Investment Authority (KenInvest) provides a basis for residence permits for investors. Minimum investment thresholds apply.

**Digital Nomad Visa:** Kenya has been developing a Digital Nomad Visa as of 2026 — check current status with the Department of Immigration.

Primary source: `https://www.kenyainvestment.go.ke/`
