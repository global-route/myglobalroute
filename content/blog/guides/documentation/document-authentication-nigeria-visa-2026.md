# Document Authentication for Nigerian Visa Applicants: Apostille, Sworn Translation and APS Guide 2026

Document authentication is one of the most common causes of visa delays and rejections. The requirements differ by destination country and document type. This guide covers the three main authentication processes Nigerian applicants encounter.

---

## The three processes

| Process | What it does | Required by |
|---|---|---|
| Apostille | Certifies the signature/seal of a Nigerian official is genuine | Most countries for Nigerian-issued documents |
| Sworn translation | Translates a document into the target language by a certified translator | Germany, France, Italy, Austria, Spain and others |
| APS certificate | Verifies Nigerian academic credentials for German visa applications | Germany (for Nigerian applicants) |

---

## 1. Apostille

### What it is

An apostille is a certificate attached to a document that verifies the authenticity of the signature or seal of the Nigerian official who signed or stamped it. It does not verify the content of the document - only that the signature/seal is genuine.

Nigeria is a member of the Hague Apostille Convention. This means Nigerian documents with an apostille are accepted in all other Hague Convention member countries without further legalisation.

### Which documents need an apostille

- Birth certificates
- Marriage certificates
- Academic certificates and transcripts (WAEC, NECO, university degrees)
- Police clearance certificates
- Notarised documents

### How to get an apostille in Nigeria

The Federal Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MFA) in Abuja issues apostilles for Nigerian documents.

**Process:**
1. Obtain the original document (or a certified true copy from the issuing authority)
2. Take it to the MFA Abuja (or a state office where available)
3. Pay the apostille fee (confirm current fee at the MFA - fees change periodically)
4. Collect the apostilled document (same day or next day in most cases)

**MFA contact:** Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Maputo Street, Wuse Zone 3, Abuja.

### MOFA online verification portal

The Federal Ministry of Foreign Affairs has introduced an online verification portal that allows embassies and institutions to verify the authenticity of apostilles issued by the MFA without contacting the MFA directly.

**Portal:** [https://www.mfa.gov.ng/](https://www.mfa.gov.ng/) - navigate to the apostille verification section.

**How it works:**
- Each apostille issued by the MFA carries a unique reference number
- The receiving embassy or institution can enter this number on the portal to confirm the apostille is genuine
- This reduces processing delays caused by manual verification requests

**Practical implication for applicants:** When submitting apostilled documents, include the MFA reference number in your cover letter so the embassy can verify online without delay. Not all embassies use the portal yet - check with the specific embassy whether they require the reference number.

**Turnaround time:** Same-day or next-day for in-person applications at MFA Abuja. Online verification by the receiving embassy is instant once the apostille is in the system.

**Fee:** Confirm the current fee directly with the MFA - fees are updated periodically and are not published on the portal.

**Important:** The apostille certifies the signature of the Nigerian official. If your document was issued by a private institution (e.g. a private university), the institution's signature must first be notarised by a Nigerian notary public before the MFA can apostille it.

### Apostille vs. embassy legalisation

Some countries that are not Hague Convention members require embassy legalisation instead of an apostille. For most destinations covered by MyPathway2Global (UK, Germany, Canada, France, Italy, Portugal, Spain, Ireland, Netherlands, etc.), apostille is sufficient.

## 2. Sworn translation

### What it is

A sworn translation is a translation of a document into another language, certified by a translator who is officially recognised (sworn) by a court or authority in the target country.

### When it is required

- **Germany:** All non-German documents submitted with a visa application must be accompanied by a certified German translation. The translator must be recognised by a German court (vereidigter Übersetzer).
- **France:** Documents in languages other than French must be translated by a translator certified by a French court of appeal.
- **Italy:** Documents must be translated into Italian and legalised at the Italian embassy.
- **Austria, Spain, Portugal:** Similar requirements - certified translation into the target language.

### How to get a sworn translation for Germany

You need a translator who is certified (vereidigt) by a German court. There are two options:

**Option A - Use a certified translator in Nigeria**
Some translators in Lagos and Abuja are certified by German courts. Search for "vereidigter Übersetzer Nigeria" or contact the German embassy in Lagos for a list of recognised translators.

**Option B - Use a certified translator in Germany**
If you have a contact in Germany, they can arrange the translation there. This is sometimes faster and cheaper.

**What to send:** A clear scan or photocopy of the original document. The translator works from the copy and certifies that the translation is accurate.

**Cost:** Typically €30-€80 per page depending on the document type and translator.

### Important: Translation + apostille order

For Germany, the standard process is:
1. Get the apostille on the original document first
2. Then get the sworn translation of the apostilled document

Some embassies accept the translation of the original without an apostille on the original - check the specific requirements of the German embassy in Lagos before proceeding.

## 3. APS Certificate (Germany only)

### What it is

The APS (Akademische Prüfstelle) certificate is a verification of Nigerian academic credentials specifically required for Nigerian applicants applying for German student visas or academic positions.

The APS is operated by the German embassy and verifies that your Nigerian secondary school and university certificates are genuine and meet German academic standards.

### Who needs it

Nigerian applicants applying for:
- German student visas (for university study)
- German research or academic positions

### What it covers

- WAEC/NECO/NABTEB secondary school results
- Nigerian university degrees and transcripts
- Any other Nigerian academic credentials submitted with the application

### How to get it

**Step 1 - Register online**
Register at the APS Nigeria portal (operated by the German embassy Lagos): [https://www.aps-nigeria.de/](https://www.aps-nigeria.de/)

**Step 2 - Prepare documents**
- Original certificates and transcripts
- Certified true copies
- Passport
- Application form

**Step 3 - Attend the APS interview**
You will be invited for an interview at the German embassy in Lagos or Abuja. The interview assesses your academic background and verifies your documents.

**Step 4 - Receive APS certificate**
If successful, you receive the APS certificate. This is submitted with your student visa application.

**Timeline:** Allow 6-8 weeks from registration to receiving the certificate. Do not book your embassy visa appointment until you have the APS certificate.

**Fee:** Confirm current fee at aps-nigeria.de - fees are updated periodically.

### What happens if APS is not satisfied

If the APS has concerns about your documents, they will inform you. You can provide additional evidence. A negative APS outcome does not automatically mean a visa refusal, but it significantly weakens the application.

## Common mistakes

1. **Getting the translation before the apostille** - for Germany, the apostille should be on the original first
2. **Using an uncertified translator** - a translation by a non-sworn translator is not accepted
3. **Submitting photocopies without certification** - most embassies require certified true copies, not plain photocopies
4. **Leaving APS too late** - 6-8 weeks minimum; start before you apply to universities
5. **Not checking the embassy's current checklist** - requirements change; always download the current checklist from the embassy website
