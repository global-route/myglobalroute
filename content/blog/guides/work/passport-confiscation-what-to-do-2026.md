Passport confiscation is illegal in every Gulf country. It is also one of the most commonly documented abuses against migrant workers in the region. The ILO, Human Rights Watch, and Amnesty International have all documented cases across Qatar, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Kuwait and Bahrain.

Here is what the law says and what to do if it happens to you.

## The law in each country

**Qatar:** Holding a worker's passport is prohibited under Qatar Labour Law. Workers can report passport confiscation to the Ministry of Labour (hotline: 16008) or the Workers' Support and Insurance Fund.

**UAE:** Passport confiscation is prohibited under UAE Federal Law. Workers can report it to the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE hotline: 800 60) or through the MOHRE app.

**Saudi Arabia:** Passport confiscation is prohibited under Saudi Labour Law. Workers can report it to the Ministry of Human Resources (hotline: 19911) or through the Musaned platform for domestic workers.

**Oman:** Passport confiscation is prohibited under Oman Labour Law. Workers can report it to the Ministry of Labour (hotline: 1500).

**Kuwait:** Passport confiscation is prohibited. Workers can report it to the Ministry of Social Affairs and Labour.

**Bahrain:** Passport confiscation is prohibited. Workers can report it to the Labour Market Regulatory Authority (LMRA).

## Why employers confiscate passports

Passport confiscation is used to control workers — to prevent them from leaving, changing employers, or reporting violations. It is a form of coercion. An employer who confiscates your passport is signalling that they do not intend to respect your legal rights.

## What to do immediately

**Step 1 — Do not hand over your passport voluntarily.** If an employer asks for your passport on arrival, refuse. You are not legally required to hand it over. If they insist, tell them you will report it to the labour ministry.

**Step 2 — If your passport has already been taken, report it immediately.** Contact the relevant labour ministry using the hotlines above. Do this as soon as possible — delays make the situation harder to resolve.

**Step 3 — Contact the Nigerian embassy or consulate.** The Nigerian embassy in the destination country can assist you. They can intervene with the employer, contact the local authorities, and if necessary issue an emergency travel document.

**Nigerian embassy contacts:**
- Qatar (Doha): +974 4467 4956
- UAE (Abu Dhabi): +971 2 4435 200
- UAE (Dubai): +971 4 397 1800
- Saudi Arabia (Riyadh): +966 11 488 0400
- Oman (Muscat): +968 2469 6100

**Step 4 — Document everything.** Keep records of: when your passport was taken, who took it, any communications with the employer about it, and any responses from the labour ministry or embassy.

**Step 5 — If you are in immediate danger, contact the police.** In all Gulf countries, the police can intervene in cases of illegal detention or coercion. Passport confiscation combined with threats or physical restriction is a criminal matter, not just a labour matter.

## If you need to leave the country

If your employer has your passport and you need to leave:

1. Contact the Nigerian embassy immediately
2. The embassy can issue an Emergency Travel Certificate (ETC) — a temporary travel document that allows you to return to Nigeria
3. The ETC process typically takes 1-3 working days
4. You will need: proof of Nigerian citizenship (any document), a police report about the passport confiscation, and the embassy fee

The embassy exists partly for this purpose. Do not be afraid to contact them.

## Before you travel — prevention

The most effective protection against passport confiscation is to never hand over your passport in the first place.

- Keep your passport on your person at all times
- Make digital copies of your passport and store them in email or cloud storage before travelling
- Know the contact numbers for the Nigerian embassy in your destination country before you travel
- Know the labour ministry hotline number for your destination country before you travel

Write these numbers down and keep them separate from your passport.
