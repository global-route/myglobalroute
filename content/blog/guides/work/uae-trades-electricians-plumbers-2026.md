The UAE construction and facilities management sector employs hundreds of thousands of skilled tradespeople. Electricians, plumbers, HVAC technicians, welders, and pipefitters are in consistent demand — particularly in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, and Sharjah where construction activity is ongoing.

This is a different route from the domestic worker visa. Trades workers are covered by UAE Labour Law, have the right to change employers after one year, and have access to the MOHRE dispute resolution system.

Primary source: [mohre.gov.ae — Work permits](https://www.mohre.gov.ae/en/services/work-permits.aspx)

## The permit

UAE work permits for trades workers are employer-sponsored. The employer applies for the permit on behalf of the worker. The worker does not apply independently.

**Process:**
1. Employer offers a job contract
2. Employer applies for work permit through MOHRE
3. Worker applies for an employment visa at the UAE embassy in Nigeria
4. Worker travels, undergoes medical examination, and receives Emirates ID

**Permit duration:** 2 years, renewable.

**Right to change employer:** After completing one year with the employer, you can transfer to a new employer without the original employer's consent (subject to conditions). Verify current rules at `mohre.gov.ae` — the one-year rule has been updated periodically.

## Salary ranges

| Role | Typical monthly salary (AED) | USD equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Electrician (general) | 2,500-4,000 | 680-1,090 |
| Licensed electrician | 4,000-7,000 | 1,090-1,905 |
| Plumber (general) | 2,500-4,000 | 680-1,090 |
| HVAC technician | 3,000-5,500 | 815-1,497 |
| Welder (certified) | 3,000-6,000 | 815-1,633 |

Most employers provide accommodation and transport. Factor this in when comparing to other destinations — AED 3,000/month with free accommodation is effectively higher purchasing power than the nominal figure suggests.

## Qualifications that help

UAE employers value recognised trade certifications. The following are well-regarded:

- **City and Guilds** (UK) — widely recognised in the UAE
- **NABTEB** (Nigeria) — accepted by many UAE employers, especially for entry-level roles
- **COREN** registration (Council for the Regulation of Engineering in Nigeria) — relevant for electrical engineers
- **Trade test certificates** from NABTEB or state technical colleges

A trade certificate does not guarantee a higher salary but significantly improves your chances of being shortlisted. Employers use it as a proxy for competence when they cannot assess skills directly.

## Where to find UAE trades employers

**Bayt.com** — the largest job board in the Middle East. Filter by UAE, trades/technical roles.

**GulfTalent.com** — professional job board covering the Gulf. Has a trades and technical section.

**Naukrigulf.com** — another major Gulf job board.

**Direct company outreach:** Large construction and facilities management companies in the UAE hire internationally. Companies to research: Emaar, ALEC, Arabtec (now restructured), Khansaheb, Transguard, Farnek, Emrill. Each has a careers portal.

**Recruitment agencies:** Several Nigerian agencies place tradespeople in the UAE. Verify any agency at the CAC register (`search.cac.gov.ng`) and confirm they do not charge upfront fees. Legitimate agencies are paid by the employer.

## What to watch for

**Salary substitution:** Some employers offer a higher salary verbally and a lower salary in the written contract. Read the contract before signing. The written contract is what MOHRE enforces.

**Accommodation deductions:** Some employers deduct accommodation costs from salary. Confirm whether accommodation is free or deducted before accepting.

**Kafala residuals:** The UAE has reformed its kafala system but some practices persist. You should be able to leave the country without employer permission (exit permit requirement was abolished in 2021 for most workers — verify current status at `mohre.gov.ae`).

**Passport retention:** Employers are not legally permitted to hold your passport. If an employer asks to keep your passport, this is illegal under UAE law. Report to MOHRE if this happens.
