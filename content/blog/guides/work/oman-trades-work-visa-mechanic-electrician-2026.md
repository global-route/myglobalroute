Oman has documented demand for trades workers — car mechanics, electricians, AC technicians, plumbers — particularly in Muscat and the northern cities. This is one of the more legitimate Gulf work corridors for African trades workers, but it comes with the same agent-layer risks as every Gulf route.

Here is what the route actually looks like.

## What the demand is

Oman's economy has a sustained need for skilled trades workers in the automotive, construction, and facilities management sectors. The country's Vision 2040 development programme has increased infrastructure spending, which drives demand for electricians and construction trades.

Primary source: Oman Ministry of Labour, mol.gov.om

Typical roles in demand:
- Car mechanic (workshop mechanic, automotive technician)
- Electrician (residential, commercial, industrial)
- AC technician (HVAC installation and maintenance)
- Plumber
- Welder

## What the salary actually is

Salary reality for trades in Oman (Muscat), verified from Oman Ministry of Labour wage data:

| Role | Salary range (OMR/month) | USD equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Car mechanic (entry) | OMR 150-200 | USD 390-520 |
| Car mechanic (experienced) | OMR 200-350 | USD 520-910 |
| Electrician | OMR 180-350 | USD 470-910 |
| AC technician | OMR 180-300 | USD 470-780 |
| Plumber | OMR 150-280 | USD 390-730 |

The offer seen in Nigerian agent channels — 150 Rial per month for a car mechanic — is at the low end of the market but within the real range for an entry-level role. It is not fabricated. The package typically includes accommodation and food provided by the employer, which significantly reduces living costs.

## What a legitimate employer package looks like

For employer-sponsored trades roles in Oman, a legitimate package includes:

- Salary stated in OMR in the employment contract
- Accommodation provided by the employer (shared housing is standard)
- Food provided or a food allowance
- Visa provided by the employer — the employer applies for the work visa, not the worker
- Air ticket provided by the employer for the initial journey
- Return ticket at end of contract

The total value of the package (salary + accommodation + food + visa + ticket) is significantly higher than the cash salary alone. A mechanic earning OMR 150/month with full accommodation and food has a real cost-of-living position equivalent to earning OMR 250-300 in cash without those benefits.

## The visa process

**Employer-sponsored employment visa:**

1. Employer applies for work visa approval through the Oman Ministry of Manpower portal
2. Employer issues offer letter and employment contract to the worker
3. Worker applies for Oman entry visa at the Oman embassy in Nigeria
4. Worker travels, completes medical examination and biometrics on arrival
5. Worker receives Oman ID (Civil Card)

Processing time: 2-4 weeks for employer-sponsored trades visa. Not 24-72 hours as some agents claim.

**The Oman Freelance Visa (Visa No. 2)** — this is a separate category for self-employed professionals. It requires proof of professional qualification, a bank statement showing OMR 3,000 minimum, and health insurance. It is not a route for employed trades workers. Agents advertising "Oman freelance visa" for mechanics and electricians are misrepresenting the visa category.

## Kafala reform — what changed in 2021

Oman reformed its Kafala (sponsorship) system in 2021:

- Workers can change employers without sponsor permission after completing their contract
- Workers can change employers during the contract in cases of employer violation
- Exit permit requirement was abolished — workers can leave Oman without employer permission

These are genuine reforms. Oman's system is now more worker-friendly than Qatar or Saudi Arabia's pre-reform systems. However, the employer still controls your visa during the contract period, and enforcement of the new rules is not universal.

## Red flags in agent offers

The most common red flag in Oman trades offers seen in Nigerian agent channels:

**Agent charging NGN 1.5 million on top of an employer-provided package.** If the employer is providing visa and ticket, there is no legitimate reason to pay an agent NGN 1.5 million. The agent is charging for a service the employer should provide for free.

**"Visa ready in 1 week."** Employer-sponsored trades visas take 2-4 weeks minimum. An agent claiming 1-week processing is either describing a tourist visa (which does not give you the right to work) or fabricating the timeline.

**No employer name provided.** A legitimate job offer comes from a named, registered employer. If an agent cannot tell you the employer's name and Oman commercial registration number, the offer is not legitimate.

## How to verify an Oman employer

Before accepting any Oman work offer:

1. Ask for the employer's commercial registration number (CR number)
2. Verify the employer exists on the Oman Ministry of Commerce portal: mociip.gov.om
3. Get a signed employment contract before travelling — in English
4. Confirm the contract states salary in OMR, working hours, accommodation, annual leave, return ticket
5. Do not pay any recruitment fee — legitimate Oman employers do not charge workers

## The honest picture

Oman is a real work destination for African trades workers. The demand is genuine. The employer-provided package model is standard and legitimate. The salary is modest but the total package value is reasonable for entry-level trades.

The problem is the agent layer. Agents charging NGN 1.5 million on top of employer-provided packages, claiming 1-week processing, and misrepresenting employment visas as freelance visas — this is where workers lose money and end up in debt before they even arrive.

The route is real. Verify the employer, get the contract, do not pay agent fees for services the employer provides.
