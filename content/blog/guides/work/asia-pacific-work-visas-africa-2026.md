The Asia-Pacific region offers structured work visa routes with clear requirements and strong paths to permanent residence. Australia and New Zealand have the most accessible routes for African professionals. Japan and South Korea require employer sponsorship and relevant qualifications.

## Australia - Three routes for different profiles

### Route 1: DAMA Regional Employer-Sponsored Stream

The Designated Area Migration Agreement (DAMA) is Australia's most accessible employer-sponsored route for African professionals. It bypasses the standard points test and offers concessions not available elsewhere.

**Concessions available under DAMA:**
- Age cap extended to 55 (standard skilled visa cap is 45)
- English language score floor reduced
- Expanded occupation lists beyond the standard skilled occupation list
- Salary concessions of up to 10% below the standard threshold

**Requirements:**
- Employer sponsorship from an approved DAMA sponsor in a designated regional area
- The employer must have an active Labour Agreement with the Department of Home Affairs
- You cannot self-sponsor

**Path to permanent residence:** After 3 years of living and working in the designated regional area, you can apply for the subclass 191 permanent residence visa.

**Primary source:** `https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/employing-and-sponsoring-someone/site-agreements/designated-area-migration-agreements`

### Route 2: Points-Tested Skilled Visas (189, 190, 491)

The standard Australian skilled migration routes require a minimum of 65 points on the points test.

**Points are awarded for:** Age (maximum at 25-32), English language, skilled employment, qualifications, partner skills, state/territory nomination (190 and 491 only).

**Key difference between subclasses:**
- 189: Independent, no nomination required, direct permanent residence
- 190: State/territory nomination required, adds 5 points, direct permanent residence
- 491: Regional nomination or family sponsorship, adds 15 points, provisional visa leading to 191 permanent residence after 3 years

**Primary source:** `https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/what-we-do/skilled-migration-program`

### Route 3: Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV) equivalent

Australia's employer-sponsored temporary work visas (Temporary Skill Shortage subclass 482) require employer sponsorship and a salary meeting the Temporary Skilled Migration Income Threshold (TSMIT).

## New Zealand - Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV)

New Zealand's AEWV is the primary employer-sponsored work visa, introduced in 2022.

**Requirements:**
- Job offer from an Immigration New Zealand accredited employer
- The employer must pass a job check confirming the role is genuine and pays at least the median wage
- Median wage: approximately NZD 29.66/hour (2026)
- NZ minimum wage from 1 April 2026: NZD 23.95/hour - the AEWV threshold is higher

**Visa duration:** Up to 3 years, renewable. Some roles qualify for up to 5 years.

**Path to permanent residence:** After 2 years on an AEWV in a role paying above the median wage, you can apply for the Skilled Migrant Category (SMC) permanent residence visa.

**Primary source:** `https://www.immigration.govt.nz/new-zealand-visas/visas/visa/accredited-employer-work-visa`

## Japan - Engineer/Specialist/International Services Visa

Japan's primary work visa for professionals is the Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services status of residence.

**Requirements:**
- Confirmed employment contract with a Japanese public or private organisation
- Relevant degree or 10 years of relevant work experience (3 years for IT roles)
- The role must match your qualification - an engineering degree for an engineering role, a humanities degree for a translation or international business role

**Processing:** Your employer applies for a Certificate of Eligibility (CoE) from the Immigration Services Agency. You then apply for the visa at the Japanese consulate in your country using the CoE.

**Path to permanent residence:** After 10 years of continuous legal residence in Japan (reduced to 5 years for highly skilled professionals under the HSP points system).

**Primary source:** `https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/status/gijinkoku`

## South Korea - E-7 Specially Designated Activities Visa

South Korea's primary work visa for skilled foreign professionals is the E-7.

**Requirements:**
- Confirmed employment contract from a Korean employer
- Bachelor's degree or higher in a relevant field, or 5+ years of relevant work experience
- The employer must obtain prior approval from the Ministry of Justice
- Specific occupation categories have different qualification requirements

**Key sectors:** IT, engineering, healthcare, education, finance, manufacturing.

**Path to permanent residence:** After 5 years of continuous legal residence in South Korea, you can apply for the F-5 permanent residence visa.

**Primary source:** `https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do`
