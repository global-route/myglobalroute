Canada's warehouse and logistics sector is one of the most active LMIA-sponsoring industries in the country. Amazon, Walmart, Loblaw, and third-party logistics companies regularly sponsor foreign workers for warehouse roles. The pay is real, the route is legitimate, and no degree is required.

Here is how it actually works.

## What LMIA sponsorship means

A Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) is a document that a Canadian employer must obtain from Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) before hiring a foreign worker for most roles.

The process:
1. Employer advertises the role to Canadian workers for a minimum period
2. If no suitable Canadian candidate is found, employer applies to ESDC for a positive LMIA
3. ESDC reviews the application and issues a positive or negative LMIA
4. With a positive LMIA, the employer makes a job offer to the foreign worker
5. Worker applies for a work permit using the LMIA number and job offer letter
6. Work permit is issued — worker can travel to Canada and begin work

The employer pays for the LMIA application (CAD 1,000 per position). The worker does not pay for the LMIA. Any agent charging you for an LMIA is charging for something the employer pays.

Primary source: Employment and Social Development Canada, canada.ca/lmia

## The roles and pay

Warehouse and logistics roles in Canada that are commonly LMIA-sponsored:

| Role | NOC Code | Typical pay (CAD/hour) |
|---|---|---|
| Warehouse worker / order picker | 75101 | CAD 18-22 |
| Forklift operator | 75101 | CAD 20-25 |
| Shipping and receiving clerk | 14404 | CAD 19-23 |
| Logistics coordinator | 13201 | CAD 22-28 |
| Truck driver (Class 1/AZ) | 73300 | CAD 25-35 |

The CAD 22/hour figure seen in agent advertisements is accurate for mid-level warehouse roles. It is not inflated.

## What the agent offers get wrong

Several offers seen in Nigerian agent channels advertise Canada warehouse work with "family sponsorship included" and "zero deposit, bank draft."

**Family sponsorship is not an employer benefit.** Family sponsorship (bringing your spouse and children to Canada) is a separate immigration process that you initiate after you have been in Canada for a period and meet the income requirements. An employer cannot include family sponsorship as part of a job offer. Any offer that lists family sponsorship as an employer benefit is misrepresenting the immigration process.

**"Zero deposit, bank draft"** — this payment structure language suggests agent fees will be charged later, after you have committed. Legitimate LMIA-sponsored work does not require you to pay agent fees. The employer pays the LMIA fee.

**"15 slots available"** — no legitimate Canadian work permit process has slots. Work permits are issued per application based on eligibility. Any offer with "limited slots" is a scam pattern.

## How to find legitimate LMIA-sponsored warehouse jobs

Legitimate LMIA-sponsored warehouse jobs are posted publicly. Employers are required to advertise the role to Canadian workers before applying for an LMIA, which means the job posting exists.

Where to search:
- Job Bank Canada: jobbank.gc.ca — the official Canadian government job board
- Indeed Canada: ca.indeed.com — filter by province and role
- LinkedIn: filter by Canada and warehouse/logistics
- Company career pages: Amazon Canada, Walmart Canada, Loblaw, Purolator, FedEx Canada

What to look for in a legitimate posting:
- Employer name and registered address
- Specific role title and duties
- Salary stated in CAD per hour
- Location (province and city)
- Contact email at the company domain (not Gmail or Yahoo)

## The work permit application process

Once you have a positive LMIA and job offer letter:

1. Apply for a Canadian work permit online at ircc.canada.ca
2. Pay the work permit application fee (CAD 155)
3. Submit: job offer letter, LMIA number, passport, proof of qualifications
4. Processing time: 2-8 weeks depending on the visa office
5. Receive work permit — travel to Canada and begin work

You do not need a visa agent to apply for a Canadian work permit. The application is online and straightforward. If you choose to use an agent, verify their RCIC (Regulated Canadian Immigration Consultant) registration at iccrc-crcic.ca.

## Path to permanent residence

After 1 year of full-time work in Canada in a NOC TEER 2 or 3 occupation (which includes most logistics roles), you may qualify for the Canadian Experience Class (CEC) under Express Entry. CEC applicants with Canadian work experience typically receive invitations at lower CRS scores than Federal Skilled Worker applicants.

Some provinces — Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta, Manitoba — also have PNP streams that target workers in in-demand occupations. A provincial nomination adds 600 CRS points, effectively guaranteeing an Express Entry invitation.

## The honest picture

Canada warehouse and logistics work is a real, legitimate route. The pay is accurate. The LMIA process is real. No degree is required. The path to permanent residence exists.

The agent layer is where the problems appear — misrepresenting family sponsorship as an employer benefit, charging fees for LMIA services the employer pays, and using "limited slots" language to create false urgency.

Find the job on Job Bank or Indeed. Verify the employer is registered. Get the LMIA number in writing. Apply for the work permit yourself or through a registered RCIC. Do not pay agent fees for LMIA services.
