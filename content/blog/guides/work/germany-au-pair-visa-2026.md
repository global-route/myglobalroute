Germany's Au Pair visa is one of the most accessible routes for young Nigerians aged 18-26 to spend time in Germany. It is not a work visa - it is a cultural exchange programme. But it provides 12 months of legal residence in Germany, German language immersion, and a foundation for future visa applications.

> **Verification note:** The German Residence Act (Aufenthaltsgesetz) Section 12 provides the legal basis for au pair stays. The specific requirements below are based on the Act and widely-cited consulate guidance. Verify current requirements with the German consulate in Lagos before applying, as consulate practice can vary.

## What the au pair programme involves

**Your role:** You live with a German host family and help with childcare and light household tasks. This is a cultural exchange - you are not a domestic worker or a nanny. The relationship is meant to be reciprocal: you help the family, they provide accommodation, meals, pocket money and support for your language learning.

**Working hours:** Maximum 30 hours per week. You must have free time to attend German language classes.

**Accommodation:** Provided by the host family. You have your own room.

**Meals:** Provided by the host family.

**Pocket money:** Minimum EUR 260/month. Some families pay more.

**Language classes:** You are expected to attend German language classes. The host family typically contributes to the cost.

## Requirements

**Age:** 18-26 at the time of application.

**Language:** Basic German language skills (A1/A2 level). You do not need to be fluent, but you must be able to communicate with the family and children.

**Host family:** You must have a confirmed host family placement before applying for the visa. Use a reputable placement agency.

**No prior children:** You must not have dependent children.

**Health insurance:** Required for the duration of your stay.

**Clean criminal record:** Police clearance certificate required.

## The visa application process

1. **Find a host family** through a reputable placement agency
2. **Sign an au pair agreement** with the host family (this is required for the visa application)
3. **Learn basic German** - take an A1/A2 course in Nigeria before applying
4. **Apply for the au pair visa** at the German consulate in Lagos
5. **Required documents:** Valid passport, au pair agreement, proof of German language skills, police clearance, health insurance, passport photographs, visa application form
6. **Processing time:** Typically 4-8 weeks at the German consulate in Lagos

**Primary source:** German Residence Act (Aufenthaltsgesetz) - `https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg/`

## What the au pair visa does and does not give you

**Does give you:**
- Legal residence in Germany for up to 12 months
- German language immersion
- Experience living in Germany
- A foundation for future visa applications (the Chancenkarte, student visa, or skilled worker visa)

**Does not give you:**
- The right to work for any employer other than your host family
- A path to permanent residence directly
- The right to bring dependants

## The pathway forward

The au pair year is most valuable as a stepping stone. After completing your au pair year:

- **German language:** You will have significantly improved your German, which adds points on the Chancenkarte points system
- **German experience:** Time spent in Germany can add points on the Chancenkarte
- **Network:** You will have contacts in Germany that can help with job searching
- **Chancenkarte:** If you have a recognised qualification, you can apply for the Chancenkarte (job search visa) after your au pair year

## Finding a reputable agency

> Be cautious of agencies that charge very high placement fees or make guarantees about visa approval. Legitimate agencies charge reasonable fees for matching services. The German consulate in Lagos can advise on recognised agencies.

**Questions to ask any agency:**
- Are you registered with the relevant German or Nigerian regulatory body?
- What is your placement fee and what does it cover?
- What support do you provide if the placement does not work out?
- Can you provide references from previous Nigerian au pairs?
