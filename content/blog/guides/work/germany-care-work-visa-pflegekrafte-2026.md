Germany's care sector is in crisis. The country needs an estimated 500,000 additional care workers by 2030. The shortage is more severe than the nursing shortage and the qualification bar is lower.

This is an underserved corridor for African workers with care work experience or qualifications.

## What care work is in Germany

Care work (Pflegearbeit) in Germany covers:

- **Altenpfleger/in** — elderly care worker, 3-year qualification, works in care homes and with home care services
- **Pflegefachkraft** — qualified care professional, 3-year qualification, broader scope
- **Pflegehilfskraft** — care assistant, 1-year qualification or on-the-job training, works under supervision
- **Betreuungskraft** — support worker, works with dementia patients and people with disabilities

The distinction matters for qualification recognition and language requirements.

## Why the bar is lower than clinical nursing

Clinical nursing (Gesundheits- und Krankenpfleger) in Germany requires:
- 3-year nursing degree
- Professional recognition from the relevant German state authority (Landesamt)
- B2 German language level for clinical roles

Care work (Altenpfleger, Pflegefachkraft) requires:
- Recognised care qualification (1-3 years depending on level)
- Professional recognition from the relevant authority
- B1 German language level is accepted for some care roles (B2 for senior roles)

B1 German is significantly more achievable than B2. B1 means you can handle everyday situations and communicate in familiar contexts. B2 means you can understand complex texts and communicate fluently. The difference in preparation time is typically 6-12 months.

## Qualification recognition

Your Nigerian care qualification needs to be assessed for equivalence to German standards. The process:

1. Check the ANABIN database (anabin.kmk.org) to see if your institution is listed
2. Apply for qualification recognition to the relevant German state authority (Landesamt or equivalent)
3. If your qualification is partially equivalent, you may need to complete an adaptation measure (Anpassungsmaßnahme) — additional training or supervised practice in Germany

For care work qualifications, the recognition process is typically handled by the state authority for health and social affairs (Landesamt für Gesundheit und Soziales or equivalent).

## The Chancenkarte route for care workers

The Chancenkarte (Opportunity Card) allows you to come to Germany to look for work without a prior job offer. For care workers:

**Points system:** You need 6 points. Points are awarded for:
- Recognised foreign qualification: 3 points
- German language skills (B2 or above): 3 points
- German language skills (A2-B1): 1 point
- Age under 35: 2 points
- Professional experience in Germany: 1 point
- Vocational training in Germany: 2 points

A care worker with a recognised qualification and B1 German scores 4 points (3 for qualification + 1 for language). Adding age under 35 gives 6 points — the minimum to qualify.

**Minimum funds:** EUR 1,091 per month via blocked account for the duration of the job search period (up to 1 year).

**Process:** Apply for Chancenkarte at the German embassy, come to Germany, find a care employer, convert to a work visa.

## Salary for care workers in Germany

| Role | Typical salary (EUR/month gross) |
|---|---|
| Care assistant (Pflegehilfskraft) | EUR 2,200-2,800 |
| Qualified care worker (Altenpfleger) | EUR 2,800-3,500 |
| Senior care worker | EUR 3,200-4,000 |

These are significantly above the German minimum wage (EUR 12.41/hour = approximately EUR 2,150/month full-time) and reflect the shortage premium in the care sector.

## The honest picture

Germany's care work shortage is real and severe. The qualification bar is lower than clinical nursing. The language requirement (B1 for some roles) is more achievable than B2. The Chancenkarte provides a route without a prior job offer.

The investment required: a recognised care qualification, B1 German (minimum), and EUR 1,091/month in a blocked account for the Chancenkarte period.

For African workers with care experience or qualifications who are willing to learn German to B1 level, this is one of the most accessible routes to Germany available.
