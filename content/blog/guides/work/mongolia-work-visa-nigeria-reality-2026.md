A Facebook post in a Nigerian migration group asked a simple question: "Pls is Mongolia a good country to travel to work bcuz my visa agent said that he can do Mongolian student and work visa for me for 8 million naira."

The post got 267 comments and 47 shares. That engagement tells you how many people are being approached with similar offers.

Here is the honest answer.

## What Mongolia actually is

Mongolia is a landlocked country in East Asia between Russia and China. Its economy is dominated by mining — coal, copper, gold. The major foreign workers in Mongolia's mining sector come from China, Russia and Kazakhstan, not West Africa.

Average monthly salary in Mongolia: USD 400-600. That is comparable to Gulf unskilled wages but with significantly higher living costs and extreme winters reaching -40°C.

Primary source: Mongolian Immigration Agency, immigration.gov.mn

## What the visa categories actually are

Mongolia issues the following visa categories relevant to work and study:

**Work visa (HG):** Requires a confirmed job offer from a registered Mongolian employer. The employer applies for a work permit on behalf of the worker. The worker then applies for the HG visa at a Mongolian embassy.

**Student visa (SU):** For enrolment at a recognised Mongolian educational institution. Does not grant the right to work.

There is no combined student-work visa category in Mongolian immigration law. An agent offering a "student and work visa" is describing something that does not exist.

## Why NGN 8 million is a red flag

Official Mongolian visa fees from the immigration.gov.mn fee schedule: USD 50-100 for most visa categories.

NGN 8 million is approximately USD 5,000 at current exchange rates. That is 50-100 times the official fee.

For context: the Germany Chancenkarte — one of the most credible work routes available to Nigerians — requires EUR 1,091 per month in a blocked account (approximately NGN 1.6 million) plus official visa fees of EUR 75. The total upfront cost is under NGN 2 million for a route that leads to a documented shortage occupation in Europe.

An agent charging NGN 8 million for Mongolia is charging more than four times the cost of a Germany Chancenkarte for a destination with no documented Nigerian labour corridor, lower wages, and extreme climate.

## The WAEC-only profile problem

The user asking the question has WAEC results only — no degree, no trade certification. Mongolia's work visa requires a job offer from a Mongolian employer. No Mongolian employer is advertising for WAEC-only Nigerian workers. The combination of WAEC-only qualifications and Mongolia as a destination has no legitimate pathway.

What WAEC-only qualifications actually open:

- Gulf trades routes (electrician, mechanic, plumber) — if you have a trade skill, not just WAEC
- Canada Francophone caregiver routes — if you speak French at CLB 5
- Some hospitality routes in UAE and Qatar — with employer sponsorship, no agent fee
- Further education to build qualifications for stronger routes

None of these require NGN 8 million upfront.

## The "working and studying" framing

This framing appears in many fraudulent offers. The structure is: pay a large fee, get a student visa, work informally on the side. The problems:

- Working on a student visa is illegal in most countries and can result in deportation and a ban
- The "work" is typically informal, unprotected, and underpaid
- If the student visa is rejected, the agent keeps the fee
- If you arrive and cannot find work, you are stranded with no legal recourse

## What to do if you have been approached with this offer

Do not pay. Ask the agent for:
1. The name of the Mongolian employer offering you a job
2. The employer's registered business number in Mongolia
3. The specific visa category under Mongolian immigration law that covers your situation
4. A signed contract from the employer before any payment

A legitimate agent can answer all four questions immediately. An agent who cannot is not operating a legitimate service.

## The honest picture on Mongolia

Mongolia is not a documented work destination for Nigerian workers. There is no shortage occupation list that includes Nigerian labour. There is no bilateral labour agreement between Nigeria and Mongolia. There is no community of Nigerian workers in Mongolia that would indicate an established corridor.

The 267 comments on that Facebook post are not evidence of opportunity. They are evidence of how many people are being targeted by the same offer.

If your business is not going well and you need to work abroad, the routes that are real and documented are in this platform. Germany, Canada, Ireland, Australia, UAE, Qatar, Oman — all have verified demand records, official source links, and honest cost data. None of them require NGN 8 million upfront.
