The UK Global Talent visa is the only UK immigration route with no salary threshold and no employer sponsorship requirement. It is gated entirely by endorsement — a formal assessment by an approved body that you are a leader or emerging leader in your field.

For African professionals in tech, research, arts or academia, it is worth understanding precisely because it bypasses the GBP 38,700 Skilled Worker salary threshold that blocks many applicants.

## The two tiers

**Exceptional talent** — for established leaders. You have a proven track record: significant publications, a major tech product, recognised creative work, or a senior research position. Settlement eligibility after 3 years.

**Exceptional promise** — for emerging leaders. You show strong potential and early evidence of impact, but have not yet reached the top of your field. Settlement eligibility after 5 years.

Both tiers use the same application process. The endorsing body decides which tier you qualify for based on your evidence.

## The six endorsing bodies

| Field | Endorsing body |
|---|---|
| Digital technology | UKRI (formerly Tech Nation) |
| Science, engineering, humanities, medicine | UKRI (UK Research and Innovation) |
| Arts and culture | Arts Council England |
| Film, TV, animation, games, post-production | BFI (British Film Institute) |
| Fashion and textiles | British Fashion Council |
| Architecture | Royal Institute of British Architects (RIBA) |

**Important:** Tech Nation ceased operations in April 2023. The digital technology endorsement route was transferred to UKRI. Verify the current process at gov.uk/global-talent before applying — the endorsement criteria and application portal may have been updated.

## What endorsers assess

Each endorsing body publishes its own criteria. For UKRI (covering tech and research), the assessment looks at:

**For exceptional talent:**
- Recognised as a leading talent in the digital technology sector
- Evidence of innovation as a founder or senior employee of a product-led digital technology company
- Proof of recognition beyond your employer — awards, press coverage, speaking at major conferences, open source contributions with significant adoption

**For exceptional promise:**
- Evidence of emerging talent — early-stage but high-potential work
- Academic or professional recognition
- Demonstrable impact in your field even if not yet at leadership level

The endorsing body does not assess your visa eligibility — UKVI does that. The endorsing body only assesses whether you meet the talent criteria.

## The application process

**Step 1 — Endorsement application**
Apply to the relevant endorsing body with your evidence pack. Fee: GBP 456. Processing: up to 8 weeks (UKRI), varies by body.

**Step 2 — Visa application**
Once endorsed, apply for the Global Talent visa through UKVI. You have 3 months from endorsement to submit the visa application. Fee: GBP 167/year. IHS: GBP 1,035/year.

**Step 3 — Entry and work**
No restrictions on employer, sector or role. You can be employed, self-employed, or run a business. You can switch employers without notifying UKVI.

**From Nigeria:** At NGN 1,650/GBP, a 3-year Global Talent visa costs approximately NGN 6.7M in fees alone, before document preparation, translation, and legal costs.

## Path to settlement

- **Exceptional talent route:** Apply for Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR) after 3 years of continuous residence
- **Exceptional promise route:** Apply for ILR after 5 years of continuous residence
- **British citizenship:** Eligible 12 months after ILR

There is no points test, no salary threshold, and no employer requirement at the settlement stage. You must have been continuously resident and not have been outside the UK for more than 180 days in any 12-month period.

## Who this is realistically for

The Global Talent visa is competitive. The endorsement bar is high — particularly for exceptional talent. For African applicants, the most realistic profiles are:

- **Tech:** Senior engineers or founders with a demonstrable product impact, open source contributions with significant adoption, or recognition at major industry events (not just local conferences)
- **Research:** PhD holders or postdocs with published papers in peer-reviewed journals, particularly in STEM fields
- **Arts/creative:** Professionals with international exhibition records, film credits, or published creative work with documented critical recognition
- **Architecture:** RIBA-recognised practitioners with significant built work

The exceptional promise tier is more accessible for earlier-career professionals, but still requires documented evidence of impact — not just potential stated in a personal statement.

## What to verify before applying

1. Confirm the current endorsing body for your field at gov.uk/global-talent — the tech route changed in 2023 and may have been updated further
2. Read the specific endorsement criteria published by your endorsing body before preparing your evidence pack
3. Confirm current visa fees and IHS rates at gov.uk — both increase annually
4. If using an immigration lawyer, verify they are registered with the Office of the Immigration Services Commissioner (OISC) at gov.uk/find-immigration-adviser

## The honest picture

The Global Talent visa is one of the best UK immigration routes for the right profile — no employer dependency, no salary threshold, fast settlement. But the endorsement bar is genuinely high. It is not a route for most applicants.

For African professionals who do not yet meet the endorsement criteria, the Skilled Worker visa (GBP 38,700 threshold, employer-sponsored) or the UK Innovator Founder visa (for business founders) are more accessible entry points. The Global Talent visa is worth targeting as a medium-term goal for those building toward leadership recognition in their field.
