In a Nigerian travel agent Telegram group with over 1,600 members, agents were publicly calling out other agents. One member wrote: "It's scary these days and I don't even know who to work with anymore." Another: "If travel agents don't trust each other, honestly, it is worrisome."

If agents cannot trust each other, users certainly cannot trust agents blindly. Here is how to verify any agent before paying anything.

## The 10-point checklist

Work through this list before paying any fee or handing over any document.

**1. Ask for the licence number**

Recruitment agencies placing Nigerian workers abroad must be licensed by the Federal Ministry of Labour and Employment. Ask for the licence number before any other conversation. A legitimate agent provides it immediately. An agent who cannot or will not provide a licence number is not licensed.

Verify at: Federal Ministry of Labour and Employment, info@labour.gov.ng

**2. Search for the business registration**

Every legitimate Nigerian business is registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC). Ask for the RC number and search it at search.cac.gov.ng. If the business does not appear, it is not registered.

**3. Get the employer's details before paying**

For work abroad offers: ask for the employer's full legal name, registered address, and business registration number in the destination country. A legitimate job offer comes from a real employer. Verify the employer exists using the destination country's official business registry.

- Qatar: cr.gov.qa
- UAE: ded.ae (Dubai) or added.gov.ae (Abu Dhabi)
- Germany: handelsregister.de
- UK: find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk
- Canada: ised-isde.canada.ca/cc/lgcy/fdrl/srch/hm

**4. Get a signed contract before paying**

A legitimate agent will provide a signed employment contract before asking for any payment. The contract must state: employer name and address, your job title and duties, salary in the local currency of the destination country, working hours, accommodation arrangements, who pays for your flight, and what happens if the employer terminates early.

If an agent asks for payment before providing a contract, do not pay.

**5. Verify the visa route exists**

Every legitimate work destination has a documented visa process. Ask the agent which specific visa category you will be applying for and verify it exists on the official government website. If the agent cannot name the specific visa category, or if the visa they describe does not appear on the official website, the offer is not legitimate.

**6. Check the salary against official data**

Agents sometimes quote inflated salaries to attract applicants. Verify the salary against official sources before believing it. If the salary quoted is significantly above market rates for that country and occupation, verify it in the contract before proceeding.

**7. Confirm the processing time is realistic**

Official visa processing times are published by every government. Any agent claiming processing times significantly faster than official timelines is either lying or describing a tourist visa being misrepresented as a work visa.

- Qatar work visa: 2-4 weeks minimum
- UAE work visa: 2-4 weeks minimum
- Germany Chancenkarte: 8-12 weeks
- Canada Express Entry: 6 months after ITA
- UK Skilled Worker: 3-8 weeks

**8. Check for pressure tactics**

Legitimate agents do not pressure you to decide quickly. Phrases like "limited slots", "offer expires today", "only 3 spots left", and "act now before the price goes up" are sales pressure tactics, not immigration realities. No legitimate government visa process has slots.

**9. Verify the destination country is realistic for your profile**

If an agent is offering you a work visa for a country with no documented labour shortage for your occupation, no bilateral labour agreement with Nigeria, and no established Nigerian worker community — ask why. Mongolia, Belarus, and obscure Caribbean destinations being offered to WAEC-only applicants for large fees are almost always fraudulent.

**10. Search for complaints**

Search the agent's name and company name on Google with the word "scam" or "fraud". Search in Nigerian Facebook groups and Telegram channels. Ask in communities like Nairaland. A pattern of complaints is a strong signal. No complaints does not mean the agent is legitimate, but many complaints is a clear warning.

## Red flags — stop immediately if you see any of these

- Asked to pay the full fee before receiving a signed contract
- Salary quoted only in NGN, not in the destination country's currency
- Job description is vague ("general duties", "housework", "assistant")
- Agent asks you to hand over your passport before travel
- Agent cannot provide their licence number or the employer's registered business name
- The job is in a country you did not apply for
- You are told you will be "placed" with an employer on arrival rather than having a confirmed employer before you travel
- The offer includes "working and studying" on a single visa
- Processing time is quoted in days for a process that takes weeks

## If you suspect fraud

- Report to NAPTIP: naptip.gov.ng | Hotline: 0800-627847-1
- Report to the Federal Ministry of Labour: info@labour.gov.ng
- Report to EFCC: efcc.gov.ng
- If you are already abroad and in danger: contact the Nigerian embassy or consulate in the destination country

## The trust problem in the Nigerian agent market

The agent trust crisis visible in Nigerian Telegram groups is real. Agents are publicly calling out other agents. Users are saying they do not know who to trust. This is the environment in which most Nigerians are trying to navigate work abroad.

The solution is not to trust agents less — it is to verify more. Every legitimate agent can pass this checklist. An agent who cannot pass it is not an agent you should work with, regardless of how professional their marketing looks.
