Germany has approximately 125,000 unfilled care worker positions. Pflegeheime (residential care homes) and Pflegedienste (home care services) across Germany are actively recruiting internationally — including from Nigeria and other African countries.

The Germany care work visa requirements are documented in the [Germany Care Work Visa (Pflegekräfte) 2026](/blog/guides/work/germany-care-work-visa-pflegekrafte-2026/) post. This post covers the step that comes before the visa: finding the employer.

Primary source: [make-it-in-germany.com — Care and nursing](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/jobs/professions/care-nursing)

## What employers are looking for

German care employers hiring internationally are looking for:

- **Language:** B1 German minimum for most care roles. Some accept A2 for auxiliary roles. This is non-negotiable — care work requires communication with residents and colleagues.
- **Qualification:** A nursing or care qualification from your home country. The level of recognition required depends on the role.
- **Willingness to complete recognition:** If your qualification is not yet recognised in Germany, employers want to see that you are in the process or willing to complete it.
- **Commitment to stay:** German employers invest significantly in the recognition and visa process. They want workers who will stay for at least 2-3 years.

## Where to find Pflegeheime

**pflege.de** — Germany's largest care sector directory. Lists thousands of Pflegeheime and Pflegedienste by region. Each listing includes contact details. Use this to build a direct outreach list.

**pflegeheime-finden.de** — Similar directory, searchable by postcode and region.

**arbeitsagentur.de** — Federal Employment Agency job board. Filter by "Pflege" (care) and your target region. Many employers post here directly.

**make-it-in-germany.com/en/jobs** — The official German government job portal for international applicants. Has a care sector filter and is available in English.

**Indeed Germany** (`de.indeed.com`) — Filter by "Pflegekraft" or "Pflegehelfer" and region.

**Caritas, Diakonie, AWO, DRK** — Germany's four major non-profit care providers operate hundreds of facilities across Germany. Each has a central careers portal:
- Caritas: `caritas.de/karriere`
- Diakonie: `diakonie.de/karriere`
- AWO: `awo.org/karriere`
- DRK (German Red Cross): `drk.de/karriere`

These organisations are experienced with international recruitment and often have dedicated international hiring programmes.

## Direct outreach — what to send

A direct email to a Pflegeheim HR department in German is effective. Keep it short:

**Subject:** Bewerbung als Pflegekraft — [Your Name], Nigeria

**Body (in German):**
> Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,
>
> ich bin [Name], [age] Jahre alt, aus Nigeria. Ich habe [X Jahre] Erfahrung in der Pflege und spreche Deutsch auf B1-Niveau. Ich suche eine Stelle als Pflegekraft in Deutschland und bin bereit, den Anerkennungsprozess zu durchlaufen.
>
> Ich freue mich über die Möglichkeit, meine Bewerbungsunterlagen zu senden.
>
> Mit freundlichen Grüßen,
> [Name]

Attach: CV in German (Lebenslauf), copy of your care qualification, German language certificate (Goethe-Institut or telc).

## The recognition process

German care qualifications are regulated at the state (Bundesland) level. The recognition authority depends on which state your employer is in.

**Two types of recognition:**

1. **Full recognition (volle Anerkennung):** Your qualification is assessed as equivalent to the German standard. You can work in the full nursing role immediately.

2. **Partial recognition (teilweise Anerkennung):** Your qualification is assessed as partially equivalent. You complete a compensation measure — either an aptitude test or an adaptation period (typically 6-12 months of supervised work). After this, you receive full recognition.

Most Nigerian nursing qualifications receive partial recognition initially. The adaptation period is completed while working — your employer pays you during this period.

**Where to apply for recognition:** The central contact point is `anerkennung-in-deutschland.de`. Enter your qualification and target state to find the correct authority.

**Employer support:** Many German care employers will support the recognition application and cover the costs. This is a standard part of international recruitment in the care sector. Negotiate this explicitly in your job offer discussion.

## Recruitment agencies — use with caution

Several agencies specialise in placing African care workers in Germany. Some are legitimate; some charge workers upfront fees.

**Red flags:**
- Agency charges you a placement fee before you have a job offer
- Agency cannot name the specific employer or facility
- Agency promises visa approval before the employer has confirmed the job

**Legitimate agencies** work on a commission paid by the employer, not the worker. Ask directly: "Do I pay any fees?" If yes, walk away.

Agencies to research (verify independently before engaging):
- Pflegekräfte aus dem Ausland (government-supported programme)
- Triple Win programme — Federal Employment Agency partnership with Philippines, Bosnia, Tunisia (verify if Nigeria is included at `arbeitsagentur.de`)

## Timeline from job search to arrival

| Stage | Typical duration |
|---|---|
| Job search and employer contact | 1-3 months |
| Job offer and recognition application | 1-2 months |
| Visa application processing | 2-4 months |
| Total | 4-9 months |

Start the German language preparation and recognition application in parallel with the job search. Do not wait for a job offer before starting German — employers will ask your level in the first contact.
