The African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) is creating new business and work corridors across the continent. Rwanda, Mauritius, Ghana and Kenya are positioning themselves as regional hubs. South Africa remains the continent's largest economy and a major destination for skilled African workers.

This post covers the most structured intra-African work routes - what they actually require, not what agents claim.

## South Africa — Critical Skills Visa

South Africa's Critical Skills Visa is the most accessible work route for qualified African professionals. It does not require a prior job offer.

**Requirements:**
- Qualification in a field on the Critical Skills List (published by the Department of Home Affairs)
- The Critical Skills List includes: IT professionals, engineers, doctors, nurses, accountants, architects, and other shortage occupations
- Evaluation of your qualification by the relevant professional body in South Africa
- Valid passport, police clearance, medical certificate

**Validity:** 5 years, renewable.

**Path to permanent residence:** After 5 years on a Critical Skills Visa, you can apply for permanent residence.

**General Work Visa (alternative):** Requires employer sponsorship and proof that no South African was available for the role (labour market test). More restrictive than the Critical Skills Visa.

**Primary source:** `https://www.dha.gov.za/`

## Rwanda — Investor and Skilled Worker Permits

Rwanda has the most streamlined business environment in Africa. Business registration takes 1-2 days online through the Rwanda Development Board (RDB).

**Work permit options:**

**Investor Permit:** For foreign investors who invest at least USD 250,000 in Rwanda. Valid for 10 years, renewable. Provides residence and work rights.

**Skilled Worker Permit:** For professionals employed by a Rwandan company. Requires a job offer from a registered Rwandan employer. Processing time: typically 1-2 weeks.

**Class G Permit (self-employed):** For self-employed professionals and consultants. Requires proof of professional qualifications and a business plan.

**Key advantage:** Rwanda's e-visa and permit system is fully digital. Applications can be submitted and tracked online.

**Primary source:** `https://www.rdb.rw/`

## Kenya — Special Pass and Work Permit

Kenya's work permit system has multiple categories. The most relevant for African professionals are:

**Class G Work Permit:** For specific employment with a named employer. Requires employer sponsorship and proof that no Kenyan was available for the role.

**Special Pass:** A temporary authorisation for up to 3 months while a full work permit is being processed. Useful for professionals who need to start work quickly.

**Class M Permit:** For investors and business owners. Requires a minimum investment threshold (varies by sector).

**Kenya Digital Nomad Visa:** Under development as of 2026 - check current status with the Department of Immigration.

**Primary source:** `https://www.immigration.go.ke/`

## Ghana — Right of Abode and Work Permit

Ghana has a unique provision for the African diaspora: the Right of Abode.

**Right of Abode:** Available to persons of African descent. Provides indefinite residence rights in Ghana without a visa. Does not automatically confer the right to work for a Ghanaian employer - a separate work permit is required for employment.

**Work Permit:** Required for foreign nationals working in Ghana. Employer-sponsored. The Ghana Investment Promotion Centre (GIPC) handles investment-related permits.

**GIPC Investor Permit:** For foreign investors who meet the minimum investment threshold (USD 200,000 for joint ventures, USD 500,000 for wholly foreign-owned enterprises). Provides residence and work rights.

**Primary source:** `https://www.gipc.gov.gh/`

## Mauritius — Occupation Permit

Mauritius has a well-structured Occupation Permit for foreign professionals and investors.

**Professional Permit:** For employed professionals. Requires a job offer from a Mauritian employer and a minimum monthly salary of MUR 60,000 (approximately USD 1,300/month). Valid for 3 years, renewable.

**Investor Permit:** For business owners. Requires a minimum investment of USD 50,000 and a minimum annual turnover of USD 100,000 from year 2. Valid for 3 years, renewable.

**Self-Employed Permit:** For independent professionals. Requires proof of professional qualifications and a business plan.

**Premium Visa (remote workers):** For remote workers earning from outside Mauritius. No minimum income threshold published. Valid for 1 year, renewable. (Covered in detail in the African Digital Nomad Hubs guide.)

**Primary source:** `https://www.edbmauritius.org/`

## The AfCFTA context

The AfCFTA creates the framework for easier movement of people and services across Africa - but it does not override individual country immigration rules. As of 2026, you still need a work permit to work legally in any African country outside your own.

What the AfCFTA does create is a strategic reason for African countries to make their work permit processes more accessible. Rwanda and Mauritius are leading this - both have invested in digital permit systems and competitive permit structures to attract African talent.
