The Gulf states - UAE, Qatar, Oman and Saudi Arabia - are the most active work visa market for African professionals. Hundreds of thousands of Nigerians, Ghanaians, Kenyans and other African nationals work across the region in construction, healthcare, hospitality, engineering and domestic services.

The market is also full of unverified claims. "Visa out in 3-5 days." "Salary 650k, payment 3.2m." "Zero deposit." These claims circulate in Telegram groups and WhatsApp channels with no source, no accountability, and no recourse when they prove false.

This post covers what official sources actually confirm for each country - and what to watch out for.

## The fundamental rule for all Gulf work visas

**You cannot apply for a Gulf work visa independently. Every Gulf work visa requires employer sponsorship.**

Your employer applies for a work permit from the relevant labour authority. Once approved, you apply for the visa. The process is employer-led, not applicant-led. If someone is offering you a Gulf work visa without a confirmed job offer from a named employer, that is a red flag.

## UAE - Employment Visa

**How it works:** Your UAE employer applies for a work permit from MOHRE (Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation). Once approved, you apply for an employment visa at the UAE consulate in your country. After arrival, you complete a medical examination and receive your Emirates ID and residence visa.

**Key facts:**
- No universal minimum salary threshold published by MOHRE - salary requirements vary by occupation, emirate and employer category
- Employment visa is typically valid for 2 years, renewable
- The employer pays the work permit and visa fees - if an agent is asking you to pay large upfront fees, ask for documentation showing these are government fees
- Processing time after employer approval: typically 2-4 weeks

**What agents cannot guarantee:** Processing time, approval outcome, or salary levels. These depend on the employer's application and MOHRE's assessment.

**Primary source:** `https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id`

## Qatar - Work Permit

**How it works:** Your Qatar employer applies for a work permit from the Ministry of Labour. Qatar operates a reformed version of the Kafala (sponsorship) system - since 2020, workers can change jobs without employer permission in some circumstances, but the employer still initiates the work permit.

**Key facts:**
- Minimum wage: QAR 1,000/month (approximately USD 275/month or NGN 450,000/month at current rates) - this is the legal floor, not a typical salary
- Most skilled positions pay significantly above the minimum wage
- Processing time: varies, typically 2-6 weeks after employer application
- Qatar has a non-discriminatory minimum wage that applies to all workers regardless of nationality

**What agents cannot guarantee:** "Visa out in 3-5 days" is not consistent with official processing timelines. Qatar work permit processing is employer-initiated and takes weeks, not days.

**Primary source:** `https://www.moi.gov.qa/`

## Oman - Work Visa and Freelance Permit

**How it works:** Standard work visa requires employer sponsorship. Oman operates a quota system limiting the number of expatriate workers per company. Your employer must have available quota before they can sponsor you.

**The Freelance Permit:** Oman introduced a freelance permit for self-employed workers. Requirements include proof of income, a registered business activity in Oman, and a valid passport. This is a legitimate route for independent professionals - but it requires you to establish a business presence in Oman, not just arrive and work.

**Key facts:**
- Oman's minimum wage applies to Omani nationals, not expatriates - there is no published universal minimum wage for foreign workers
- The "150 rial salary" cited in some agent materials (approximately USD 390/month) is at the lower end of what employers offer - verify any job offer against the actual employment contract
- Employer-provided accommodation and food are common in construction and hospitality sectors but must be specified in the employment contract

**What agents cannot guarantee:** Quota availability, processing time, or that a specific employer will honour verbal commitments. Get everything in writing before paying any fees.

**Primary source:** `https://www.mol.gov.om/`

## Saudi Arabia - Work Visa

**How it works:** Saudi Arabia operates the Kafala system. Your employer applies for a work permit (Iqama) on your behalf. The process involves the employer's registration with the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD).

**Key facts:**
- Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 has created new categories including the Premium Residency permit for high-net-worth individuals and investors
- Standard work visa processing: typically 4-8 weeks after employer application
- Nitaqat system: employers must maintain a minimum ratio of Saudi nationals to expatriates - this affects which sectors and roles are available to foreign workers
- Healthcare, construction, engineering and domestic services are the primary sectors for African workers

**Primary source:** `https://www.mol.gov.sa/`

## What to verify before accepting any Gulf job offer

1. **The employer's name and registration number** - verify they are a registered company in the destination country
2. **The employment contract** - get it in writing before paying any fees. It must specify salary, accommodation, working hours, and what happens if the employer terminates early
3. **Who pays the fees** - government work permit fees are typically paid by the employer. If you are being asked to pay large upfront fees, ask for documentation
4. **The job description** - "general worker" or "domestic worker" contracts have different protections than skilled worker contracts
5. **The agent's credentials** - ask for their registration number with the relevant Nigerian or Ghanaian regulatory body for recruitment agencies

## The agent fee question

When an agent quotes you NGN 2.5M, NGN 3.2M or NGN 3.5M for a Gulf work visa, that is their service fee - not a government fee. Government fees for Gulf work visas are typically paid by the employer.

A legitimate agent should be able to show you:
- The breakdown of what is government fee and what is their service fee
- The employment contract from the employer
- Their registration with the relevant regulatory body
- A written agreement before you pay anything

If they cannot provide these, do not pay.
