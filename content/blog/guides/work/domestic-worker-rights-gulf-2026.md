# Domestic Worker Rights in Qatar, UAE and Saudi Arabia 2026

## Why this matters

Domestic work is one of the most common routes for African workers to the Gulf. It is also one of the highest-risk. Workers in private homes have limited visibility to labour inspectors, limited ability to report problems, and are often in debt to recruitment agents before they arrive.

This article covers what the law says in each country, where the gaps are, and what to check before accepting any domestic work offer.

---

## Qatar

### What the law covers

Qatar's Law No. 15 of 2017 on Domestic Workers gives domestic workers the following rights:

- Written employment contract (mandatory)
- Maximum 10 hours of work per day
- One rest day per week
- 3 weeks paid annual leave per year
- Medical care provided by employer
- End-of-service gratuity (3 weeks per year of service)
- Repatriation at employer's expense at end of contract

### The Kafala reform (2020)

Qatar's 2020 labour reforms extended some protections to domestic workers:
- Exit permit abolished — domestic workers can leave Qatar without employer permission
- Job mobility — workers can change employers after completing their contract or after one year of service in some circumstances
- Minimum wage of QAR 1,000/month applies to all workers including domestic workers

### Where enforcement is weak

- Domestic workers work in private homes, not workplaces. Labour inspectors cannot enter private homes without a complaint.
- Workers who report violations risk retaliation, loss of accommodation and loss of income simultaneously.
- The Labour Dispute Resolution Committees exist but require the worker to initiate a complaint — difficult without legal support or language skills.

Qatar Ministry of Labour: mol.gov.qa

## UAE

### What the law covers

UAE Federal Law No. 10 of 2017 on Domestic Workers covers:

- Written employment contract (mandatory, in Arabic and a language the worker understands)
- Maximum 12 hours of work per day (including 8 hours rest)
- One rest day per week
- 30 days paid annual leave per year (after one year of service)
- Medical insurance provided by employer
- End-of-service gratuity (one month per year of service after one year)
- Repatriation at employer's expense

### No universal minimum wage

The UAE does not publish a universal minimum wage for domestic workers. The employment contract governs the salary. The Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE) provides a standard contract template — insist on using it.

### Passport confiscation

Passport confiscation is illegal under UAE law. If an employer takes your passport, report it to MOHRE via the Tasheel service centres or the MOHRE app.

### Primary source

UAE Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation: mohre.gov.ae

## Saudi Arabia

### What the law covers

Saudi Arabia's Domestic Workers Regulation (2013, amended 2021) covers:

- Written employment contract (mandatory)
- Maximum 10 hours of work per day
- One rest day per week (Friday)
- 30 days paid annual leave per year (after one year of service)
- Medical care provided by employer
- End-of-service gratuity (one month per year of service)
- Repatriation at employer's expense

### Kafala system

Saudi Arabia's Kafala system remains in place. Your visa is tied to your employer. You cannot change employers without your employer's permission during the contract period. The 2021 reforms allowed some categories of workers to change employers, but domestic workers were largely excluded from these reforms.

### No minimum wage for domestic workers

Saudi Arabia does not publish a minimum wage for domestic workers. The employment contract governs the salary. Verify the salary in SAR (Saudi Riyal) in the contract — not in NGN or USD equivalents quoted by agents.

### Primary source

Saudi Ministry of Human Resources: mol.gov.sa

## Red flags — what to watch for before accepting any offer

These are warning signs that a domestic work offer may be fraudulent or exploitative:

- You are asked to pay a recruitment fee before travelling. Legitimate employers pay recruitment costs — workers should not.
- The job description is vague ("housework", "general duties") with no specifics on hours, salary or accommodation.
- You are pressured to decide quickly or told the offer will expire.
- The agent cannot provide the employer's name, address or trade licence number.
- The salary is quoted in NGN or USD only — insist on the local currency figure in the contract.
- You are told you will not need your passport once you arrive.

## Before you travel — checklist

- [ ] You have a signed employment contract in a language you understand
- [ ] The contract specifies salary in local currency (QAR / AED / SAR)
- [ ] The contract specifies working hours, rest days and annual leave
- [ ] You have the employer's full name and address
- [ ] You have the employer's national ID or trade licence number
- [ ] You have not paid any recruitment fee
- [ ] You have a copy of the contract stored separately from your travel documents
- [ ] You have the contact number for the relevant labour ministry in the destination country
- [ ] Someone in Nigeria knows your employer's details and your expected arrival date
