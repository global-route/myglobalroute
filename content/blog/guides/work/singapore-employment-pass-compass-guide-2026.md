Singapore is one of Asia's leading technology hubs. The Employment Pass (EP) is the primary work visa for professionals earning above SGD 5,000 per month. Since September 2023, new EP applications are assessed under the COMPASS framework — a points-based system that evaluates candidates on five criteria.

Here is what COMPASS actually measures and what African tech workers need to know.

## The Employment Pass basics

The Singapore Employment Pass is for foreign professionals, managers and executives. It is employer-sponsored — the employer applies on behalf of the candidate.

**Minimum salary:** SGD 5,000 per month for most sectors. SGD 5,500 for financial services.

**Eligibility:** Acceptable degree from a recognised university, or equivalent professional qualifications. The employer must assess that the candidate meets the requirements for the role.

**Duration:** 2 years for first-time applicants, renewable for up to 3 years.

Primary source: Ministry of Manpower Singapore, mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass

## What COMPASS is

COMPASS (Complementarity Assessment Framework) was introduced in September 2023 for new EP applications. It is a points-based framework with five criteria. Applicants need at least 40 points to pass.

**The five COMPASS criteria:**

| Criterion | Maximum points | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Salary | 20 | How your salary compares to local PMET benchmark for your occupation and age |
| Qualifications | 20 | Whether your degree is from a top-tier institution |
| Diversity | 20 | Whether your nationality is underrepresented in the employer's workforce |
| Local workforce support | 20 | Whether the employer has a strong ratio of local to foreign PMETs |
| Skills bonus | 10 | Whether your occupation is on the shortage skills list |

**Salary criterion in detail:** MOM publishes benchmark salaries by occupation and age group. If your salary is at or above the benchmark for your occupation and age, you score 20 points. If it is below, you score fewer points. This means older candidates need higher salaries to score well on this criterion.

**Diversity criterion:** If your nationality makes up less than 5% of the employer's PMET workforce, you score 20 points. If your nationality makes up 25% or more, you score 0 points. For African applicants, who are typically underrepresented in Singapore tech companies, this criterion is likely to score well.

**Exemption:** Applicants earning SGD 10,000 or more per month are exempt from COMPASS.

## Tech roles in demand

Singapore has documented demand in these technology categories:

- Software engineers and developers
- Data scientists and analysts
- Cybersecurity professionals
- Cloud and DevOps engineers
- AI and machine learning engineers
- Fintech professionals

Singapore's tech sector is concentrated in fintech, logistics technology, and enterprise software. Major employers include DBS Bank, Grab, Sea Group, Shopee, and the Singapore offices of Google, Meta, Amazon, and Microsoft.

## What African applicants need to know

**Degree recognition:** Singapore does not have a formal degree recognition process equivalent to Germany's ANABIN or Canada's WES. The employer assesses whether your degree meets their requirements. Degrees from Nigerian universities are generally accepted by Singapore employers for tech roles, but the employer makes the final determination.

**Salary benchmarks by age:** MOM publishes salary benchmarks by occupation and age. For software engineers, the benchmark salary increases significantly with age. A 35-year-old software engineer needs a higher salary than a 25-year-old to score the same points on the salary criterion. Check the current benchmarks at mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass/compass before applying.

**The COMPASS diversity advantage:** Most Singapore tech companies have workforces dominated by Singaporean, Indian, and Chinese nationals. African applicants from Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya or South Africa are typically underrepresented, which means the diversity criterion is likely to score 20 points. This is a genuine structural advantage for African applicants in the COMPASS framework.

**Financial services premium:** If you are applying for a role in banking, insurance, or fintech, the minimum salary is SGD 5,500 not SGD 5,000. Singapore's financial services sector is one of the largest in Asia and actively hires tech professionals.

## How to find Singapore EP-eligible roles

Singapore employers post jobs on:
- LinkedIn (filter by Singapore and your tech role)
- JobStreet Singapore: jobstreet.com.sg
- MyCareersFuture: mycareersfuture.gov.sg (Singapore government job portal)
- Company career pages for major Singapore tech employers

What to look for: roles with salaries stated in SGD, employer registered in Singapore, job description matching your skills.

## The application process

1. Employer finds your profile or you apply to a job posting
2. Employer assesses your eligibility and makes a job offer
3. Employer applies for your EP through EP Online (mom.gov.sg/eservices/ep-online)
4. MOM processes the application — typically 3-8 weeks
5. EP is approved — you apply for an entry visa if required for your nationality
6. Travel to Singapore and complete formalities on arrival

You do not apply for the EP yourself. The employer does. If an agent is offering to "apply for your Singapore EP" without a specific employer involved, that is not how the process works.

## The honest picture

Singapore is a real and high-value destination for African tech professionals. The COMPASS framework is transparent and the diversity criterion works in favour of African applicants. The salary threshold (SGD 5,000/month) is achievable for mid-level tech professionals.

The route requires a genuine job offer from a Singapore employer. It is not a route you can buy through an agent. The path is: build your skills, apply to Singapore employers directly, get a job offer, let the employer handle the EP application.
