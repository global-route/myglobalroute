The Kafala system has been the subject of international pressure and domestic reform across all six Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) states since 2020. Here is the current status in each country, verified against official sources.

## Qatar

**Exit permit:** Abolished October 2020. Workers can leave Qatar without employer permission.

**Job mobility:** Introduced August 2020, expanded 2021. Workers can change employers without employer permission after completing their contract. Workers can also change employers during the contract in cases of employer violation (non-payment of wages, abuse, unsafe conditions).

**Minimum wage:** QAR 1,000/month for all workers including domestic workers, introduced March 2021. Non-wage benefits (food, accommodation) must be provided or a QAR 300 food allowance and QAR 500 accommodation allowance paid.

**Domestic workers:** Covered by Law No. 15 of 2017. Maximum 10 hours/day, one rest day/week, 3 weeks annual leave, medical care, end-of-service gratuity.

**Enforcement gaps:** Passport confiscation remains documented despite being illegal. Labour Tribunal processes are slow. Domestic workers in private homes have limited access to inspectors.

**Primary source:** Qatar Ministry of Labour, adlsa.gov.qa

---

## UAE

**Exit permit:** Abolished. Workers can leave the UAE without employer permission.

**Job mobility:** Workers can change employers after completing their contract. Workers in certain categories can change employers during the contract with 30 days notice.

**Minimum wage:** No universal minimum wage. A minimum wage for domestic workers was introduced in 2022 but the amount is not publicly fixed — it is set by the employment contract. The UAE has a wage protection system (WPS) that monitors salary payments electronically.

**Domestic workers:** Federal Law No. 10 of 2017. Maximum 12 hours/day including 8 hours rest, one rest day/week, 30 days annual leave after one year, medical insurance, end-of-service gratuity.

**Enforcement gaps:** Passport confiscation is illegal but documented. Domestic workers in private homes have limited inspection access. Wage theft in construction sector remains documented.

**Primary source:** UAE Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation, mohre.gov.ae

---

## Saudi Arabia

**Exit permit:** Partially reformed. Workers in most categories can leave Saudi Arabia without employer permission for visits up to 72 hours. For longer absences, employer permission is still required in some categories.

**Job mobility:** Workers in most categories can change employers after one year of service without employer permission. Domestic workers were largely excluded from the 2021 reforms.

**Minimum wage:** No published minimum wage for domestic workers. The employment contract governs salary.

**Domestic workers:** Domestic Workers Regulation 2013, amended 2021. Maximum 10 hours/day, one rest day/week (Friday), 30 days annual leave after one year, medical care, end-of-service gratuity. Domestic workers were excluded from the 2021 job mobility reforms.

**Enforcement gaps:** The most significant gaps in the GCC. Domestic worker protections are weakest here. Passport confiscation is illegal but widely documented. Saudi Vision 2030 labour reforms are ongoing but domestic worker protections remain limited.

**Primary source:** Saudi Ministry of Human Resources, mol.gov.sa

---

## Oman

**Exit permit:** Abolished. Workers can leave Oman without employer permission.

**Job mobility:** Workers can change employers without sponsor permission after completing their contract, introduced 2021. Workers can also change employers during the contract in cases of employer violation.

**Minimum wage:** OMR 325/month for Omani nationals. No published minimum wage for migrant workers — the employment contract governs.

**Domestic workers:** Covered under the Labour Law with some exclusions. Reforms ongoing.

**Enforcement gaps:** Smaller than Qatar and UAE but present. Passport confiscation documented.

**Primary source:** Oman Ministry of Labour, mol.gov.om

---

## Kuwait

**Exit permit:** Still required in most cases. Kuwait has made the least progress on Kafala reform among the six GCC states.

**Job mobility:** Limited. Workers generally cannot change employers without sponsor permission during the contract period.

**Minimum wage:** No universal minimum wage for migrant workers.

**Enforcement gaps:** Significant. Kuwait has faced repeated international criticism for slow reform progress.

**Primary source:** Kuwait Ministry of Social Affairs and Labour, msal.gov.kw

---

## Bahrain

**Exit permit:** Abolished 2009 — Bahrain was the first GCC state to abolish the exit permit requirement.

**Job mobility:** Workers can change employers without sponsor permission after one year of service. Bahrain's Migrant Worker Protection System (LMRA) provides a complaints mechanism.

**Minimum wage:** No universal minimum wage for migrant workers.

**Enforcement gaps:** Domestic workers have weaker protections than other categories. Passport confiscation documented.

**Primary source:** Bahrain Labour Market Regulatory Authority, lmra.gov.bh

## What this means for African workers

The reforms are real but uneven. Qatar and Oman have made the most substantive changes. Saudi Arabia and Kuwait have made the least.

For any Gulf work offer, the practical questions remain:

1. Is the employer registered and verifiable?
2. Is there a signed employment contract before travel?
3. Does the contract state salary in local currency, working hours, accommodation, and return ticket?
4. Is the employer paying for the visa — not the worker?
5. Is the role in a category covered by the reforms (most employed workers) or excluded (domestic workers in Saudi Arabia)?

The reforms reduce but do not eliminate the risks of Gulf employment. The safety callout on the Work Abroad Agent tool exists for this reason.
