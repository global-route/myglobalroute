The Netherlands Highly Skilled Migrant (kennismigrant) permit is one of the most accessible skilled work routes in Europe for African tech professionals. No points system, no language requirement, fast processing, and a clear path to permanent residence.

## What the permit is

The Highly Skilled Migrant permit is a residence and work permit for highly skilled workers earning above a salary threshold. It is employer-sponsored — the employer applies on behalf of the worker through the IND (Immigration and Naturalisation Service).

The employer must be a recognised sponsor (erkend referent). Most large companies, multinationals, and tech firms in the Netherlands are recognised sponsors. Startups and smaller companies can apply for recognised sponsor status.

Primary source: IND Netherlands, ind.nl/en/residence-permits/work/highly-skilled-migrant

## Salary thresholds (2026)

| Age | Annual salary threshold |
|---|---|
| Under 30 | EUR 46,107 |
| 30 and over | EUR 63,503 |
| Graduates (within 3 years of graduation) | EUR 36,889 |

These thresholds are updated annually on 1 January. Verify the current threshold at ind.nl before applying.

The salary must be paid in cash — benefits, bonuses, and allowances do not count toward the threshold.

## No points system, no language requirement

Unlike Germany's Chancenkarte (points-based) or Canada's Express Entry (CRS score), the Netherlands Highly Skilled Migrant permit has no points system. If your employer is a recognised sponsor and your salary meets the threshold, the permit is issued.

There is no Dutch language requirement. The Netherlands has one of the highest English proficiency rates in the world and most tech companies operate entirely in English.

## Processing time

For recognised sponsors, the IND processes applications within 2 weeks. This is significantly faster than most European work permits (Germany: 8-12 weeks, UK: 3-8 weeks).

## Tech roles in demand

Amsterdam and Eindhoven are the primary tech hubs. Amsterdam has a large fintech, e-commerce, and media tech sector. Eindhoven is home to ASML (semiconductor equipment), Philips, and a growing deep-tech ecosystem.

Roles in demand:
- Software engineers (all stacks)
- Data engineers and scientists
- Cybersecurity professionals
- Cloud and DevOps engineers
- Product managers
- UX/UI designers

Major employers: ASML, Booking.com, Adyen, TomTom, Philips, ING, ABN AMRO, and the Netherlands offices of Google, Microsoft, Amazon, and Netflix.

## Path to permanent residence

After 5 years of legal residence in the Netherlands, you can apply for permanent residence (permanent verblijfsvergunning). After 5 years of legal residence, you can also apply for Dutch citizenship (naturalisation), which requires passing a civic integration exam and a Dutch language test (B1 level).

The Highly Skilled Migrant permit counts toward the 5-year residence requirement.

## How to find Netherlands employers

- LinkedIn (filter by Netherlands and your tech role)
- Glassdoor Netherlands
- Indeed Netherlands: indeed.nl
- Dutch tech job boards: techleap.nl, startup.amsterdam
- Company career pages for major Netherlands employers

What to look for: employer listed as a recognised IND sponsor (most large companies are), salary stated in EUR meeting the threshold for your age.

## The honest picture

The Netherlands Highly Skilled Migrant permit is one of the most straightforward skilled work routes in Europe. The salary threshold is achievable for mid-to-senior tech professionals. The processing is fast. There is no language barrier.

The route requires a genuine job offer from a Netherlands employer. It is not a route you can buy through an agent. The path is: apply to Netherlands employers directly, get a job offer above the salary threshold, let the employer handle the permit application.
