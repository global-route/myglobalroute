"Job assistance available when you get there."

This phrase appears in visa offers for Antigua and Barbuda, some Caribbean destinations, and occasionally Gulf routes. It sounds helpful. It is one of the most dangerous phrases in Nigerian migration.

## What it actually means

Job assistance when you get there means:

- No employer has confirmed a job for you
- You have no employment contract
- You have no confirmed salary
- You have no legal right to work until an employer is found
- The agent's obligation ends when you land

You are travelling to a foreign country with money spent on a visa and a flight, no income, and no guarantee that work will materialise.

## How the pattern works

1. Agent advertises a destination with "job assistance on arrival" and "zero deposit"
2. Applicant pays for visa processing and flight
3. Applicant arrives in the destination country
4. Agent's local contact attempts to find work — or does not
5. Applicant has no income, no return ticket money, and no legal recourse

The agent has been paid. The applicant is stranded.

## Why it is different from legitimate job search visas

Germany's Chancenkarte, Portugal's D3, and similar job search visas are legitimate routes to travel and look for work. The difference is structural:

| | Job assistance on arrival | Legitimate job search visa |
|---|---|---|
| Issued by | Agent | Government |
| Legal right to job search | No | Yes |
| Minimum funds required | Not verified | Verified by embassy |
| Duration | Undefined | Fixed (1-2 years) |
| Recourse if no job found | None | Return home legally |
| Cost | Agent fee + flight | Official visa fee + funds |

A government-issued job search visa gives you a legal status, a defined period, and a minimum funds requirement that means you can survive while searching. Job assistance on arrival gives you none of these.

## The Antigua and Barbuda pattern

Offers for Antigua and Barbuda with "job assistance when you get there" and "10-21 days approval" are a documented pattern in Nigerian agent channels. Antigua and Barbuda is not a documented labour shortage destination for Nigerian workers. There is no bilateral labour agreement between Nigeria and Antigua. The "job assistance" is not backed by any employer relationship.

## What NAPTIP documents

The National Agency for the Prohibition of Trafficking in Persons (NAPTIP) regularly documents cases where Nigerians travelled abroad on the promise of work that did not materialise. The pattern — travel first, find work later — is a common trafficking vector. Workers who arrive with no income and no return ticket are vulnerable to accepting exploitative conditions to survive.

## The rule

A job offer is confirmed when you have a signed employment contract from a named, registered employer, stating your salary in the local currency of the destination country, before you travel.

"Job assistance when you get there" is not a job offer. It is a promise with no legal weight and no enforcement mechanism.

Do not travel for work without a confirmed, signed employment contract.
