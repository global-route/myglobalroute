In Nigerian agent channels, Oman work offers are frequently described as "Oman Freelance Visa." The actual route being sold is almost always an employment visa. They are different categories.

## The Oman Freelance Visa (Visa No. 2)

The Oman Freelance Visa is a self-employment permit introduced as part of Oman Vision 2040 reforms. It is for professionals who want to work independently — consultants, freelancers, independent contractors.

**Requirements:**
- Proof of professional qualification in your field
- Bank statement showing minimum OMR 3,000 (approximately USD 7,800)
- Health insurance
- Valid passport
- Application submitted to the Royal Oman Police

**Who it is for:** IT consultants, engineers, accountants, and other professionals who have their own clients or contracts and want to operate independently in Oman.

**Who it is not for:** Mechanics, electricians, domestic workers, hospitality workers, or anyone taking up an employed position with a specific employer.

Primary source: Oman Ministry of Labour, mol.gov.om

## The Oman Employment Visa

The Oman Employment Visa is an employer-sponsored work visa. The employer applies for the visa through the Ministry of Manpower portal. The worker does not apply independently.

**Process:**
1. Employer applies for work visa approval
2. Employer issues offer letter and employment contract
3. Worker applies for Oman entry visa at the Oman embassy
4. Worker travels, completes medical and biometrics on arrival

**Who pays:** The employer pays for the employment visa. Workers do not pay for Oman employment visas. Any agent charging you for an employment visa is charging for something the employer should provide.

## Why agents conflate them

The freelance visa framing serves two purposes for agents:

**It sounds better.** "Freelance visa" sounds modern and independent. "Employment visa" sounds like you are being placed in a job, which raises the question of why you are paying an agent rather than the employer paying for the visa.

**It obscures the fee structure.** If you are taking up an employed position, the employer should pay for your visa. By calling it a freelance visa, the agent can charge you for a visa the employer should provide for free.

## How to tell which one you actually need

If you are going to Oman to work for a specific employer — a mechanic workshop, a hotel, a construction company, a hospital — you need an employment visa. The employer applies for it. You do not pay for it.

If you are going to Oman to work independently with your own clients — as a consultant, freelancer, or independent contractor — you may need the freelance visa. You apply for it yourself. You need OMR 3,000 in your bank account.

The offers seen in Nigerian agent channels for mechanics, electricians, and hospitality workers are employment visa routes. They are being called freelance visas. The distinction matters because it determines who pays for the visa and what your rights are once you arrive.

## The practical test

Ask the agent: "Who is my employer in Oman?"

If they can name a specific employer with a registered business, you are looking at an employment visa. The employer should pay for it.

If they cannot name a specific employer, you are being asked to pay for a visa to travel to Oman without a confirmed job. That is a different risk entirely.
