# How to Verify a Recruitment Agent is Legitimate 2026

## The problem

Recruitment fraud targeting Nigerians seeking work abroad is widespread. Common patterns include:

- Agents charging large upfront fees for jobs that do not exist
- Agents providing genuine-looking contracts for jobs that turn out to be different on arrival
- Agents facilitating trafficking under the guise of legitimate employment
- Agents impersonating licensed agencies or using similar names to legitimate firms

This guide gives you concrete steps to verify an agent before paying anything or handing over documents.

---

## Step 1 — Ask for the licence number

Recruitment agencies placing Nigerian workers abroad must be licensed by the Federal Ministry of Labour and Employment. Ask the agent for their licence number before any other conversation.

A legitimate agent will provide this immediately. An agent who cannot or will not provide a licence number is not licensed.

**Verify the licence:**
- Contact the Federal Ministry of Labour and Employment: info@labour.gov.ng
- Visit the Ministry office: 1 Hill Station Hotel Road, Abuja
- Ask specifically for the Private Employment Agency (PEA) licence register

---

## Step 2 — Verify the employer independently

A legitimate job offer comes from a real employer. Before paying any fees, verify the employer exists and the job is real.

**For Gulf employers (Qatar, UAE, Saudi Arabia):**
- Qatar: Search the employer's Commercial Registration number at cr.gov.qa
- UAE: Search the employer's trade licence on the Dubai DED portal (ded.ae) or Abu Dhabi DED portal
- Saudi Arabia: Search the employer's Commercial Registration at mc.gov.sa

**For European employers:**
- Germany: Search the company on the Handelsregister at handelsregister.de
- UK: Search Companies House at find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk
- Ireland: Search the Companies Registration Office at cro.ie

If the employer does not appear in the official register, the job offer is not from a registered business.

---

## Step 3 — Get the employment contract before paying

A legitimate agent will provide a signed employment contract before asking for any payment. The contract must specify:

- Employer's full legal name and registered address
- Your job title and duties
- Your salary in the local currency of the destination country
- Working hours and rest days
- Accommodation arrangements
- Who pays for your flight and return
- What happens if the employer terminates early

If an agent asks for payment before providing a contract, do not pay.

---

## Step 4 — Check the salary is realistic

Agents sometimes quote inflated salaries to attract applicants. Verify the salary against official sources:

- Qatar: Minimum wage is QAR 1,000/month. Skilled roles typically QAR 2,000-8,000/month.
- UAE: No universal minimum, but AED 3,000-5,000/month is typical for semi-skilled roles.
- Saudi Arabia: No published minimum for domestic workers. SAR 1,500-3,000/month is typical for domestic roles.
- Germany: Minimum wage is EUR 12.41/hour (2026). Skilled roles EUR 2,500-4,500/month.
- UK: National Living Wage is GBP 11.44/hour (2026). Skilled roles GBP 2,500-4,500/month.

If the salary quoted is significantly above these ranges, verify it in the contract before believing it.

---

## Step 5 — Verify the visa route is real

Every legitimate work destination has a documented visa process. Ask the agent which specific visa category you will be applying for and verify it exists on the official government website.

- Qatar: mol.gov.qa
- UAE: mohre.gov.ae
- Saudi Arabia: mol.gov.sa
- Germany: make-it-in-germany.com
- UK: gov.uk/browse/visas-immigration

If the agent cannot name the specific visa category, or if the visa they describe does not appear on the official website, the offer is not legitimate.

---

## Red flags — stop and verify before proceeding

Any of these should cause you to pause and verify independently:

- Asked to pay before receiving a signed contract
- Salary quoted only in NGN or USD, not in the destination country's currency
- Job description is vague ("general duties", "housework", "assistant")
- Agent pressures you to decide quickly
- Agent asks you to hand over your passport before travel
- Agent cannot provide their licence number
- Agent cannot provide the employer's registered business name and address
- The job is in a country you did not apply for
- You are told you will be "placed" with an employer on arrival rather than having a confirmed employer before you travel

---

## If you suspect fraud

- Report to NAPTIP: naptip.gov.ng | Hotline: 0800-627847-1
- Report to the Federal Ministry of Labour: info@labour.gov.ng
- Report to the Nigeria Police Force Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC): efcc.gov.ng
- If you are already abroad and in danger: contact the Nigerian embassy or consulate in the destination country
