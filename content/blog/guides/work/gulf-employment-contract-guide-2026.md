Most problems that African workers face in the Gulf — unpaid wages, changed job descriptions, unexpected deductions, passport confiscation — could have been identified or prevented by reading the employment contract carefully before signing.

Here is what to check.

## The 10 things every Gulf employment contract must contain

**1. Employer's full legal name and registered address**

The contract must name the employer as a registered legal entity, not just a person's name. Verify the employer's commercial registration number against the official business registry of the destination country.

**2. Your job title and duties**

The job title and duties must match what you were told you would be doing. Vague descriptions like "general duties" or "assistant" are red flags. If the contract says "domestic worker" but you were told you would be working in a hotel, the contract is wrong.

**3. Salary in local currency**

The salary must be stated in the local currency of the destination country — QAR for Qatar, AED for UAE, SAR for Saudi Arabia, OMR for Oman. A salary stated only in NGN or USD is not enforceable in the destination country's courts.

**4. Working hours and rest days**

The contract must state your daily working hours and your weekly rest day. Gulf labour laws set maximums: Qatar 10 hours/day, UAE 12 hours/day including 8 hours rest, Saudi Arabia 10 hours/day. If the contract states hours above these limits, it is not compliant with local law.

**5. Accommodation arrangements**

The contract must state whether accommodation is provided by the employer, and if so, what type. "Accommodation provided" is not sufficient — it should specify whether it is shared or private, and whether utilities are included.

**6. Who pays for the flight**

The contract must state who pays for your initial flight to the destination country and your return flight at the end of the contract. Legitimate Gulf employers pay for both. If the contract is silent on this, ask before signing.

**7. Annual leave entitlement**

Qatar: 3 weeks per year. UAE: 30 days per year after one year of service. Saudi Arabia: 30 days per year after one year of service. Oman: 30 days per year. If the contract states less than the legal minimum, it is not compliant.

**8. End-of-service gratuity**

Gulf countries require employers to pay an end-of-service gratuity when the employment ends. The amount varies by country and length of service. The contract should reference this entitlement.

**9. Notice period and termination conditions**

The contract must state the notice period required from both sides and the conditions under which the contract can be terminated. If the employer can terminate immediately without notice for any reason, that is a red flag.

**10. Dispute resolution**

The contract should state which country's law governs the contract and how disputes are resolved. For Gulf employment, this should be the labour law of the destination country, not Nigerian law.

## Contract substitution — the most common problem

Contract substitution is when the contract you signed before travelling is replaced with a different contract on arrival. The new contract typically has lower pay, different duties, or worse conditions.

This is illegal in all Gulf countries. If it happens:

1. Do not sign the new contract
2. Contact the relevant labour ministry immediately
3. Request repatriation at the employer's expense — this is your right under Gulf labour law
4. Contact the Nigerian embassy or consulate

**Qatar:** Ministry of Labour hotline: 16008
**UAE:** MOHRE hotline: 800 60
**Saudi Arabia:** Ministry of Human Resources: 19911
**Oman:** Ministry of Labour: 1500

## What to do if you receive a contract only in Arabic

Ask for an English version. All major Gulf employers dealing with international workers have English contracts. If the employer refuses to provide an English version, that is a red flag.

Do not sign a contract in a language you do not understand. You are signing a legal document that will govern your rights for the duration of your stay.

## The pre-travel checklist

Before signing any Gulf employment contract:

- [ ] Employer's full legal name and commercial registration number are stated
- [ ] Job title and duties match what you were told
- [ ] Salary is stated in local currency (QAR/AED/SAR/OMR)
- [ ] Working hours are within legal limits
- [ ] Accommodation arrangements are specified
- [ ] Employer pays for initial and return flights
- [ ] Annual leave entitlement meets legal minimum
- [ ] End-of-service gratuity is referenced
- [ ] Contract is in a language you understand
- [ ] You have a signed copy before travelling
