In Nigerian Telegram groups and WhatsApp channels, Qatar work visa offers appear regularly. A typical offer looks like this:

"Qatar Employment Visa (Freelance Visa). Salary: NGN 650,000/month. Payment: NGN 3,200,000. Medical, ID and ticket provided. Visa out in 3-5 days."

Three things in that offer are red flags. Here is what Qatar work visas actually look like.

## Red flag 1 — "Visa out in 3-5 days"

No legitimate Qatar work visa processes in 3-5 days.

Qatar employment visa processing timeline, verified from Qatar Ministry of Labour (adlsa.gov.qa):

- Standard employment visa: employer applies through the Ministry of Labour portal, worker receives visa approval — minimum 2-4 weeks
- Qatar Freelance/Self-Employment Permit: applicant submits documents to Ministry of Interior — minimum 2-4 weeks
- Medical examination and biometrics: additional 3-7 days after visa approval

Tourist visas process in 24-72 hours. Work authorisations do not. Any agent claiming a Qatar work visa in 3-5 days is either describing a tourist visa (which does not give you the right to work) or fabricating the timeline.

## Red flag 2 — NGN 3.2 million fee

Qatar employment visas are paid for by the employer, not the worker.

Under Qatar Labour Law (Law No. 14 of 2004 and subsequent amendments), employers are responsible for visa fees, medical examination costs, return air ticket at end of contract, and accommodation for most employment categories.

Official Qatar visa fees from the Ministry of Interior fee schedule: QAR 1,000-2,000 for most work permit categories. At current exchange rates that is approximately NGN 150,000-300,000 in official government fees.

NGN 3.2 million is approximately USD 2,000. This is not a visa fee — it is an agent fee for a service the employer should provide for free.

If an employer is asking you to pay for your own Qatar work visa, that is a violation of Qatar labour law and a documented red flag for exploitative employment.

## Red flag 3 — "Freelance visa" framing for employment work

The Qatar Freelance Visa (Self-Employment Permit) is a real visa category introduced as part of post-2022 World Cup labour reforms. But it is not what most of these offers are selling.

The Qatar Freelance Visa requires:
- Proof of professional qualification in your field
- Bank statement showing minimum QAR 10,000 (approximately NGN 1.5 million)
- Health insurance
- Valid passport
- Application submitted to Ministry of Interior

This is a visa for self-employed professionals — consultants, freelancers, independent contractors. It is not a route for domestic workers, construction workers, or general labourers. Agents calling a standard employment visa a "freelance visa" are using the term to make the offer sound more legitimate.

## What legitimate Qatar work looks like

Qatar does have genuine demand for workers across multiple categories. The legitimate process:

For skilled workers (engineers, IT, healthcare): employer posts job, interviews candidate, applies for work visa through Ministry of Labour portal, worker receives visa approval letter, worker applies for Qatar entry visa at embassy, worker travels and completes medical and biometrics on arrival. Total cost to worker: zero for visa. Worker pays own travel costs only if not covered by employer.

For semi-skilled and unskilled workers (hospitality, construction, domestic): employer or licensed recruitment agency recruits worker, employer applies for work visa, worker signs employment contract before travelling in a language they understand, worker does not pay recruitment fees — Qatar law prohibits charging workers recruitment fees.

Salary reality verified from Qatar Ministry of Labour wage data:
- Minimum wage: QAR 1,000/month (non-domestic workers)
- Construction general labour: QAR 1,200-1,800/month
- Hospitality (hotel): QAR 1,500-2,500/month
- Domestic worker: QAR 1,500-2,000/month (separate minimum wage category)

## Kafala reform — what changed and what has not

Qatar reformed its Kafala system following international pressure ahead of the 2022 World Cup.

What changed: exit permit abolished (2020) — workers can leave Qatar without employer permission. Job mobility (2021) — workers can change employers without employer permission after completing their contract. Minimum wage introduced (2021) — QAR 1,000/month non-domestic, QAR 1,100/month domestic.

What has not changed: workers are still tied to a specific employer on their visa. Enforcement of the new rules is inconsistent — documented cases of employers still confiscating passports despite it being illegal. Domestic workers have weaker protections than other categories. Dispute resolution through the Labour Tribunal is slow.

The reforms are real but incomplete. Qatar is a higher-risk destination for unskilled and domestic workers than the agent offers suggest.

## How to verify a Qatar job offer is legitimate

Before accepting any Qatar work offer:

1. Ask for the employer's commercial registration number (CR number) — all Qatar businesses must be registered
2. Verify the employer exists on the Qatar Ministry of Commerce portal
3. Get a signed employment contract before travelling — in English or your language
4. Confirm the contract states: salary, working hours, accommodation, annual leave, return ticket
5. Do not pay any recruitment fee — legitimate Qatar employers do not charge workers
6. Check the Qatar Ministry of Labour's list of blacklisted employers at adlsa.gov.qa — employers with labour violations are listed

If an agent cannot provide the employer's CR number, the offer is not legitimate.

## The honest picture

Qatar does have genuine labour demand. The country is building infrastructure, running hotels, and employing domestic workers at scale. The work is real.

The problem is the agent layer between Nigerian workers and Qatar employers. Agents charging NGN 3 million for a visa the employer should provide for free, promising 3-5 day processing that takes 3-4 weeks, and calling employment visas "freelance visas" to sound more credible — this is the pattern that leads to workers arriving in Qatar with debt, no contract, and no recourse.

The rule is simple: if you are paying for a Qatar work visa, something is wrong.
