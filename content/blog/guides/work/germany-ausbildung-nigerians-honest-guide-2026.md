Germany's Ausbildung system is one of the most respected vocational training frameworks in the world. A completed Ausbildung in nursing, electrical installation, IT, or mechatronics is a recognised German qualification that opens doors across Europe.

It is also genuinely difficult for Nigerian applicants to access. This post explains why — and who it actually works for.

Primary source: [make-it-in-germany.com — Ausbildung](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/jobs/training-apprenticeship/ausbildung)

## What Ausbildung is

Ausbildung (dual vocational training) is a 2-3 year programme combining workplace training with vocational school (Berufsschule). You are employed by a company, attend school 1-2 days per week, and work in the company the rest of the time.

At the end, you take a final exam set by the relevant chamber (Handwerkskammer for trades, Industrie- und Handelskammer for commercial/industrial roles). Pass the exam and you have a recognised German vocational qualification.

Over 300 recognised Ausbildung occupations exist. Popular ones for international applicants: nursing assistant (Pflegefachhelfer), IT specialist (Fachinformatiker), mechatronics technician (Mechatroniker), electrician (Elektroniker), cook (Koch).

## Why most Nigerian applicants fail to get in

**Barrier 1 — German language at B2**

The training is conducted in German. The Berufsschule is in German. Your colleagues and supervisors speak German. Without B2, you cannot follow the training, pass the exams, or function in the workplace.

Most Nigerian applicants who apply for Ausbildung have A2 or B1 German. Employers know this and reject applications accordingly. B2 takes 18-24 months to reach from zero. Most applicants are not willing to invest this time before applying.

**Barrier 2 — Finding an employer willing to sponsor an international applicant**

German employers who take on Ausbildung trainees are making a 2-3 year commitment. They invest time in training, pay the stipend, and deal with the administrative burden of sponsoring a non-EU worker's visa.

Many small and medium-sized German employers (Mittelstand) are not set up to handle international recruitment. They prefer local applicants or EU citizens who do not require visa sponsorship.

Finding an employer willing to sponsor a Nigerian applicant requires targeted outreach, strong German language skills, and often a personal connection or referral.

**Barrier 3 — The stipend is not a living wage**

EUR 600-800/month in year 1 is below the German minimum wage. It is designed for young Germans living with their parents or in subsidised student accommodation. For a Nigerian adult who has relocated to Germany, EUR 700/month does not cover rent (EUR 500-800 for a room in a shared flat), food, transport, and other costs.

Most international Ausbildung trainees need savings of EUR 5,000-10,000 to bridge the gap in year 1 before the stipend increases.

## Who Ausbildung actually works for

**Profile 1 — Already in Germany on another visa**

If you are already in Germany — on a student visa, a family reunification visa, or a Chancenkarte — and you want to pivot to a vocational qualification, Ausbildung is accessible. You are already in the country, you have been building German language skills, and you can approach employers directly.

**Profile 2 — Strong B2 German, specific trade target**

If you have invested 18-24 months in German language preparation and have a specific trade in mind (nursing, IT, electrical), Ausbildung is a viable route. The key is targeting employers in sectors with documented shortages — healthcare and IT have the most active international recruitment.

**Profile 3 — Younger applicants (under 25) without a degree**

Ausbildung is designed for young people entering the workforce. German employers are more receptive to younger applicants. If you are under 25, do not have a university degree, and are willing to invest in German language preparation, Ausbildung is a legitimate path to a German qualification and long-term residence.

## What works better for most Nigerian applicants

| Profile | Better route |
|---|---|
| University degree + work experience | Chancenkarte or direct job search |
| Healthcare professional (nurse, physio) | Recognition + Critical Skills permit (Ireland) or direct employment (Germany) |
| IT professional | Chancenkarte or direct job search |
| No degree, willing to train | Ausbildung — but only with B2 German first |
| Trades (electrician, plumber) | Direct employment via employer sponsorship |

## The honest timeline for Ausbildung from Nigeria

| Stage | Duration |
|---|---|
| German language preparation (A1 to B2) | 18-24 months |
| Job search for Ausbildung placement | 3-6 months |
| Visa application processing | 2-4 months |
| Ausbildung training | 2-3 years |
| **Total to qualified status** | **5-7 years** |

This is a long commitment. It is not wrong — a German vocational qualification is genuinely valuable. But applicants should enter with clear eyes about the timeline and the financial requirements.
