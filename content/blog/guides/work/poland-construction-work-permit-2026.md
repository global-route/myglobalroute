Poland is an EU member state with a documented shortage of construction and manufacturing workers. The country has historically relied on Ukrainian labour, and the ongoing conflict has created gaps that Polish employers are increasingly filling from other sources, including Africa.

This is a real corridor. It is not widely discussed in Nigerian migration communities, but it is growing.

## The labour shortage

Poland's construction sector has been one of the fastest-growing in Europe, driven by EU infrastructure funding and domestic housing demand. The shortage of skilled and semi-skilled construction workers is documented by the Polish Central Statistical Office (GUS) and employer associations.

Manufacturing — particularly automotive components, electronics assembly, and food processing — also has documented shortages.

Primary source: Office for Foreigners Poland, gov.pl/web/udsc-en

## The work permit process

Poland uses an employer-sponsored work permit system. The employer applies for the permit on behalf of the worker.

**Two main permit types:**

**Temporary residence and work permit (zezwolenie na pobyt czasowy i pracę):** Combined residence and work permit. Employer applies to the regional Voivode (provincial governor's office). Processing time: 1-3 months.

**Work permit type A (zezwolenie na pracę typ A):** Work permit only, for workers who will apply for a visa separately. Employer applies to the Voivode. Processing time: 2-4 weeks.

For most African workers, the temporary residence and work permit is the more practical route as it combines both documents.

## Salary and conditions

**Minimum wage:** PLN 4,300 per month gross (approximately EUR 1,000) as of January 2026.

**Typical wages for construction and manufacturing:**

| Role | Typical wage (PLN/month gross) |
|---|---|
| General construction worker | PLN 4,500-6,000 |
| Electrician | PLN 5,500-8,000 |
| Welder | PLN 5,000-7,500 |
| Carpenter/joiner | PLN 5,000-7,000 |
| Manufacturing assembly worker | PLN 4,300-5,500 |
| Forklift operator | PLN 4,500-6,000 |

PLN 5,000/month is approximately EUR 1,160 or USD 1,220. Living costs in Polish cities outside Warsaw are significantly lower than Western Europe — a worker earning PLN 5,000 can live comfortably and save.

## Living costs

Poland is one of the most affordable EU countries:

- Accommodation (shared flat, outside Warsaw): PLN 800-1,200/month
- Food: PLN 600-800/month
- Transport: PLN 100-150/month (monthly pass)
- Total monthly living cost: PLN 1,500-2,150

A worker earning PLN 5,000 gross (approximately PLN 3,800 net after tax) can save PLN 1,500-2,300 per month.

## Schengen travel

Poland is a Schengen member. A Polish residence permit allows visa-free travel across all 29 Schengen countries. This is a genuine benefit — you can visit Germany, France, Netherlands and other EU countries during your time in Poland.

A Polish residence permit does not give you the right to work in other EU countries. To work in Germany, you still need a German work permit.

## How to find Polish employers

Polish employers recruiting foreign workers advertise on:
- Pracuj.pl — Poland's largest job board
- OLX.pl — includes construction and manufacturing roles
- Indeed Poland: pl.indeed.com
- LinkedIn (filter by Poland)

Recruitment agencies specialising in foreign worker placement in Poland are also active. Verify any agency's registration with the Polish National Labour Inspectorate (PIP) before paying fees.

## The honest picture

Poland is a real and growing work corridor for African workers in construction and manufacturing. The wages are modest by Western European standards but the living costs are low, the Schengen travel benefit is real, and the path to longer-term residence in the EU exists.

The route requires a genuine employer. It is not a tourist visa route. The employer applies for the permit, and the worker applies for a Polish visa at the Polish embassy or consulate serving Nigeria.

This corridor is underserved by Nigerian migration content. It is worth researching seriously.
