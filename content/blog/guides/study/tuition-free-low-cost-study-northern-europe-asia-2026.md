# Tuition-Free and Low-Cost Study in Northern Europe and Asia 2026

## The Honest Overview

"Free university" claims require careful reading. Here is what each country actually offers non-EU African students:

| Country | Tuition for non-EU students | Living funds required | Key scholarship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Norway | €0 (public universities) | NOK 170,368/year (~€15,000) | Government Quota Scheme |
| Sweden | SEK 80,000-140,000/year (~€7,000-€12,000) | SEK 10,656/month (~€950) | SISGP (covers fees + stipend) |
| Finland | €6,000-€18,000/year | €800/month | Finnish Government Scholarship Pool |
| Japan | ¥535,800/year (~€3,200) standard; €0 with MEXT | ¥117,000/month stipend (MEXT) | MEXT (full coverage) |

---

## Norway: Genuinely Tuition-Free

Norway is the only country in this guide where tuition-free applies universally to non-EU students at public universities. This is not a scholarship - it is the standard fee structure.

### What you need

- **Admission:** Full-time admission to an accredited Norwegian university or university college
- **Living funds:** NOK 170,368 per academic year (or NOK 15,488/month) - [source: Norwegian Directorate of Immigration](https://www.udi.no/en/want-to-apply/studies/studietillatelse/)
- **Language:** Most programmes are in Norwegian. English-taught programmes exist at Master's level - check the institution directly
- **Health insurance:** Covered by the Norwegian National Insurance Scheme after 12 months of residence; private insurance required initially

### The real cost

Tuition is free. Living costs are not. Oslo is one of the most expensive cities in Europe. Budget NOK 15,000-18,000/month (€1,350-€1,620) for accommodation, food and transport. The NOK 170,368 living funds requirement reflects this.

### Government Quota Scheme

Norway's Quota Scheme (Kvoteordningen) provides scholarships for students from developing countries, including Nigeria. It covers living costs and sometimes travel. Applications are made through Norwegian universities - check each institution's international office for availability.

**Official source:** [Norwegian Directorate of Immigration - Study](https://www.udi.no/en/want-to-apply/studies/studietillatelse/)

## Sweden: Fees Apply, Scholarships Available

Sweden introduced tuition fees for non-EU/EEA students in 2011. Fees vary by programme and institution.

### Tuition fees (non-EU students)

- Undergraduate: SEK 80,000-120,000/year (€7,000-€10,500)
- Master's: SEK 80,000-140,000/year (€7,000-€12,000)
- Fees are set by each university - check the specific programme

### Living funds required

SEK 10,656/month (2026 rate) - [source: Swedish Migration Agency](https://www.migrationsverket.se/en/you-want-to-apply/study/higher-education.html). This is approximately €950/month.

### Swedish Institute Study Scholarships (SISGP)

The Swedish Institute offers full scholarships for Master's students from eligible countries including Nigeria. The scholarship covers:
- Full tuition fees
- Monthly living allowance of SEK 11,000 (~€980)
- Travel grant
- Insurance

Applications open in February each year for programmes starting the following autumn. Competition is high - the Swedish Institute receives thousands of applications.

**Official source:** [Swedish Migration Agency - Higher Education](https://www.migrationsverket.se/en/you-want-to-apply/study/higher-education.html)

## Finland: Fees Apply, Scholarships Available

Finland introduced tuition fees for non-EU/EEA students in 2017. Fees are lower than Sweden on average.

### Tuition fees (non-EU students)

- Bachelor's: €6,000-€12,000/year
- Master's: €8,000-€18,000/year
- Fees vary significantly by institution and programme

### Living funds required

€800/month - [source: Finnish Immigration Service](https://migri.fi/en/income-requirement-for-students). For a full academic year, this means €9,600 must be available when applying.

### Finnish Government Scholarship Pool

Finland offers scholarships through individual universities and the Finnish Government Scholarship Pool for doctoral-level students. For Master's level, most scholarships are institution-specific - check the university's international office directly.

Many Finnish universities offer tuition fee waivers for high-achieving international students. These are competitive and institution-specific.

**Official source:** [Finnish Immigration Service - Studies](https://migri.fi/residence-permit-application-for-studies)

## Japan: Low Fees, MEXT Covers Everything

Japan's standard university tuition for international students is approximately ¥535,800/year (approximately €3,200) - low by European standards. With the MEXT scholarship, all costs are covered.

### MEXT Scholarship

Japan's Ministry of Education scholarship is one of the most comprehensive available to African students:

- **Tuition:** Fully covered (entrance fee + tuition)
- **Monthly stipend:** ¥117,000 (approximately €700/month) for undergraduate; ¥144,000-¥145,000 for postgraduate
- **Travel:** Return airfare covered
- **Duration:** Full programme length (typically 4 years undergraduate, 2 years Master's, 3 years PhD)

### How to apply for MEXT

Applications are made through the Japanese embassy in Nigeria, typically in **April-May** each year for programmes starting the following April. The process involves:

1. Apply at the Japanese Embassy in Abuja
2. Written examination (Japanese language and academic subjects)
3. Interview at the embassy
4. Recommendation to Japanese universities
5. University acceptance

The embassy-recommended route is competitive. Some universities also accept direct MEXT applications - check the JASSO (Japan Student Services Organization) website for details.

### Self-funded study in Japan

Without MEXT, you need:
- Admission to a Japanese university
- Proof of funds: approximately ¥2,000,000 (€12,000) for the first year
- A Certificate of Eligibility (CoE) issued by the university

**Official source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/status/student.html)

## Practical Comparison for Nigerian Applicants

### If your priority is lowest upfront cost

**Norway** - zero tuition, but you need NOK 170,368 (~€15,000) in living funds. No scholarship competition required.

### If your priority is maximum financial support

**MEXT Japan** - covers everything including travel. But competition is high and the process takes 12-18 months from application to arrival.

### If your priority is English-language programmes

**Norway and Sweden** both have English-taught Master's programmes. Finland has fewer. Japan has some English-taught programmes but most are in Japanese.

### If your priority is post-study work rights

- **Norway:** 1-year job search permit after graduation
- **Sweden:** 1-year job search permit after graduation
- **Finland:** 1-year job search permit after graduation
- **Japan:** Can apply for work visa after graduation; strong demand for engineers and IT professionals
