Four European countries stand out for Nigerian students targeting September 2027 intake: Lithuania, Slovakia, Latvia, and Norway. Each has lower barriers than the UK, Germany, or the Netherlands — lower tuition, more flexible entry requirements, or no IELTS requirement.

This post covers what each country actually requires. It does not cite approval rate statistics — no immigration authority publishes these figures by nationality, and any source claiming specific percentages is not citing an official source.

Verify all figures at the official university and immigration authority before applying. September 2027 admissions are open now at most institutions.

## Lithuania

**Official source:** `migracija.lt` (migration), `studyinlithuania.lt` (study portal)

Lithuania joined the Schengen Area in 2007 and has been building its international student intake steadily. It is a full EU member state — a Lithuanian degree is an EU-recognised qualification.

**Tuition:** EUR 3,000-9,000 per year depending on programme and university. Private universities tend to be at the higher end. Public universities such as Vilnius University and Kaunas University of Technology are at the lower end for most programmes.

**Entry qualifications:**
- BSc applications: OND, NCE, or HND accepted at many universities as equivalent to secondary school completion for foundation or direct entry. Verify with the specific university.
- MSc applications: 60% CGPA required at most universities. A small number accept third class or 2:2 — verify per institution.

**Language:** IELTS not compulsory. Most universities accept a letter of English proficiency, a previous degree taught in English, or conduct their own English assessment. Confirm with the admissions office.

**Dependants:** Allowed for MSc applications. Not standard for BSc. Verify with the Lithuanian Migration Department.

**Application fees:** Most universities charge application fees. Verify per institution.

**Path after study:** Lithuania offers a job search permit after graduation. EU Blue Card is available for graduates who secure qualifying employment.

**Verify at:** `studyinlithuania.lt` for university listings, `migracija.lt` for visa and residence permit requirements.

## Slovakia

**Official source:** `mzv.sk` (Ministry of Foreign Affairs), `studyinslovakia.sk`

Slovakia is a Schengen and EU member state. Tuition is among the lowest in Europe for international students, and the country has a growing English-taught programme offering.

**Tuition:** EUR 1,000-4,000 per year. Some programmes at public universities are taught in Slovak at no cost — English-taught programmes carry fees. Comenius University in Bratislava and Slovak University of Technology are the main public institutions.

**Entry qualifications:**
- BSc applications: Secondary school certificate required. Nigerian WAEC/NECO accepted at most institutions.
- MSc applications: Third class and 2:2 accepted at most universities — lower bar than many Western European countries.

**Language:** IELTS not compulsory for English-taught programmes. Universities typically require proof of English proficiency through previous education in English or an internal assessment.

**Dependants:** Allowed for MSc applications. Verify with the Slovak Foreign Police.

**Application fees:** Most universities charge application fees. Verify per institution.

**Path after study:** Slovakia offers a temporary residence permit for job search after graduation. EU Blue Card available for qualifying employment.

**Verify at:** `studyinslovakia.sk`, `mzv.sk/web/en`

## Latvia

**Official source:** `pmlp.gov.lv` (Office of Citizenship and Migration Affairs), `studyinlatvia.lv`

Latvia is a Schengen and EU member state. It has one of the lowest tuition ranges in the EU for international students. Note: Latvia no longer allows dependants on student visas — this changed in recent years.

**Tuition:** EUR 1,000-5,000 per year. Riga Technical University and the University of Latvia are the main public institutions with English-taught programmes.

**Entry qualifications:**
- BSc applications: Secondary school certificate. Nigerian WAEC/NECO accepted.
- MSc applications: Third class and 2:2 accepted at most institutions.

**Language:** IELTS not compulsory. English proficiency demonstrated through previous education or university assessment.

**Dependants:** No longer allowed on student residence permits. Verify current policy at `pmlp.gov.lv` before applying.

**Application fees:** Most universities charge application fees.

**Path after study:** Latvia offers a job search permit after graduation. EU Blue Card available.

**Verify at:** `studyinlatvia.lv`, `pmlp.gov.lv`

## Norway

**Official source:** `udi.no` (Norwegian Directorate of Immigration), `studyinnorway.no`

Norway is not an EU member but is part of the Schengen Area and the European Economic Area. Public universities charge no tuition fees for any student — including non-EU Africans. This is the most significant financial advantage Norway offers.

**Tuition:** EUR 0 at public universities. Private universities charge fees — verify per institution. The University of Oslo, NTNU (Trondheim), and the University of Bergen are the main public institutions.

**The catch:** Norway requires proof of NOK 170,368 in living funds per academic year (approximately EUR 15,000 at current rates). Tuition-free does not mean cost-free. Living costs in Norway are among the highest in Europe.

**Entry qualifications:**
- BSc applications: OND, NCE, or HND accepted at some institutions for foundation entry. Verify per university — Norwegian universities have specific equivalency assessments.
- MSc applications: 2:1 or above required at most Norwegian universities. Third class and 2:2 are generally not accepted for MSc — this is a harder requirement than Lithuania or Slovakia.

**Language:** IELTS compulsory for most English-taught programmes. Minimum scores vary by programme — typically IELTS 6.0-6.5. Some programmes require Norwegian language proficiency.

**Dependants:** Allowed for MSc applications. Verify at `udi.no`.

**Proof of funds:** Must be demonstrated at the time of application submission — not just at visa interview. NOK 170,368 must be in a bank account you control.

**Application fees:** No application fees at most public universities.

**Path after study:** Norway offers a job seeker permit after graduation. Non-EU graduates can apply for a skilled worker permit if they secure qualifying employment.

**Verify at:** `studyinnorway.no`, `udi.no/en/want-to-apply/studies/`

## What to do now for September 2027

September 2027 intake applications are open at most European universities now. Deadlines vary — many close between January and April 2027.

**Step 1 — Choose your country and programme.** Use the tuition ranges and entry requirements above to shortlist. If you have a 2:2 or third class, Lithuania and Slovakia are more accessible than Norway for MSc.

**Step 2 — Verify requirements at the specific university.** The figures in this post are ranges — individual universities set their own thresholds. Contact the admissions office directly.

**Step 3 — Prepare your documents.** Educational credential evaluation (some universities require this), English proficiency evidence, bank statements for proof of funds, academic transcripts.

**Step 4 — Apply early.** Popular English-taught programmes fill quickly. Do not wait until the deadline.

**Step 5 — Verify visa requirements at the official immigration authority.** Each country's immigration authority is listed above. Requirements change — always check the primary source before submitting.
