# Qatar Kafala Reform 2024: What Actually Changed for Workers

## Background: What Is Kafala

The Kafala (sponsorship) system ties a migrant worker's legal status in Qatar to their employer. Under the traditional system, workers could not change jobs, leave the country, or access legal protections without employer permission. Employers held significant power over workers' lives.

Qatar came under intense international scrutiny following the 2022 FIFA World Cup, which accelerated reform announcements.

---

## What Has Changed (On Paper)

### Exit Permit Abolished (2020)

Before 2020, most workers needed employer permission to leave Qatar. This exit permit requirement was abolished in September 2020 for most workers.

**Current position:** Workers can leave Qatar without employer permission. Domestic workers and some other categories were initially excluded but the reform has been extended.

**In practice:** Most workers can now leave. However, workers in debt to recruitment agents or with unresolved contract disputes may face practical barriers even without a formal exit permit requirement.

---

### Job Mobility Without Permission (2021)

From September 2021, workers can change jobs without employer permission in certain circumstances:
- After completing their contract period
- After one year of service (in some cases)
- If the employer has violated the contract

**In practice:** Employers can and do contest job changes. Workers who attempt to change jobs without employer agreement often face contract disputes, withheld end-of-service benefits, or threats of deportation. The legal right exists but enforcement is inconsistent.

---

### Non-Discriminatory Minimum Wage (2021)

Qatar introduced a minimum wage in March 2021 that applies to all workers regardless of nationality:
- QAR 1,000 per month basic wage
- QAR 500 food allowance (if employer does not provide food)
- QAR 500 accommodation allowance (if employer does not provide accommodation)
- **Total: QAR 2,000 per month minimum**

This was significant because previous minimum wages in Qatar applied only to certain nationalities. The non-discriminatory minimum wage covers all workers including domestic workers.

**In practice:** Enforcement is uneven. Workers in isolated domestic settings or on remote construction sites have limited ability to report violations.

---

### Domestic Worker Protections (2020)

A law passed in 2020 extended labour protections to domestic workers for the first time. Domestic workers gained:
- Right to a written contract
- Maximum 10-hour working day
- One rest day per week
- 3 weeks annual leave
- End-of-service gratuity

**In practice:** Domestic workers remain among the most vulnerable. They work in private homes, away from labour inspectors, and often have limited access to complaint mechanisms.

---

## What Has Not Changed

**The sponsorship relationship remains.** Workers are still tied to a specific employer through the Kafala framework. The employer still controls the worker's residency permit. If the employment relationship ends, the worker's legal status in Qatar is at risk.

**Recruitment debt remains a major problem.** Many African workers pay recruitment agents in their home countries to secure Qatar jobs. These fees can be USD 2,000-5,000. Workers arrive in debt and are vulnerable to exploitation because they cannot afford to leave.

**Complaint mechanisms are limited.** Workers who report violations risk retaliation, deportation or loss of income. The Labour Dispute Resolution Committees exist but are not easily accessible to workers without legal support.

**Domestic workers remain in a separate, weaker legal category** despite the 2020 law. Enforcement in private homes is structurally difficult.

---

## What This Means for African Workers Considering Qatar

**The reforms are real but partial.** Qatar has made genuine legal changes. The exit permit abolition and minimum wage are meaningful improvements. But the structural power imbalance between employer and worker remains.

**Recruitment agent fees are the biggest risk.** If you pay a large fee to a recruitment agent to get a Qatar job, you are starting in debt and in a vulnerable position. Legitimate employers do not charge workers recruitment fees. If you are asked to pay, that is a red flag.

**Domestic work carries the highest risk.** If you are considering domestic work in Qatar, understand that you will be working in a private home with limited oversight and limited ability to report problems. The legal protections exist but enforcement is weak.

**Verify your employer before you go.** Check that your employer is registered with the Qatar Ministry of Labour. Verify your contract terms before you travel. Do not travel without a signed contract in a language you understand.

---

## Minimum Wage in Context

QAR 2,000 per month (approximately USD 550 or GBP 430 at 2026 rates) is the legal minimum. Professional and skilled workers earn significantly more. The minimum wage is relevant primarily for domestic workers, construction workers and hospitality staff.
