# Australia TSMIT Increase 2024: New Minimum Salary for Sponsored Workers

## What Is the TSMIT

The Temporary Skilled Migration Income Threshold (TSMIT) is the minimum annual earnings an employer must pay a sponsored overseas worker in Australia. It applies to the Temporary Skill Shortage (TSS) visa (subclass 482) and related employer-sponsored streams.

The TSMIT exists to prevent employers from using overseas workers to undercut Australian wages.

---

## The Increases

| Date | TSMIT |
|---|---|
| Before July 1, 2023 | AUD 53,900 |
| July 1, 2023 | AUD 70,000 |
| July 1, 2024 | AUD 73,150 |

The July 2023 increase was the largest in the TSMIT's history - a 30% jump in one step. The July 2024 increase brought it to AUD 73,150.

The TSMIT is reviewed annually. Expect further increases in July 2025 and beyond.

---

## What It Means in Practice

Your employer must pay you at least AUD 73,150 per year. But that is only the floor. They must also pay you the Annual Market Salary Rate (AMSR) - what Australian workers doing the same job in the same location earn.

If the AMSR for your occupation in your city is AUD 85,000, your employer must pay AUD 85,000, not AUD 73,150.

The TSMIT is the minimum. The AMSR is the actual requirement for most professional roles.

---

## Which Visas Are Affected

**Affected:**
- TSS visa (subclass 482) - Short-term stream and Medium-term stream
- Employer Nomination Scheme (subclass 186) - Temporary Residence Transition stream
- Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional (subclass 494)

**Not affected:**
- Skilled Independent visa (subclass 189)
- Skilled Nominated visa (subclass 190)
- Skilled Work Regional visa (subclass 491)
- DAMA regional stream (separate concession arrangements apply)
- Student visa (subclass 500)

---

## Impact on African Applicants

AUD 73,150 is approximately USD 47,000 or GBP 37,000 at 2026 exchange rates. For professional roles in tech, engineering, healthcare and finance in Australian cities, this is achievable. For trades and technical roles in regional areas, DAMA concessions may allow lower salary thresholds.

If you are applying through the DAMA regional stream, the TSMIT may not apply - DAMA agreements can include salary concessions for specific occupations in designated regional areas. Check the specific DAMA agreement for your region and occupation.

---

## Checking the AMSR for Your Occupation

The Department of Home Affairs does not publish a fixed AMSR table. Your employer determines the AMSR by checking:
- What they pay Australian workers in the same role
- Industry salary surveys
- Award rates under the Fair Work Act

If you are negotiating a job offer, ask your employer to confirm the AMSR for your role and location before you apply.
