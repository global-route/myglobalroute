Australia's standard skilled migration pathways - the 189, 190 and 491 points-tested visas - are competitive, quota-driven and increasingly difficult for African applicants to access without high CRS scores or state nomination. The Designated Area Migration Agreement (DAMA) system is different. It is employer-driven, regionally focused, and carries concessions that do not exist anywhere else in the Australian migration system.

This playbook covers what DAMAs are, what the concessions actually mean, how to find a DAMA employer, and what the path to permanent residence looks like.

## What is a DAMA

A Designated Area Migration Agreement is a formal agreement between the Australian Government and a regional or territory authority - a state government, a local council, or a regional development body. The agreement allows employers in that designated area to sponsor overseas workers under terms that go beyond what standard skilled migration permits.

As of 2026, active DAMAs cover regions including the Northern Territory, South Australia, Western Australia's Pilbara and Kimberley regions, the Great South Coast in Victoria, and several other regional areas. Each DAMA has its own occupation list, concession levels and employer eligibility criteria.

The Department of Home Affairs maintains the authoritative list of active DAMAs and their designated areas at `immi.homeaffairs.gov.au`.

## The concessions - what they actually mean

This is the core reason DAMAs matter for African applicants.

**Age cap extended to 55.** Standard skilled visas cap applicants at 45 years old. Under a DAMA, the age cap can be extended to 55 for specific occupations. If you are 46-55 and have relevant skills, a DAMA employer sponsorship may be the only points-tested pathway available to you.

**English score floor reduction.** Standard skilled visas require a minimum English score - typically IELTS 6.0 overall with no band below 5.0 for most occupations. Under a DAMA, the English floor can be reduced for specific occupations. The exact reduction depends on the individual DAMA agreement and occupation.

**Expanded occupation lists.** DAMAs can include occupations that do not appear on the standard Medium and Long-term Strategic Skills List (MLTSSL) or Short-term Skilled Occupation List (STSOL). If your occupation is not on the standard lists, a DAMA may still provide a pathway.

**Salary concessions up to 10%.** The standard Temporary Skilled Migration Income Threshold (TSMIT) applies to most sponsored workers. Under a DAMA, employers can pay up to 10% below the TSMIT for specific occupations in specific regions. This makes it easier for regional employers with lower wage structures to sponsor overseas workers.

## Who DAMA employers are

DAMA employers are businesses operating in designated regional areas that have entered into a Labour Agreement with the Department of Home Affairs under the relevant DAMA. They are typically in sectors with documented labour shortages - hospitality, agriculture, construction, healthcare, and trades.

To find DAMA employers:
- Contact the relevant regional authority directly - they maintain lists of approved DAMA sponsors
- Check regional job boards and employer websites in designated areas
- The Northern Territory Government, South Australian Government and other DAMA authorities publish employer directories

You cannot apply for a DAMA visa without a sponsoring employer. The employer initiates the Labour Agreement and nominates you.

## The application process

**Step 1 - Employer Labour Agreement.** The employer must have an active Labour Agreement with the Department of Home Affairs under the relevant DAMA. If they do not have one, they must apply for it first - this takes time.

**Step 2 - Nomination.** Once the Labour Agreement is in place, the employer nominates you for a specific occupation under the agreement. The nomination must be approved by the Department of Home Affairs.

**Step 3 - Visa application.** After nomination approval, you apply for the Temporary Skill Shortage (TSS) visa subclass 482 under the Labour Agreement stream. You must meet the health and character requirements and provide evidence of your qualifications and work experience.

**Step 4 - Regional work.** You must live and work in the designated regional area for the duration of your TSS visa. You cannot transfer to a non-regional employer without losing your DAMA concessions.

## The PR vector - subclass 191

After 3 years of living and working in a designated regional area on a TSS visa under a DAMA Labour Agreement, you become eligible to apply for the Permanent Residence (Skilled Regional) visa subclass 191.

The subclass 191 is a direct permanent residence pathway. It does not require a new points test or state nomination. The primary requirement is 3 years of regional work and meeting the income threshold for those 3 years.

This is the DAMA's most significant advantage over the standard 491 pathway - the 491 also requires 3 years regional work before the 191, but the 491 requires a points test score and state nomination to enter. The DAMA bypasses both.

## Proof of funds

The Department of Home Affairs does not publish a fixed personal funds threshold for DAMA-sponsored workers. Your employer's salary covers living costs. The indicative setup cost for initial relocation and settlement is approximately AUD 5,300 (approximately USD 3,500) covering visa application fees, travel, and initial accommodation deposit.

Visa application fees for the TSS subclass 482 are AUD 3,115 for the primary applicant as of 2026. Confirm current fees at `immi.homeaffairs.gov.au` before applying.
