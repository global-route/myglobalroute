## What is Express Entry?

Express Entry is **Canada's invitation-based immigration system** for skilled workers. It's the fastest pathway to Permanent Residency (PR).

### How It Works (30-Second Overview)

1. You create an online profile
2. You enter the "Express Entry pool"
3. Canada conducts monthly draws
4. If your CRS score qualifies, you get an Invitation to Apply (ITA)
5. You submit full application within 60 days
6. Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) processes (6 months average)
7. You become a permanent resident

**That's it.** No employer sponsorship needed. No job offer required (though it helps).

### Who It's For

Express Entry is for **skilled workers with:**
- Post-secondary education (college diploma, bachelor's, master's, or PhD)
- Work experience (preferably 2+ years)
- English or French language proficiency (CLB 7+)

### Why It's Fast

- **6-month processing:** Unlike family sponsorship (18-24 months) or employer sponsorship (12-18 months)
- **Transparent system:** You know your exact CRS score before applying
- **No interviews needed:** Most applications approved without video call
- **Predictable:** Draw criteria published monthly

## Express Entry Programs Explained

There are **three pathways** under Express Entry. Which one suits you?

### Program 1: Federal Skilled Worker (FSW)

**Who it's for:** Skilled professionals in NOC 0, A, or B occupations

**Requirements:**
- Bachelor's degree or higher
- 2+ years **paid work experience** (full-time, NOC 0/A/B)
- English CLB 7+ OR French CLB 7+
- No job offer required

**NOC Occupations Included:**
- Software developers (2173)
- Accountants (1111)
- Engineering managers (2112)
- Nurses (3012)
- Architects (2161)
- Financial analysts (1112)

**Typical CRS Score Range:** 400-520

**Best for:** Professionals with degrees and 2+ years work experience

---

### Program 2: Federal Skilled Trades (FST)

**Who it's for:** Skilled tradespersons

**Requirements:**
- 2+ years work experience in designated trades
- High school diploma or equivalent
- English CLB 5+ (lower requirement)
- Valid job offer OR Provincial Nominee Program support

**Designated Trades:**
- Electricians
- Plumbers
- Welders
- Heavy equipment operators
- Construction managers

**Typical CRS Score Range:** 250-400 (lowest of all programs)

**Best for:** Tradespersons with in-demand skills and work experience

---

### Program 3: Canadian Experience Class (CEC)

**Who it's for:** Skilled workers with Canadian work experience

**Requirements:**
- 3+ years **Canadian work experience** (NOC 0/A/B)
- English CLB 7+ OR French CLB 7+
- Work experience must be in Canada (not your home country)
- No specific education requirement (though helps)

**Typical CRS Score Range:** 320-450

**Best for:** Nigerians already working in Canada on a work permit who want PR

---

### Comparison: Which Program Should You Choose?

| Factor | FSW | FST | CEC |
|--------|-----|-----|-----|
| Education Required | Bachelor's+ | High school | None |
| Work Experience | 2+ years anywhere | 2+ years anywhere | 3+ years IN Canada |
| Language Requirement | CLB 7+ | CLB 5+ | CLB 7+ |
| Without Job Offer | ✅ Yes | ❌ Usually needs offer | ✅ Yes |
| Typical CRS Score | 450-520 | 280-400 | 350-450 |
| Best for Nigerians | ✅ MOST | CMS/trades only | ✅ If already in Canada |

**Recommendation for Nigerians:** **Federal Skilled Worker (FSW)** is the most common path.

## CRS Score: The Complete Breakdown

Your **Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score** determines if you get an ITA. It's out of **1,200 points** total.

### CRS Score Components

#### Core Components (460 points maximum)

**Age:** 0-132 points
- Maximum points: Age 20-29
- Decreases after age 29
- Minimum consideration: Typically age 35-45 still competitive

**Typical scores:**
- Age 20-29: 110-132 points
- Age 30-39: 100-121 points
- Age 40-49: 70-91 points
- Age 50+: 0-37 points

---

**Education:** 0-150 points
- PhD: 150 points ✅ (highest)
- Master's degree: 126 points
- 2+ bachelor's degrees: 120 points
- Bachelor's degree: 120 points ✅ (most common)
- 2-year college diploma: 91 points
- 1-year certificate: 46 points
- High school only: 0 points

---

**Language Ability (English or French):** 0-136 points

**English CLB Scale:**
- CLB 9: 136 points (native-like fluency)
- CLB 8: 130 points ✅ (very common target)
- CLB 7: 119 points ✅ (minimum for FSW)
- CLB 6: 98 points
- CLB 5: 65 points
- Below CLB 5: 0 points (not eligible)

**How to get CLB scores:**
- **IELTS:** 6.5+ = CLB 7 (most Nigerians take this)
- **TOEFL:** 88+ = CLB 7
- **Duolingo:** 110+ = CLB 7

**Cost:** IELTS ₦60,000-80,000, TOEFL ₦50,000-70,000

---

**Work Experience:** 0-80 points

Awarded based on **1+ years of experience in primary occupation:**

- 6+ years: 80 points
- 5-5.99 years: 70 points
- 4-4.99 years: 60 points
- 3-3.99 years: 50 points ✅ (common target)
- 2-2.99 years: 40 points
- 1-1.99 years: 30 points
- Less than 1 year: 0 points

---

#### Spouse/Common Law Partner Factors (Up to 215 points)

If you have a spouse applying **together:**

- **Education:** 0-58 points
- **Language:** 0-72 points
- **Work experience:** 0-70 points
- **Other factors:** 0-15 points

**Important:** Single applicants skip this section (0 points).

---

#### Transferable Skills (Up to 100 points)

Bonus points if your education or experience transfers to Canada:

- **Education + work in same field:** 0-100 points
- **Relevant education:** 0-50 points
- **Relevant work experience:** 0-50 points

**Example:** A software engineer from Nigeria with a tech degree + 5 years coding experience = full 100 points.

---

#### Additional Factors (Up to 600 points!)

This is where **Provincial Nominee Program (PNP)** scores come in:

- **PNP Nomination:** 600 points 🔥 (automatic ITA)
- **Valid job offer:** 200 points
- **Provincial residence:** 30 points
- **Language: French CLB 5+:** 30 points
- **Sibling in Canada:** 20 points

## Step-by-Step Application Guide

### Phase 1: Preparation (Months 1-2)

#### Step 1: Assess Your Eligibility

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Do you have a diploma/degree? (required)
- [ ] Do you have 1+ year work experience? (required for most)
- [ ] Can you get CLB 7+ English? (required)
- [ ] Are you under 45 years old? (optional but helps)

If YES to all → proceed.

#### Step 2: Gather Your Documents

**You'll need:**
- Valid passport
- Educational credentials (degree/diploma)
- Work experience letters (dates, duties, salary)
- Language test results (IELTS, TOEFL, or CLB)
- Police certificate (criminal background check)
- Medical exam

**Where to get each:**

| Document | How to Obtain | Cost | Time |
|----------|---------------|------|------|
| Degree/Transcript | University registrar | ₦10,000-30,000 | 1-4 weeks |
| Work Letter | Your employer | Free | 1 week |
| IELTS Test | British Council Nigeria | ₦60,000 | 2-3 weeks |
| Police Certificate | Nigeria Police | ₦5,000-15,000 | 2 weeks |
| Medical Exam | IRCC-approved clinic | ₦50,000-100,000 | 1 week |

**Total time:** 4-8 weeks to gather all documents

---

#### Step 3: Get a Language Test

**Which test to take?**

**IELTS (Most popular for Nigerians)**
- Cost: ₦60,000-70,000
- Score validity: 2 years
- Required score: Band 7 = CLB 7 minimum
- Test format: 3 hours
- Available in: Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, etc.

**TOEFL iBT**
- Cost: ₦50,000-60,000
- Score validity: 2 years
- Required score: 88 = CLB 7 minimum
- Test format: 3 hours
- More widely accepted internationally

**Duolingo English Test (Budget Option)**
- Cost: ₦8,000-10,000 (cheapest!)
- Score validity: 2 years
- Required score: 110 = CLB 7 minimum
- Test format: 1 hour (online)
- Growing acceptance

**Which should you take?**
- **Nigeria only:** Duolingo (cheapest, fastest)
- **International:** IELTS (most recognized)
- **Academic background:** TOEFL (traditional)

**Pro tip:** Take the test at least **1 month before you submit your Express Entry profile** to ensure scores arrive in time.

---

#### Step 4: Calculate Your Estimated CRS Score

Use **Canada's official CRS calculator**: https://www.cic.gc.ca/english/immigrate/skilled/crs-tool.asp

**Fill in:**
- Age
- Education level
- Work experience (years)
- Language test scores

**Your estimated score appears** → determines if you need PNP help or if you can apply solo.

---

### Phase 2: Create Your Express Entry Profile (Month 3)

#### Step 5: Create IRCC Account

1. Visit https://www.canada.ca/express-entry
2. Click "Sign in" or "Create account"
3. Use **two-factor authentication** (SMS code to phone)
4. Verify your email

#### Step 6: Enter Your Information

Fill in the following sections:

**Section A: Personal Information**
- Full name (as per passport)
- Date of birth
- Nationality
- Marital status
- Email

**Section B: Contact Information**
- Mailing address (Nigerian address is fine)
- Phone number
- Preferred language of correspondence

**Section C: Language Abilities**
- English test scores (IELTS/TOEFL/Duolingo)
- French test scores (optional but adds points)

**Section D: Education**
- Highest level of education
- Field of study
- Name of institution
- Country of education
- Year obtained

**Section E: Work Experience**
- Job title
- Employer name
- NOC code (look this up!)
- Years employed
- Full-time or part-time
- Duties performed

**Section F: Intended Destination**
- Which province you want to settle in
- Canadian work experience? (if any)

#### Step 7: Verify CRS Score

After entering all information, IRCC shows your **calculated CRS score**.

**If score is 500+:** Submit and wait for draws
**If score is 400-499:** You're in the pool but may need PNP
**If score is below 400:** Get more work experience or PNP nomination

#### Step 8: Submit Your Profile

Click "Submit" and your profile enters the **Express Entry Pool**.

**Important:** Your profile is **active for 12 months**. After 12 months, it expires and you must resubmit.

---

### Phase 3: Wait for Invitation to Apply (Weeks 1-24)

Once your profile is in the pool, you wait for monthly draws.

**How often does IRCC draw from the pool?**

Typically **weekly or bi-weekly** depending on immigration targets.

**What CRS score gets invited?**

Usually between **490-520** in 2026.

**If you get invited:**
- You receive an **Invitation to Apply (ITA)**
- You have **60 days** to submit your full application
- Proceeding to Phase 4 (Full Application)

**If you don't get invited after 3-6 months:**
- **Option A:** Improve your CRS (more work experience, language improvement, or PNP)
- **Option B:** Apply for PNP (Provincial Nominee Program) to get 600-point boost
- **Option C:** Consider alternative pathways (employer sponsorship, study permit → work permit)

---

### Phase 4: Submit Full Application (Days 1-60 After ITA)

When you receive an ITA, you have **60 days** to submit your full application.

#### Step 9: Prepare Your Full Application Package

You'll need to upload:
- Scanned passport (all pages)
- Educational credentials (degree/transcripts)
- Work experience letters (from all employers listed)
- Police certificate (from Nigeria)
- Medical examination results (from IRCC-approved doctor)
- Proof of funds (bank statements, sponsorship letters if applicable)
- Language test results (official results, not self-reported)
- Spouse/partner documents (if applicable)

**Important:** All documents must be:
- In English (translate to English if in another language)
- Recent (within last 6 months for most documents)
- Certified/notarized where required

---

#### Step 10: Pay Application Fee

**Fee structure (2026):**

| Item | Cost CAD | Cost NGN |
|------|----------|----------|
| Principal applicant | $715 | ₦400,000 |
| Spouse/partner | $215 | ₦120,000 |
| Each dependent child | $215 | ₦120,000 |
| Right of Permanent Residency fee | $500 | ₦280,000 |
| **Total (single applicant)** | **$1,215** | **₦680,000** |

**Payment methods:**
- Credit/debit card (online)
- Wire transfer

---

#### Step 11: Submit Your Application

1. Log into IRCC portal
2. Upload all documents
3. Review for errors
4. Pay fees
5. Submit before 60-day deadline

**Track your application status:** Login to portal anytime to see progress

---

### Phase 5: IRCC Processing (Months 1-6)

Once you submit, IRCC reviews your application.

**Processing time:** Average **6 months** (can be 4-8 months)

**What IRCC checks:**
- Document authenticity (especially police cert and education)
- Work experience verification (may contact your employer)
- Medical exam results
- Background security check

**What happens if there are issues:**
- IRCC sends a **request for additional information (RFI)**
- You have **30 days** to respond
- Common RFI reasons: Missing documents, unclear work history, credential verification needed

**Important:** Respond to **any RFI immediately**. Delays can slow processing.

---

### Phase 6: Receive Permanent Residency (Month 6+)

**When IRCC approves:**

1. You receive an **approval email**
2. Your **Confirmation of Permanent Residency (CoPR)** is sent
3. You receive an **expiry date** for your CoPR (usually 12 months)
4. You need to **travel to Canada before the expiry date**

**Final step: Landing in Canada**
- Bring your passport and CoPR letter
- Present to border agent upon arrival
- Get your **Permanent Resident card** mailed within 6 weeks
- You're now a permanent resident! 🎉

## Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) Strategy

If your CRS score is below 490-500, **PNP is your secret weapon**.

### What is PNP?

Each Canadian province nominates candidates for permanent residency based on **their local labor market needs**.

**The Magic:** A PNP nomination adds **600 points to your CRS score**, making you **automatically invited** in the next draw.

### Which Provinces Need Which Skills?

| Province | Top Occupations | Min CRS | Processing |
|----------|-----------------|---------|------------|
| **Ontario** | Software engineers, accountants, nurses | 400 | 6-9 months |
| **British Columbia** | Tech workers, healthcare, skilled trades | 380 | 4-6 months |
| **Alberta** | Engineers, construction, oil & gas | 350 | 3-6 months |
| **Manitoba** | Skilled trades, healthcare, agriculture | 280 | 4-8 weeks |
| **Nova Scotia** | Healthcare, tech, skilled trades | 300 | 6-12 weeks |
| **Saskatchewan** | Healthcare, engineers, skilled trades | 290 | 6-8 weeks |

### PNP Process

1. **Research which province** needs your skills
2. **Create a profile** on the provincial website (separate from Express Entry)
3. **Wait 1-6 months** for an invitation from the province
4. **If invited:** Submit full application to the province
5. **If approved:** Province nominates you for PR
6. **You then get:** 600 CRS points automatically
7. **Next Express Entry draw:** You get ITA within weeks

## Common Rejection Reasons

Based on 2025 IRCC data, the **top 5 reasons** Nigerian Express Entry applications are rejected:

### 1. Credential Recognition Issues (22%)

**Problem:** Nigerian degree not recognized as equivalent to Canadian education.

**Why it happens:**
- Nigerian university not in IRCC's recognized list
- Degree doesn't meet Canadian standards
- Lack of English-language instruction proof

**Fix:**
- Get World Education Services (WES) **Educational Credential Assessment (ECA)**: CAD $220 (₦125,000)
- WES confirms your degree is equivalent to Canadian bachelor's
- Takes 3-4 weeks
- Include WES report in your full application

---

### 2. Work Experience Not Recognized (18%)

**Problem:** Your work experience is classified in wrong NOC code or deemed "not skilled".

**Why it happens:**
- Role doesn't meet Canada's "skilled" threshold (NOC 0, A, or B required)
- Supervisor roles miscategorized
- Seasonal or casual work not counted
- Employer letter lacks required details

**Fix:**
- Use exact job duties to find correct NOC code at https://www.noc.esdc.gc.ca/
- Get detailed work letter from employer including:
  - Exact job title
  - Main duties and responsibilities
  - Full-time hours
  - Salary/pay information
  - Employment dates

---

### 3. Language Scores Invalidated (12%)

**Problem:** Language test scores expire, don't match profile, or aren't officially received.

**Why it happens:**
- Test results expired (validity is 2 years)
- Submitted unofficial score screenshot instead of official letter
- Wrong test date or score on profile

**Fix:**
- Request official test results directly from test provider (British Council, ETS, etc.)
- Ensure test results are dated within 2 years of your application
- Have test provider send results directly to IRCC

---

### 4. Insufficient Proof of Funds (10%)

**Problem:** Bank statements don't show enough liquid funds to support yourself in Canada.

**Why it happens:**
- Bank account balance too low (see required amounts below)
- Funds are in fixed deposits or investments
- Large deposits made just before application (suspicious)
- Unclear source of money

**Required funds to prove (2026):**
- Single applicant: CAD $16,000 (₦9M)
- Spouse: CAD $20,000 (₦11.2M)
- Each child: CAD $4,500 (₦2.5M)

**Fix:**
- Maintain bank statements showing funds for 6+ months before applying
- Include sponsorship letter if someone else is funding you
- Avoid large deposits right before application (explains them if needed)

---

### 5. Background or Security Concerns (8%)

**Problem:** Police certificate denied, missing documents, or background investigation finds issues.

**Why it happens:**
- Police certificate rejected (not notarized properly, expired)
- Missing documents from one of your residence countries
- Criminal record (even minor offenses can disqualify)
- IRCC security checks find concerning patterns

**Fix:**
- Get police certificate from Nigeria Police, properly notarized
- Include police certs from ALL countries you've lived 6+ months
- Disclose **any** criminal record honestly (lying is grounds for permanent ban)
- Provide clear explanation letters if background has gaps

## FAQ

### Q: Can I apply to Express Entry while I'm in Nigeria?

**A:** Yes! You **don't need to be in Canada** to apply.

- Create profile in Nigeria
- Get documents while in Nigeria
- When you receive ITA, submit application from Nigeria
- Arrange medical exam at IRCC-approved clinic in Lagos
- Get police certificate from Nigeria
- Once approved, travel to Canada with your CoPR letter

---

### Q: What if I already have a job offer from a Canadian employer?

**A:** Your job offer **adds 200 CRS points**, which can push you above the cutoff.

**The job offer must:**
- Be in NOC 0, A, or B (skilled occupation)
- Be permanent (not temporary)
- Be full-time (30+ hours/week)
- Have approved LMIA (Labor Market Impact Assessment)

**With 200-point boost:**
- 400 CRS + 200 job offer = **600 points** (guaranteed ITA)

---

### Q: Do I need a job offer to get PR?

**A:** **No.** Job offer is optional (just worth 200 points).

Most people get PR without a job offer, then find a job after landing in Canada.

---

### Q: Can I bring my family on my Express Entry application?

**A:** **Yes.** Your spouse/partner and dependent children can be included.

**How:**
- Add them to your profile before submitting
- Their information counts toward your CRS score
- When you get PR, they get PR at the same time
- Spouse's education and language ability **adds points** to your CRS

**Cost increases:**
- Spouse/partner: +CAD $215 (₦120,000)
- Each child: +CAD $215 (₦120,000)

---

### Q: What's the difference between Express Entry and a regular work permit?

**Express Entry = Permanent Residency** (PR)
- Apply for once, approved = lifetime PR
- Can sponsor family later
- Can work anywhere in Canada
- Path to citizenship after 3 years
- Best long-term option

**Work Permit = Temporary**
- Valid for 1-3 years
- Tied to specific employer or location
- No path to PR directly (though can bridge to it)
- Family cannot follow easily
- Must leave when permit expires

**Express Entry is the goal** because it's permanent.

---

### Q: What happens if I'm rejected?

**A:** You can reapply anytime.

**What to do:**
1. Request detailed rejection reason from IRCC
2. Fix the specific issue (usually credential recognition, work experience, or background)
3. Reapply with corrected documents
4. Many applicants succeed on 2nd attempt

---

### Q: Can I switch from a work permit to Express Entry?

**A:** **Yes.** If you're already working in Canada on a work permit:

**Path to PR via Express Entry:**
1. Continue working in Canada (3+ years needed)
2. Use your Canadian work experience to apply for **Canadian Experience Class (CEC)**
3. CEC requires 3+ years Canadian work experience
4. CEC usually lower CRS cutoff than FSW
5. Get ITA and PR within 6 months

**This is often easier** than trying to get sponsored by an employer.

---

### Q: How much does Express Entry actually cost (all-in)?

**Official Fees:**
- Application fee: CAD $715 (₦400,000)
- Right of Permanent Residency fee: CAD $500 (₦280,000)
- **Subtotal:** CAD $1,215 (₦680,000)

**Supporting Costs:**
- Language test (IELTS): ₦60,000
- Credential assessment (WES): CAD $220 (₦125,000)
- Police certificate: ₦10,000
- Medical exam: ₦50,000-100,000
- **Subtotal:** ~₦245,000-295,000

**TOTAL ALL-IN:** ~CAD $2,300-2,500 (₦1.3M-1.4M)

---

### Q: If I get PR, can I bring my parents later?

**A:** **Yes**, but as a separate sponsorship.

**After you get PR:**
- Wait 1 year to establish Canadian residence
- Become Canadian citizen (optional, but helps)
- Sponsor your parents through **Family Class sponsorship**
- Parents can become PR (takes 12-18 months)

**Parent sponsorship requires:**
- You must have CAD $40,000+ income/assets (to support parents)
- Parents must pass medical/security checks
- Takes 12-18 months (slower than Express Entry)

## Final Thoughts

Express Entry is **Canada's fastest path to PR** for skilled workers. The process is transparent, efficient, and designed to give you permanent residency within 6-12 months.

**For Nigerian professionals with:**
- Bachelor's degree or higher
- 2+ years work experience
- IELTS 7.5+ (CLB 8)

**Your CRS score can reach 420-500 range**, putting you competitive for draws within 6-12 months.

**The three-pronged strategy to guarantee ITA:**
1. **Maximize your CRS:** Get IELTS CLB 9, reach 3+ years experience, add French
2. **Consider PNP:** Research provinces that need your skills (400+ CRS = automatic invite)
3. **Get job offer bonus:** If you have Canadian connections, a job offer adds 200 points

**Start today:** Create your Express Entry profile even if you don't have all documents yet. See your CRS score. Then decide if you need PNP or can apply solo.
