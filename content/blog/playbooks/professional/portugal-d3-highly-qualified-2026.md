Portugal's D3 visa sits between the standard work visa and the Digital Nomad (D8) route. It is for professionals who have a qualifying employment contract or service agreement with a Portuguese entity - not a foreign employer, not remote work for a non-Portuguese client, but a direct relationship with a Portuguese company or institution.

The D3 is faster to process than the standard work visa, carries a direct 5-year PR vector, and is available to tech specialists, researchers, senior managers and executives. For African professionals with a job offer from a Portuguese employer, it is the most direct route to long-term residence.

## What the D3 is and who it is for

The D3 Highly Qualified Activity Residence Visa is issued under Portuguese immigration law for third-country nationals who will perform highly qualified activities in Portugal. The legal basis is Article 61-A of Law 23/2007 (the Portuguese Foreigners Act), as amended.

Qualifying activities include:

- **Senior management roles** - directors, C-suite executives, heads of department at Portuguese companies
- **Specialist technical roles** - software engineers, data scientists, architects, engineers, financial specialists requiring a degree or equivalent professional qualification
- **Research roles** - researchers at Portuguese universities, research institutes or R&D departments
- **Teaching roles** - lecturers and professors at accredited Portuguese higher education institutions

The activity must be performed under a qualifying employment contract or service agreement with a Portuguese entity. The remuneration must be above the Portuguese minimum wage (EUR 1,020/month in 2026).

## Qualification requirements

You must hold a higher education degree or equivalent professional qualification relevant to the highly qualified activity. For non-degree holders, AIMA requires a minimum of 3 years of relevant professional experience demonstrating equivalent competence.

Your degree does not need to be recognised in Portugal before applying - AIMA assesses equivalence as part of the D3 application. However, for regulated professions (medicine, law, engineering in some contexts), professional recognition may be required separately.

## The income threshold

AIMA does not publish a single fixed income threshold for the D3 visa. The requirement is that your remuneration is above the Portuguese minimum wage and is consistent with the highly qualified nature of the activity.

In practice, D3 applicants are expected to earn significantly above the minimum wage. A software engineer or senior manager earning EUR 2,000-3,000/month gross is well within the expected range. A role paying EUR 1,100/month would be scrutinised more carefully.

Your employment contract must specify the salary, role title and duration. Open-ended contracts are preferred over fixed-term contracts for the residence permit application.

## Application process

**Step 1 - Secure the employment contract.** Your Portuguese employer issues a qualifying employment contract or service agreement. The contract must specify the role, salary, start date and duration.

**Step 2 - Apply for the D3 long-stay visa.** Apply at the Portuguese consulate in your country. Required documents typically include: valid passport, employment contract, proof of qualifications, criminal record certificate, proof of accommodation in Portugal, and the visa application fee (approximately EUR 90). Processing time varies by consulate - allow 2-4 months.

**Step 3 - Enter Portugal.** The D3 long-stay visa allows you to enter Portugal and begin work.

**Step 4 - Apply for residence permit at AIMA.** Within 4 months of arrival, apply to AIMA (Agencia para a Integracao, Migracoes e Asilo) for the residence permit. The residence permit is initially valid for 2 years and is renewable.

**Step 5 - Renewal.** Renew the residence permit before expiry. Renewals are typically for 3-year periods after the initial 2-year permit.

## PR and citizenship vector

After 5 years of legal residence in Portugal, you can apply for permanent residence (autorizacao de residencia permanente). The 5 years must be continuous - absences of more than 6 consecutive months or 8 months total in any 2-year period can interrupt the count.

After 5 years of legal residence, you can also apply for Portuguese citizenship. Portuguese citizenship gives you an EU passport and freedom of movement, work and residence across all 27 EU member states.

## D3 vs D8 Digital Nomad - the key difference

The D8 Digital Nomad visa requires that you work remotely for a foreign employer or foreign clients - your income must come from outside Portugal. The D3 requires the opposite - your employer or client must be a Portuguese entity.

If you have a job offer from a Portuguese company, D3 is the correct route. If you work remotely for a non-Portuguese employer, D8 is the correct route. The two are mutually exclusive.

## NHR tax regime

Portugal's Non-Habitual Resident (NHR) tax regime historically offered a flat 20% income tax rate for qualifying professionals for 10 years. The NHR regime was reformed in 2024 - the current version (IFICI) applies to specific categories including technology and innovation professionals. Check current NHR/IFICI eligibility with a Portuguese tax adviser before relying on it in your financial planning.

## Proof of funds

The indicative setup cost for a D3 applicant is approximately EUR 2,580 (USD 2,800) covering: D3 visa fee (EUR 90), AIMA residence permit fee (approximately EUR 320), initial accommodation deposit (approximately EUR 1,500 for first and last month), and incidentals. Your employment salary covers ongoing living costs.
