Mauritius is the only African country with a passport that opens the EU Schengen area, the UK, Singapore and most of Asia without a prior visa appointment. It is also the only African country with a dedicated remote work visa, a holding company structure used by global private equity, and a retirement permit that requires no employment at all.

Most guides cover one route. This post covers all six — and explains the 5-year residence play that most people miss.

## The six routes at a glance

| Route | Who it is for | Duration | Leads to PR? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Premium Visa | Remote workers, freelancers | 1 year, renewable | No (but can transition) |
| Occupation Permit — Professional | Employed by Mauritius company | 3 years, renewable | Yes — 10-year permit after 3 years |
| Occupation Permit — Investor | Running a Mauritius business | 3 years, renewable | Yes — 10-year permit after 3 years |
| Occupation Permit — Self-Employed | Independent professional | 3 years, renewable | Yes — 10-year permit after 3 years |
| Student Permit | Enrolled at Mauritius institution | Duration of course | No (can transition to OP) |
| Retirement Permit | Age 50+, passive income | 10 years, renewable | Yes — renewable indefinitely |

## Route 1 — Premium Visa (remote workers and digital nomads)

The Premium Visa is Mauritius's dedicated route for remote workers. You live in Mauritius while your income comes from outside the country.

**Who qualifies:**
- Employees working remotely for a foreign employer
- Freelancers and consultants with clients outside Mauritius
- Digital entrepreneurs with non-Mauritius revenue

**Requirements:**
- Valid passport (6+ months validity)
- Proof of remote employment or self-employment (contract, client agreements, or employer letter)
- Proof of accommodation in Mauritius
- Health insurance covering Mauritius
- Clean criminal record certificate

**Income threshold:** The EDB does not publish a fixed minimum. In practice, USD 2,000-3,000/month is the expected range. There is no published application fee.

**Duration:** 1 year, renewable. No published limit on renewals.

**What it does not give you:** The Premium Visa is not a work permit. You cannot work for a Mauritius-based employer on this visa. It does not directly lead to permanent residence — but after 2+ years on a Premium Visa, you can apply to transition to an Occupation Permit if you establish a Mauritius business or find local employment.

**Tax position:** Mauritius operates a territorial tax system. Foreign-sourced income is not taxed in Mauritius. The 15% flat income tax rate applies only to Mauritius-sourced income. Remote workers earning entirely from outside Mauritius may have minimal Mauritius tax liability — confirm with a local tax adviser.

**Primary source:** `https://www.edbmauritius.org/`

## Route 2 — Occupation Permit: Professional (employed professionals)

The Occupation Permit (Professional) is for foreign nationals employed by a Mauritius-registered company. It is the most direct route to long-term residence.

**Requirements:**
- Confirmed job offer from a Mauritius employer
- Minimum monthly salary: MUR 60,000 (approximately USD 1,300)
- Relevant qualification or experience for the role
- Application submitted by the employer to the EDB

**Duration:** 3 years, renewable.

**Path to 10-year permit:** After 3 continuous years on an Occupation Permit, you can apply for a 10-year Occupation Permit. This is the key milestone on the path to long-term residence.

**Family:** Dependants (spouse and children under 24) can join on a Dependent Permit.

**Primary source:** `https://www.edbmauritius.org/`

---

## Route 3 — Occupation Permit: Investor (business owners)

The Occupation Permit (Investor) is for foreign nationals who establish and run a business in Mauritius.

**Requirements:**
- Minimum initial investment: USD 50,000
- Minimum annual turnover: USD 100,000 from year 2
- Business must be registered in Mauritius
- Application to the EDB

**Duration:** 3 years, renewable. Leads to 10-year permit after 3 years.

**Who uses it:** African entrepreneurs who want a Mauritius-based business — not a holding company (that is the GBL route), but an operating business serving the Mauritius or regional market.

**Primary source:** `https://www.edbmauritius.org/`

---

## Route 4 — Occupation Permit: Self-Employed (independent professionals)

The Self-Employed category is for professionals who want to work independently in Mauritius — consultants, architects, accountants, IT specialists — without being employed by a Mauritius company.

**Requirements:**
- Relevant professional qualification
- Minimum annual income: MUR 600,000 (approximately USD 13,000/year)
- Business registration in Mauritius

**Duration:** 3 years, renewable. Leads to 10-year permit after 3 years.

---

## Route 5 — Student Permit

The Student Permit allows foreign nationals to study at a recognised Mauritius institution.

**Requirements:**
- Acceptance letter from a recognised Mauritius institution
- Proof of financial means to cover tuition and living costs
- Valid passport
- Application to the Passport and Immigration Office (PIO)

**Duration:** Duration of the course.

**What it does not give you:** The Student Permit does not lead directly to permanent residence. However, graduates who find employment in Mauritius can apply for an Occupation Permit (Professional) — transitioning from student to long-term resident.

**Why Mauritius for study:** Mauritius has several internationally recognised institutions including the University of Mauritius and Middlesex University Mauritius. English is an official language. Tuition fees are lower than UK or Australian equivalents.

**Primary source:** `https://passport.govmu.org/`

---

## Route 6 — Retirement Non-Citizen Permit

The Retirement Permit is for persons aged 50 and above who want to live in Mauritius on passive income.

**Requirements:**
- Age 50 or above
- Transfer of minimum USD 1,500/month (or USD 18,000/year) into a Mauritius bank account
- Valid passport
- Health insurance

**Duration:** 10 years, renewable.

**What it gives you:** Long-term legal residence in Mauritius with no employment or business requirement. The 10-year permit is renewable indefinitely as long as the income transfer requirement is met.

**Path to further residence:** After 10 years on a Retirement Permit, you have established a long-term residence history in Mauritius. This strengthens any future application for naturalisation, though citizenship is not automatic.

**Primary source:** `https://www.edbmauritius.org/`

## The 5-year residence play — what most guides miss

The Mauritius passport is the strongest in Africa. Visa-free access to approximately 156 countries including the EU Schengen area, the UK, Singapore, Japan and most of Asia. For African professionals who currently hold a Nigerian, Ghanaian or Kenyan passport — which opens far fewer doors — Mauritian citizenship is a meaningful upgrade.

Here is the path:

**Step 1 — Establish legal residence (Year 1-3)**
Enter on a Premium Visa or Occupation Permit. Build genuine ties to Mauritius: bank account, accommodation, tax registration.

**Step 2 — Transition to Occupation Permit if on Premium Visa (Year 2-3)**
The Premium Visa does not lead to permanent residence. To build toward long-term status, transition to an Occupation Permit by establishing a Mauritius business (Investor category, USD 50,000 minimum) or finding local employment (Professional category, MUR 60,000/month minimum).

**Step 3 — Apply for 10-year Occupation Permit (Year 3)**
After 3 continuous years on an Occupation Permit, apply for the 10-year permit. This is the key milestone — it gives you stable, long-term legal residence.

**Step 4 — Build toward naturalisation (Year 5+)**
Mauritius citizenship by naturalisation requires a period of continuous legal residence and is at the discretion of the Minister of Home Affairs. The exact qualifying period is not fixed by statute in the same way as some countries — it is assessed on a case-by-case basis. Genuine residence, economic contribution, and clean record are the key factors.

**The honest caveat:** Mauritian citizenship is not a guaranteed outcome of 5 years' residence. It is a discretionary process. The 10-year permit is the realistic target for most applicants — it provides stable long-term residence and the practical benefits of living in Mauritius without the uncertainty of a citizenship application.

## Why Mauritius works as a base

**Passport strength:** ~156 countries visa-free. The strongest in Africa by a significant margin.

**Tax:** 15% flat income tax. No capital gains tax. Territorial system — foreign income not taxed. No inheritance tax.

**Financial centre:** Well-developed banking sector. MCB, SBM, Absa Mauritius. Straightforward account opening for permit holders.

**Connectivity:** Island-wide 4G/5G and fibre. Co-working spaces in Port Louis and Grand Baie.

**Time zone:** UTC+4. Overlaps with European morning hours and East African business hours throughout the day.

**Language:** English and French are official languages. All government processes are in English.

**Cost of living:** Higher than Nigeria or Ghana, lower than the UK or UAE. A comfortable lifestyle costs approximately USD 2,000-3,500/month depending on accommodation choice.

**Safety:** Mauritius consistently ranks as one of the safest countries in Africa.
