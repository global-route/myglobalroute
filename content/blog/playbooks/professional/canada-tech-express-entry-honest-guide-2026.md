You have seen the ads. "Canada needs tech talent." "Software developers, data analysts, DevOps engineers — Canada has strong demand for your skills." "Get PR in less than 11 months."

The first two claims are true. The third is misleading. Here is what the process actually looks like.

## What is true

Canada does have documented demand for tech workers. The following roles are all NOC TEER 0 or TEER 1 occupations — the highest skill tier — and all qualify for the Federal Skilled Worker Program (FSWP) under Express Entry:

| Role | NOC Code | TEER |
|---|---|---|
| Software Developer | 21232 | 1 |
| Data Scientist / Analyst | 21211 | 1 |
| Cybersecurity Analyst | 21220 | 1 |
| DevOps / Cloud Engineer | 21223 | 1 |
| Network Engineer | 21222 | 1 |
| QA / Test Engineer | 21231 | 1 |
| Web Developer | 21234 | 1 |
| IT Project Manager | 20012 | 0 |
| Computer Engineer | 21311 | 1 |
| Electrical Engineer | 21310 | 1 |

Primary source: [canada.ca/express-entry/federal-skilled-workers](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/who-can-apply/federal-skilled-workers.html)

The salary range cited in the ads — CAD 70,000 to CAD 120,000 — is broadly accurate for mid-level tech roles in Canada. Verified from Job Bank Canada (jobbank.gc.ca):

- Software Developer: median CAD 85,000 (range CAD 70,000-110,000)
- Data Scientist: median CAD 95,000 (range CAD 75,000-120,000)
- Cybersecurity Analyst: median CAD 88,000 (range CAD 70,000-105,000)
- DevOps / Cloud Engineer: median CAD 100,000 (range CAD 80,000-130,000)

These are real numbers. The opportunity is real.

## What the ads get wrong

**"Get PR in less than 11 months"**

This claim conflates two separate timelines:

1. IRCC's processing target after you receive an Invitation to Apply (ITA): 6 months
2. The time it takes to receive an ITA: depends entirely on your CRS score and when IRCC runs draws

The ads imply that if you are eligible, you will get PR in 11 months. That is not how it works. You can be eligible and sit in the pool for 12-18 months without receiving an invitation if your CRS score is not high enough.

Recent tech category draw cutoffs have ranged from 481 to 500 CRS. A typical Nigerian applicant with a bachelor's degree, 3 years of experience, and IELTS 7.0 scores approximately 440-460 CRS. That gap matters.

**"Free PR eligibility check in 60 seconds"**

This is a CRS calculator, not an eligibility assessment. It tells you your approximate score. It does not tell you whether your degree will be recognised, whether your work experience will be accepted, or when the next draw will happen. It is a lead capture tool.

## The actual eligibility requirements

To enter the Express Entry pool under FSWP:

**Points threshold:** Minimum 67 points on the FSWP selection factors (education, language, experience, age, arranged employment, adaptability). This is the pool entry requirement — not the CRS score.

**Work experience:** At least 1 year of continuous full-time (or equivalent part-time) paid skilled work experience in a NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 occupation in the past 10 years. The experience can be anywhere in the world — it does not have to be in Canada.

**Language:** CLB 7 minimum in all four skills. For IELTS General Training: 6.0 in each band. Higher scores earn significantly more CRS points — going from IELTS 7.0 to 8.0 can add 30-50 CRS points.

**Education:** A bachelor's degree or higher. Foreign degrees must be assessed by WES or another designated organisation. Allow 3-5 months for WES assessment. Cost: approximately USD 220 for a basic assessment.

**Settlement funds:** CAD 14,690 for a single applicant (2026 figure). This is a proof-of-funds balance, not a cost — you must show you have this available, not spend it.

## How CRS score is calculated

Your CRS score is built from four factors:

**Core human capital (up to 500 points):**
- Age: maximum points at 20-29, declining after 30
- Education: bachelor's = 120 points, master's = 135 points, PhD = 150 points
- Language (first official language): IELTS 9.0 in all bands = maximum points
- Canadian work experience: 0 if none

**Spouse/partner factors (up to 40 points if applicable)**

**Skill transferability (up to 100 points):**
- Education + language combination
- Education + Canadian work experience combination
- Foreign work experience + language combination

**Additional points (up to 600 points):**
- Provincial nomination: 600 points (near-guaranteed invitation)
- Job offer: 50-200 points depending on NOC level
- Canadian study: 15-30 points
- French language: 15-50 points
- Sibling in Canada: 15 points

A typical profile — Nigerian, age 28, bachelor's degree, 3 years experience, IELTS 7.0 in all bands, no job offer, no Canadian experience — scores approximately 440-460 CRS. Recent draw cutoffs for tech category draws have been 481-500. The gap is real and requires a strategy to close.

## How to improve your CRS score

**Retake IELTS:** Going from 7.0 to 8.0 in all bands adds approximately 30-50 CRS points. This is the highest-return action for most applicants.

**Get a provincial nomination:** Provincial Nominee Programs (PNPs) add 600 CRS points — effectively guaranteeing an invitation. Ontario, British Columbia, and Alberta all have tech streams. Research the specific PNP requirements for your occupation.

**Get a Canadian job offer:** A valid job offer from a Canadian employer at NOC TEER 0 adds 200 points; TEER 1-3 adds 50 points. This requires actively applying to Canadian employers and interviewing remotely.

**French language:** If you speak French at CLB 7 or above, you earn additional CRS points. Combined with English, this can add 50 points.

## What the process looks like step by step

1. Get IELTS General Training results (allow 4-6 weeks)
2. Get WES educational credential assessment (allow 3-5 months)
3. Create Express Entry profile on IRCC portal
4. Enter the pool — your CRS score is calculated automatically
5. Wait for an Invitation to Apply (ITA) — timing depends on CRS score and draw schedule
6. Receive ITA — you have 60 days to submit a complete application
7. Submit application with all documents
8. IRCC processes application — target 6 months
9. Receive Confirmation of Permanent Residence (COPR)
10. Complete landing in Canada

## What to watch out for

**Agents claiming to guarantee an ITA:** No one can guarantee an ITA. IRCC controls the draw schedule and cutoffs. Any agent making this claim is lying.

**"Slots available" offers:** No legitimate Canadian immigration process has slots. Canada work permits and PR applications are processed individually. Any offer with "limited slots" is a scam pattern.

**RCIC requirement:** In Canada, only Regulated Canadian Immigration Consultants (RCICs) can legally provide immigration advice for a fee. If an agent is offering Canadian PR services from Nigeria without an RCIC number, they are operating outside the law. Verify RCIC registration at iccrc-crcic.ca.

**Processing time claims:** IRCC targets 6 months after ITA. Total timeline including pool wait is typically 12-24 months. "PR in 3-6 months" is not realistic for most applicants.

## The honest summary

The opportunity is real. Canada does need tech workers. The roles listed in the ads are genuine NOC TEER 0/1 occupations. The salary range is accurate.

The process is slower and more competitive than the ads suggest. Your CRS score matters more than your eligibility. The most common gap for Nigerian applicants is IELTS score — improving from 7.0 to 8.0 is the single highest-return action before submitting an Express Entry profile.

Start with IELTS. Then WES. Then build your profile with accurate information and wait for a draw. That is the honest version.
