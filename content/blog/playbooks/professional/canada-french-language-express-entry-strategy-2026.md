On October 27, 2026, Canada invited 5,000 Express Entry candidates through a French-language draw. The CRS cut-off was 407.

In the same period, general program Express Entry draws required CRS scores of 490 or above.

That is an 83-point gap. For context, 83 CRS points is roughly equivalent to a provincial nomination — one of the most valuable things you can add to an Express Entry profile. French language proficiency at CLB 7 creates a structural advantage in Canada's immigration system that most Nigerian applicants are not using.

Primary source: [canada.ca — Category-based selection rounds of invitations](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/submit-profile/rounds-invitations/category-based-selection.html)

## Why French draws have lower cut-offs

Canada has a policy goal of increasing francophone immigration outside Quebec. Quebec manages its own immigration system — the federal Express Entry system targets the other nine provinces and three territories.

French-speaking communities in Ontario, Manitoba, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, and other provinces are declining as a share of the population. IRCC uses category-based selection draws to specifically invite French-speaking candidates, separate from the general pool.

The result: French-language draws pull from a smaller pool of candidates, so the CRS cut-off is lower. This is not a loophole — it is deliberate policy.

## The CRS advantage in numbers

| Draw type | CRS cut-off (Oct 27, 2026) | Invitations |
|---|---|---|
| French-language category | 407 | 5,000 |
| General program (typical) | 490+ | Varies |
| Difference | ~83 points | — |

83 points is significant. Here is what adds roughly 83 points to a typical profile:
- A provincial nomination: +600 points (far more)
- A Canadian job offer (NOC 0/A/B): +50-200 points
- A sibling in Canada: +15 points
- Improving IELTS from CLB 9 to CLB 10: ~5-10 points

French proficiency at CLB 7 is one of the most accessible ways to add meaningful CRS points for candidates who are already French speakers or willing to invest in French preparation.

## What you need

**French language test:** TEF Canada or TCF Canada. These are the only accepted French tests for Express Entry. DELF and DALF are not accepted.

**Minimum level:** CLB 7 in all four skills (reading, writing, listening, speaking) to qualify for the French-language category. Higher scores add more CRS points.

**CLB 7 in TEF Canada approximately equals:**
- Listening: 309-348
- Speaking: 310-348
- Reading: 207-232
- Writing: 310-348

**Where to take TEF Canada:** Alliance Française centres in Lagos, Abuja, and Port Harcourt administer TEF Canada. Book early — test slots fill quickly.

**Where to take TCF Canada:** Also available at Alliance Française centres. TCF Canada is generally considered slightly easier to prepare for than TEF Canada — both are accepted equally by IRCC.

## How French points work in CRS

Your CRS score includes points for:

1. **First official language (English):** Up to 136 points for CLB 10 in all four skills
2. **Second official language (French):** Up to 24 additional points for CLB 7+ in all four skills

If you have strong English (CLB 9-10) and add French at CLB 7, you gain up to 24 additional CRS points from the second language component alone. Combined with the lower cut-off in French-language draws, this is a compounding advantage.

## Who this works for

**Already speaks French:** Nigerians who attended French-medium schools, lived in francophone countries (Benin, Cameroon, Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal), or studied French formally are the primary beneficiaries. If you already speak French at a conversational level, CLB 7 is achievable with focused test preparation.

**Willing to learn French:** If you are serious about Canada PR and your CRS score is stuck below 470, investing 6-12 months in French preparation to reach CLB 7 is a legitimate strategy. The CRS advantage is structural and ongoing — it is not a one-time draw anomaly.

**Caregivers and PSWs:** The Francophone Community Immigration Pilot (FCIP) specifically targets French-speaking caregivers and personal support workers. See the [Canada Francophone Immigration for Caregivers post](/blog/playbooks/professional/canada-francophone-immigration-caregivers-2026/) for the employer-driven route.

## Preparation resources

**Alliance Française Nigeria:** Lagos (`alliancefrancaise.org.ng`), Abuja, Port Harcourt. Offers French courses from beginner to advanced, and administers TEF Canada and TCF Canada.

**TV5Monde:** Free French-language television and online content. Useful for listening comprehension.

**RFI (Radio France Internationale):** Free French-language radio and podcasts. `rfi.fr/fr/podcasts`

**Duolingo:** Useful for vocabulary building at beginner level. Not sufficient alone for CLB 7 — supplement with structured courses.

**TEF Canada preparation books:** Available from Alliance Française and online. Practice with timed tests under exam conditions.
