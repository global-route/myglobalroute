A visa refusal letter is a document. It tells you specifically why your application was refused. Most applicants read it once, feel discouraged, and either give up or reapply with the same documents. Neither approach works.

This case study covers three refusal patterns — Schengen tourist, UK visitor, and Canada Express Entry — with the specific reasons cited and what changed in successful reapplications.

## Case 1 — Schengen tourist visa refusal (Germany)

**Applicant:** Adaeze, 26, marketing executive, Lagos. Applied for a 10-day Schengen tourist visa to Germany. Refused.

**Refusal reason (from letter):**
> "The information submitted regarding the justification for the purpose and conditions of the intended stay was not reliable. You have not provided sufficient proof of your intention to leave the territory of the Member States before the expiry of the visa."

**What this means:** The consulate was not convinced she would return to Nigeria. This is the most common Schengen refusal reason for Nigerian applicants. It is not about the trip itself — it is about ties to Nigeria.

**What was weak in the original application:**
- Bank statement showed NGN 450,000 (approximately EUR 280) — low for a 10-day trip to Germany
- Employment letter was generic — did not specify her role, salary, or that she had approved leave
- No evidence of property ownership or other ties to Nigeria
- No previous travel history

**What changed for the successful reapplication (4 months later):**
- Bank statement showed NGN 2,800,000 (approximately EUR 1,750) — 3 months of consistent balance
- New employment letter on company letterhead, signed by HR director, specifying her role (Marketing Manager), monthly salary (NGN 380,000), and that she had 10 days approved annual leave
- Added: tenancy agreement for her Lagos apartment
- Added: letter from her employer confirming she was expected back at work on a specific date
- Added: evidence of a previous Ghana visa (showed travel history and return)

**Result:** Approved on reapplication.

**Key lesson:** The refusal was about ties to Nigeria, not about the trip. Every document in the reapplication was designed to answer one question: why will she come back?

## Case 2 — UK Standard Visitor visa refusal

**Applicant:** Tunde, 34, software engineer, Abuja. Applied for a UK Standard Visitor visa to attend a tech conference. Refused.

**Refusal reason (from letter):**
> "I am not satisfied that you are a genuine visitor. I note that you have not provided sufficient evidence of your financial circumstances in Nigeria. I am not satisfied that you have sufficient funds to cover the cost of your visit without recourse to public funds or employment in the UK."

**What was weak:**
- Bank statement showed NGN 1.2M but with a large deposit 2 weeks before application — a pattern UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) specifically flags as "funds parking"
- No payslips provided — only a bank statement
- Conference registration was provided but no hotel booking or return flight

**What changed for the successful reapplication (6 months later):**
- Bank statement showing 6 months of consistent salary credits (NGN 380,000/month) — no large sudden deposits
- 6 months of payslips from his employer
- Hotel booking confirmation (refundable)
- Return flight booking (refundable)
- Conference registration and invitation letter
- Letter from employer confirming his role, salary, and that the company was sponsoring his conference attendance
- Added: evidence of previous travel (Ghana, South Africa) with return stamps

**Result:** Approved on reapplication.

**Key lesson:** The UK specifically looks for "funds parking" — a large deposit shortly before application. 6 months of consistent salary credits is far more convincing than a large balance with no history.

## Case 3 — Canada Express Entry refusal

**Applicant:** Emeka, 29, accountant, Lagos. Submitted an Express Entry application after receiving an ITA with CRS 471. Refused at the PR stage.

**Refusal reason (from letter):**
> "You have not satisfied the officer that you meet the requirements of the Federal Skilled Worker class. Specifically, you have not provided sufficient evidence of your claimed work experience in NOC 11100 (Financial auditors and accountants)."

**What was weak:**
- Reference letter from employer was a single paragraph — did not describe specific duties
- The duties described did not clearly match the NOC 11100 lead statement and main duties
- No supporting documents (payslips, tax returns, professional membership)

**What changed for the successful reapplication:**

A new Express Entry profile was submitted. When the next ITA was received:

- New reference letter from employer: 2 pages, on company letterhead, signed by direct supervisor and HR director. Listed specific duties matching the NOC 11100 main duties verbatim (auditing financial records, preparing financial statements, analysing financial data, advising on financial matters).
- Added: 2 years of payslips
- Added: ICAN (Institute of Chartered Accountants of Nigeria) membership certificate
- Added: tax clearance certificate from FIRS
- Added: promotion letter showing career progression

**Result:** Approved on reapplication.

**Key lesson:** NOC matching is precise. The reference letter must describe duties that match the NOC lead statement and main duties. Generic letters ("responsible for financial matters") do not work. Specific letters ("prepared monthly financial statements in accordance with IFRS, audited accounts for 12 client companies per year") do.

## The pattern across all three cases

| Case | Core refusal reason | What fixed it |
|---|---|---|
| Schengen | Insufficient ties to Nigeria | Employment letter with salary + return date + tenancy agreement |
| UK | Funds parking / insufficient financial evidence | 6 months consistent salary history + payslips |
| Canada | Insufficient work experience evidence | Detailed reference letter matching NOC duties + supporting documents |

In every case, the refusal letter told the applicant exactly what was missing. The fix was to provide what was asked for — not to reapply with the same documents and hope for a different result.

## What not to do after a refusal

- Do not reapply immediately with the same documents
- Do not pay an agent who promises to "fix" your refusal without explaining what they will change
- Do not lie about previous refusals on future applications — this is grounds for permanent ban
- Do not assume the refusal was arbitrary — read the letter carefully
