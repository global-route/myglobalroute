The UAE is one of the most active destinations for Nigerian skilled workers — construction, healthcare, hospitality, and facilities management all have documented demand. Unlike Germany or Canada, the UAE does not require language preparation or a points score. The barrier is lower, but the process has specific steps.

This case study covers two profiles: a skilled electrician and a registered nurse. Exchange rate used: AED 1 = NGN 110.

## Profile A — Skilled electrician

Chidi, 28, electrician, Lagos. City and Guilds Level 3 electrical installation. 4 years experience. Goal: UAE employer-sponsored work permit, Dubai or Abu Dhabi.

## Profile B — Registered nurse

Ngozi, 32, registered nurse, Abuja. BNSc Nursing (University of Abuja, 2015). 6 years experience. Goal: UAE nursing registration, Dubai Health Authority (DHA) licence.

## Key differences from Germany/Canada

| Factor | UAE | Germany | Canada |
|---|---|---|---|
| Language requirement | None | B2 German | IELTS 6.0+ |
| Preparation time | 3-6 months | 18-24 months | 6-12 months |
| Out-of-pocket cost | NGN 165,000-495,000 | NGN 3.6M | NGN 1.5M+ |
| Path to permanent residence | No (UAE has no PR for most workers) | Yes (after 5 years) | Yes (direct PR routes) |
| Family | Limited (salary threshold applies) | Yes | Yes |

The UAE is the lowest-barrier, lowest-cost entry point. The trade-off is no permanent residence pathway for most workers — you are on a renewable work permit indefinitely.

## What to watch for

**Salary substitution:** Verbal offer higher than written contract. Read the contract before signing.

**Accommodation deductions:** Confirm whether accommodation is free or deducted from salary.

**Passport retention:** Illegal under UAE law. Report to MOHRE if an employer asks to keep your passport.

**Upfront fees:** Legitimate employers do not charge workers visa or placement fees.
