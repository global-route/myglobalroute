Romania joined the Schengen Area in January 2025. It is an EU member with public universities, a functioning work permit system, and a specific citizenship repatriation program that is widely misunderstood in Nigerian migration circles. Here is the full picture.

## The citizenship repatriation program — what it actually is

Romania operates a simplified citizenship repatriation program under Articles 10 and 11 of Law 21/1991. This is not a citizenship by investment program. It is not a general naturalisation route. It is a legal remedy for a specific historical situation.

**Who qualifies:**

The program applies to persons who:
- Were Romanian citizens and lost citizenship involuntarily (e.g. due to communist-era territorial changes affecting Moldova, northern Bukovina, or southern Dobruja, or due to forced renunciation under the communist regime)
- Are descendants of such persons — up to the third generation (children, grandchildren, great-grandchildren)
- Have not committed acts against Romania's fundamental interests

**The key point for African applicants:** The vast majority of Nigerians and other Africans do not have Romanian ancestry. This program is primarily relevant to descendants of Romanians who emigrated to Africa during the communist era, or to persons of mixed Romanian-African heritage. It is not a backdoor to EU citizenship for people without genuine Romanian lineage.

**What "simplified" means:** You do not need to establish residency in Romania before applying. You apply through the Romanian embassy or consulate in your country of residence, or through the National Citizenship Authority (Autoritatea Națională pentru Cetățenie — ANC) in Bucharest.

**The process:**

1. Gather documentary evidence of your ancestor's Romanian citizenship and the circumstances of its loss
2. Obtain certified translations of all documents into Romanian
3. Submit the application to ANC or the Romanian consulate
4. ANC reviews the file — processing takes 12–24 months in practice
5. If approved, you take an oath of allegiance and receive Romanian citizenship
6. Apply for a Romanian passport

**Documents typically required:**
- Birth certificates for each generation in the lineage (applicant → parent → grandparent → Romanian ancestor)
- Marriage certificates where surnames changed
- Death certificates for deceased ancestors
- Documents proving the ancestor held Romanian citizenship
- Documents proving the circumstances of citizenship loss
- All documents must be apostilled and translated into Romanian by a certified translator

**Primary source:** Autoritatea Națională pentru Cetățenie (ANC), cetateniaromana.ro

**What agents are selling vs. what is real:** Some agents in Nigerian Telegram and WhatsApp groups advertise Romanian citizenship repatriation for fees of NGN 2–5 million with promises of 6–12 month processing. The processing timeline at ANC is officially 12 months but routinely takes 18–24 months. No agent can accelerate ANC's internal review. If an agent is charging upfront fees and promising fast processing, verify their credentials before paying anything.

## Study in Romania — what the numbers confirm

Romania's public universities are among the most affordable in the EU for non-EU international students.

| Programme | Annual tuition (EUR) |
|---|---|
| Engineering, IT, natural sciences | EUR 2,000–3,500 |
| Economics, business, law | EUR 2,500–4,000 |
| Humanities, social sciences | EUR 2,000–3,000 |
| Medicine, dentistry | EUR 5,000–9,000 |
| Pharmacy | EUR 4,000–6,000 |

Key institutions: University of Bucharest, Babeș-Bolyai University (Cluj-Napoca), Alexandru Ioan Cuza University (Iași), Politehnica University of Bucharest, Carol Davila University of Medicine and Pharmacy.

**English-taught programmes:** Available at most major universities, particularly in medicine, engineering and business. More common than in Bulgaria or Hungary. Verify the specific programme language before applying.

**Living costs:**

| Item | Monthly cost (EUR) |
|---|---|
| Shared accommodation (Bucharest) | EUR 200–350 |
| Food | EUR 150–200 |
| Transport (monthly pass) | EUR 15–20 |
| Total | EUR 400–600 |

Bucharest is cheaper than Warsaw, Prague or Budapest. Cluj-Napoca and Iași are cheaper still.

## The study visa process

**Type D long-stay visa:** Required for courses over 90 days. Applied for at the Romanian embassy or consulate serving Nigeria (the Romanian Embassy in Abuja covers Nigeria).

Requirements:
- Acceptance letter from a recognised Romanian university
- Proof of sufficient funds (approximately EUR 500/month for the duration of study)
- Health insurance
- Accommodation confirmation
- Passport valid for at least 6 months beyond the intended stay
- Visa fee: approximately EUR 120

Processing time: 4–8 weeks

After arrival, you apply for a Romanian residence permit at the local Immigration Inspectorate (Inspectoratul General pentru Imigrări — IGI) within 30 days.

**Primary source:** Romanian Ministry of Foreign Affairs, mae.ro

## Work in Romania — what the permit requires

Romania's work permit system requires employer sponsorship. The employer applies for a work permit on your behalf before you apply for a visa.

**General work permit requirements:**
- A confirmed job offer from a Romanian employer
- The employer must demonstrate the position could not be filled by a Romanian or EU citizen (labour market test)
- Minimum salary: RON 3,700/month (approximately EUR 745) — the national minimum wage as of 2026
- Skilled roles typically pay RON 5,000–8,000/month (EUR 1,000–1,600)

**EU Blue Card:** Available for highly qualified workers with a university degree and a salary of at least 1.5x the average gross salary in Romania (approximately EUR 2,100/month as of 2026). Leads to permanent residence after 5 years.

**Processing:** The employer applies to the General Inspectorate for Immigration. Processing takes 30–60 days. After approval, you apply for a Type D work visa at the Romanian consulate.

## Schengen membership — what it means

Romania joined Schengen on 1 January 2025 (air and sea borders). This means:

- A Romanian residence permit allows visa-free travel across all Schengen countries
- You can travel to Germany, France, Netherlands, Spain and other Schengen countries without a separate visa
- You cannot work in other Schengen countries without a work permit from that country — Schengen travel rights and work rights are separate

This is the same distinction that applies to Bulgaria, Croatia and other newer Schengen members.

## Path to permanent residence and citizenship (standard route)

For those who do not qualify for the repatriation program, the standard route is:

- 5 years of continuous legal residence → permanent residence permit
- 8 years of continuous legal residence → naturalisation eligibility (reduced to 5 years for those who have studied in Romania for at least 4 years)
- Romanian language proficiency required for naturalisation
- Romanian citizenship grants full EU citizenship

## What to verify before applying

1. For the repatriation program: confirm your lineage documentation is complete before engaging any agent or lawyer
2. For study: confirm the language of instruction for your specific programme directly with the university
3. For study: confirm the university is accredited by the Romanian Ministry of Education (edu.ro)
4. For work: confirm the employer is registered and the job offer is genuine before paying any agent fees
5. Verify the Romanian embassy or consulate serving Nigeria and their current appointment availability at mae.ro

## The honest picture

Romania is a legitimate and underrated EU study destination. Tuition is genuinely affordable, Schengen membership is real, and the path to permanent residence exists for those who stay.

The citizenship repatriation program is real but narrow. It applies to a specific group of people with documented Romanian ancestry. It is not a general EU citizenship route for Africans without that lineage. Anyone selling it as such is misrepresenting the program.

For students who can find English-taught programmes and manage EUR 500–600/month in living costs, Romania is a credible option — cheaper than Western Europe, inside Schengen, and with a functioning path to EU permanent residence.
