Saudi Arabia is the largest economy in the Gulf and a major employer of African professionals across healthcare, construction, engineering, IT and domestic services. Vision 2030 — the kingdom's economic diversification programme — has created new sectors and new demand. This audit covers what official sources confirm.

## Standard work visa — Kafala and Nitaqat

Saudi Arabia's standard work visa operates through the Kafala (sponsorship) system. Your employer applies for a work permit before you can apply for a visa.

**Process:**
1. Employer applies for a work permit from the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD)
2. Employer must be in the Platinum or Green Nitaqat band to sponsor new expatriate workers
3. You apply for an employment visa at the Saudi consulate in Abuja
4. You enter Saudi Arabia and complete a medical examination at an approved clinic
5. You receive your Iqama (residence permit) — valid 1-2 years, renewable

**Nitaqat bands:**

| Band | Colour | Can sponsor new expatriates? |
|---|---|---|
| Platinum | — | Yes — highest quota |
| Green | — | Yes |
| Yellow | — | No |
| Red | — | No |

Ask any employer their current Nitaqat band before accepting an offer.

## Vision 2030 — new sectors and opportunities

Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 programme is creating new employment opportunities:

| Sector | Vision 2030 target | Relevance for Africans |
|---|---|---|
| Tourism | 100 million visitors/year by 2030 | Hospitality, hotel management, tour operations |
| Entertainment | SAR 30 billion sector by 2030 | Events, media, creative industries |
| Healthcare | 70% localisation target | Nurses, doctors, technicians still in demand |
| Technology | NEOM and smart city projects | Engineers, IT professionals, project managers |
| Renewable energy | 50% renewable by 2030 | Solar/wind engineers, project managers |

**Healthcare demand:** Saudi Arabia has a documented shortage of nurses and allied health professionals. The Saudi Commission for Health Specialties (SCFHS) verifies foreign qualifications. Nigerian nurses with NMCN registration can apply for SCFHS equivalency — the process takes 3-6 months.

## Premium Residency — for high-net-worth individuals

The Premium Residency (Iqama Mumayaza) does not require employer sponsorship.

**Permanent Premium Residency:**
- One-time fee: SAR 800,000 (approximately USD 213,000 / NGN 319M)
- No annual renewal
- Work, invest and reside without employer sponsorship
- Bring dependants

**Temporary Premium Residency:**
- Annual fee: SAR 100,000 (approximately USD 26,700 / NGN 40M)
- Renewable annually
- Same rights as permanent category

**Who it is for:** High-net-worth individuals, senior executives, investors. Not a realistic route for most Nigerian applicants at current fee levels.

Primary source: [iqama.gov.sa](https://www.iqama.gov.sa/)

## Kafala reform — what has changed

Saudi Arabia has introduced reforms:
- Workers can transfer employers without the original employer's consent after 12 months of employment (Musaned portal for domestic workers, MHRSD portal for other workers)
- Exit and re-entry visas no longer require employer approval for most workers
- Wage Protection System (WPS) — employers must pay salaries through the banking system; non-payment can be reported to MHRSD

**What has not changed:** Your Iqama is still tied to your employer. Contract substitution remains a risk.

## Honest assessment

Saudi Arabia is a legitimate and high-paying work destination for Nigerian professionals in healthcare, engineering and IT. The salaries are among the highest in the Gulf. Key practical considerations:

- **Contract substitution:** A documented risk. Ensure your contract is signed and stamped before you travel. The MHRSD has a contract verification portal (musaned.com.sa for domestic workers).
- **Passport confiscation:** Illegal under Saudi labour law. Report to MHRSD or the Nigerian embassy in Riyadh (+966 11 488 7000) if it occurs.
- **Healthcare licensing:** Nigerian healthcare professionals must obtain SCFHS equivalency before practising. Budget 3-6 months and SAR 500-2,000 in fees.
- **Domestic worker risk:** The highest-risk category. Use the WAA safety checklist, verify the employer, and ensure the Nigerian embassy has your details before departure.
- **Agent verification:** Verify any agent's registration with NANTA and the Saudi Ministry of Human Resources before paying fees.

## Common rejection reasons

- Employer in Yellow or Red Nitaqat band (cannot sponsor)
- Attested documents not accepted (wrong attestation chain)
- Medical fitness certificate from unapproved clinic
- Criminal record
- Healthcare professionals without SCFHS equivalency
- Misrepresentation of qualifications or experience
