Oman is a Gulf state with a significant African workforce, particularly in construction, healthcare, domestic services and trades. The agent market is active — some claims are accurate, many are not. This audit covers what the Ministry of Labour officially confirms.

## Standard work visa — employer-sponsored

Oman's standard work visa requires employer sponsorship. Your employer must have available Omanisation quota before they can sponsor you.

**Process:**
1. Employer applies for a work permit from the Ministry of Labour (mol.gov.om)
2. Employer must have available Omanisation quota for your occupation category
3. You apply for an employment visa at the Oman consulate in Lagos or Abuja
4. You enter Oman and complete a medical examination at an approved clinic
5. You receive your residence card — valid 2 years, renewable

**Omanisation quota:** Oman requires employers to maintain a minimum ratio of Omani nationals to expatriate workers. The ratio varies by sector. Employers who have exceeded their quota cannot sponsor new expatriate workers. Confirm quota availability with any employer before accepting an offer.

**Salary ranges (verified from field research and Ministry of Labour data):**

| Sector | Typical monthly salary (OMR) | USD equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Domestic worker | 100-150 | 260-390 |
| Construction trades | 150-250 | 390-650 |
| Skilled trades (electrician, plumber, mechanic) | 200-350 | 520-910 |
| Healthcare (nurse) | 400-600 | 1,040-1,560 |
| Healthcare (doctor) | 700-1,200 | 1,820-3,120 |
| Engineering | 500-900 | 1,300-2,340 |
| IT/software | 600-1,200 | 1,560-3,120 |

**Processing time:** 4-8 weeks after employer application.

Primary source: [mol.gov.om](https://www.mol.gov.om/)

## Freelance permit — for self-employed professionals

Oman's freelance permit allows qualified professionals to work independently. It requires establishing a legitimate business presence in Oman — it is not a tourist visa with work rights.

**Requirements:**
- Proof of professional qualifications relevant to the business activity
- Registered business activity in Oman (through the Ministry of Commerce)
- Valid passport
- Clean criminal record
- Proof of financial means

**What it allows:** Working independently for multiple clients in Oman. You cannot be employed by an Omani company on a freelance permit — that requires an employment visa.

**What agents are selling:** Agents advertise "Oman 2-year freelance visa" with fees of NGN 2.5M-4M. The freelance permit system is real, but the NGN figures are agent service fees, not government fees. Government fees are paid in Omani Rials. The process requires genuine business registration — it is not a shortcut to employment.

Primary source: [mol.gov.om](https://www.mol.gov.om/)

## Kafala reform — what has changed

Oman has introduced reforms to the Kafala system:
- Workers can transfer employers without the original employer's consent after completing their contract period
- The "absconding" report system has been modified — employers cannot file absconding reports for workers who have completed their contracts
- Workers can file complaints with the Ministry of Labour if employers withhold wages or passports

**What has not changed:** Your residence permit is still tied to your employer. If you leave before your contract ends without agreement, you may face legal complications.

## Honest assessment

Oman is a legitimate work destination for Nigerian trades workers, healthcare professionals and engineers. The salaries are lower than UAE or Qatar but the cost of living is also lower. Key practical considerations:

- **Passport confiscation:** Illegal in Oman. If an employer confiscates your passport, report it to the Ministry of Labour or the Nigerian embassy in Muscat (+968 2469 5000).
- **Contract substitution:** A known risk in the Gulf. Ensure your contract is signed before you travel and matches what you were promised. The Ministry of Labour has a contract verification portal.
- **Domestic worker risk:** Domestic worker roles carry the highest risk of exploitation. The WAA safety gate applies — verify the employer, get a contract, and ensure the Nigerian embassy has your details.
- **Agent verification:** Verify any agent's registration with the Nigerian Association of Travel Agents (NANTA) or the Oman Ministry of Labour before paying fees.

## Common rejection reasons

- Employer has no available Omanisation quota
- Attested documents not accepted (wrong attestation chain — must be MOFA Nigeria → Oman embassy)
- Medical fitness certificate from unapproved clinic
- Criminal record (any conviction is typically disqualifying)
- Misrepresentation of qualifications
