Kenya is East Africa's largest economy and the regional hub for technology, financial services and professional services. Nigerian nationals can enter Kenya without a visa - but working legally requires a work permit. Here is what official sources confirm.

## Entry - visa-free for Nigerians

Nigerian nationals can enter Kenya without a visa for up to 90 days. You receive an entry stamp on arrival at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport or other ports of entry.

**This does not give you the right to work.** Visa-free entry is for tourism and short business visits. Working in Kenya requires a work permit.

**Primary source:** `https://www.immigration.go.ke/`

## Work permit - Class G (specific employment)

The Class G work permit is the standard route for foreign nationals employed by a Kenyan company.

**Requirements:**
- Confirmed job offer from a registered Kenyan employer
- The employer must demonstrate that no Kenyan national was available for the role (labour market test)
- Relevant qualifications and work experience
- Valid passport, police clearance, medical certificate

**Processing time:** 2-3 months.

**Special Pass:** While your Class G permit is being processed, you can apply for a Special Pass (valid 3 months) to start work legally. This is particularly useful for professionals who need to start immediately.

## Class M permit - investors and business owners

The Class M permit is for foreign investors and business owners.

**Requirements:**
- Minimum investment threshold (varies by sector - check with Kenya Investment Authority)
- Registered business in Kenya
- Valid passport

**Primary source:** `https://www.kenyainvestment.go.ke/`

## Why Kenya for African professionals

**Silicon Savannah:** Nairobi's technology ecosystem is the most developed in Africa. Key employers include Safaricom (M-Pesa), Andela, Twiga Foods, and hundreds of startups. For tech professionals, Nairobi offers more opportunities than most other African cities.

**Financial services:** Nairobi is East Africa's financial capital. Major banks, insurance companies, and the Nairobi Securities Exchange create demand for finance professionals.

**Regional headquarters:** Many multinational companies use Nairobi as their East Africa regional headquarters - creating demand for management, marketing and operations professionals.

**Cost of living:** Nairobi is more expensive than Lagos for some items but significantly cheaper than European or Gulf cities. A comfortable professional lifestyle costs approximately USD 1,500 - 2,500/month.
