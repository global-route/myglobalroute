Cape Verde is an archipelago of ten islands in the Atlantic Ocean, 570 kilometres off the coast of Senegal. It is politically stable, Portuguese-speaking, and has been actively positioning itself as a destination for remote workers since 2021. For African remote workers, it offers the lowest cost of living among the three African digital nomad destinations covered on this platform.

## Entry - visa-free for Nigerians

Nigerian nationals can enter Cape Verde without a visa for up to 30 days. For longer stays, you apply for the Remote Work Visa.

## Remote Work Visa

Cape Verde's Remote Work Visa (Visto de Trabalho Remoto) is issued by the Cape Verde Immigration Service (SEF-CV) for an initial period of 6 months, renewable for up to 2 years total.

**Requirements:**
- Valid passport
- Proof of remote employment or freelance income sourced outside Cape Verde
- Proof of accommodation in Cape Verde
- Health insurance
- Clean criminal record

**Income threshold:** Approximately EUR 1,500/month. This is the commonly cited figure - not a fixed published requirement. The SEF-CV assesses sufficient financial means on a case-by-case basis.

**What it allows:** Living and working remotely in Cape Verde. You cannot work for a Cape Verde-based employer on this visa.

**Application fee:** Approximately EUR 60-90.

**Primary source:** `https://www.sefcv.gov.cv/`

## Cost of living

Cape Verde is the most affordable of the three African digital nomad destinations (Mauritius, Cape Verde, Namibia).

| Item | Monthly cost (EUR) |
|---|---|
| 1-bedroom apartment | 300-500 |
| Food and groceries | 200-350 |
| Transport | 50-100 |
| Utilities and internet | 80-120 |
| **Total comfortable lifestyle** | **630-1,070** |

## Currency stability

The Cape Verdean Escudo (CVE) is pegged to the Euro at a fixed rate (1 EUR = 110.265 CVE). This means your EUR-denominated income has predictable purchasing power in Cape Verde - no currency risk.

## Time zone

UTC-1 (UTC+0 in summer). Good overlap with West African and European business hours.
