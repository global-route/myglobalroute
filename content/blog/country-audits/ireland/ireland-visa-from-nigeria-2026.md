# Ireland Visa from Nigeria: The 2026 Reality Check

## Quick Summary

- **Critical Skills Employment Permit minimum salary:** €38,000/year (most occupations)
- **Study visa living funds:** ~€7,000/year (indicative)
- **Stamp 4 PR pathway:** After 2 years on Critical Skills permit
- **Third Level Graduate Scheme:** 24-month stay-back permission after graduation
- **Official source:** [Immigration Service Delivery](https://www.irishimmigration.ie/)

**Evidence note:** Ireland does not publish visa approval rates by nationality. This guide covers documented requirements only.

---

## Critical Skills Employment Permit

Ireland's Critical Skills Employment Permit (CSEP) is the primary work route for skilled professionals from outside the EU. It is employer-sponsored but has no annual quota, which makes it more accessible than quota-based systems like Italy's Decreto Flussi.

### Requirements

- **Job offer:** Required from an Irish employer registered with the Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment
- **Minimum annual salary:** €38,000 for most occupations; €32,000 for occupations on the Critical Skills Occupations List
- **Occupation:** Must be in an eligible occupation - the Critical Skills Occupations List is published by the Department of Enterprise
- **Qualifications:** Degree-level qualification relevant to the role, or 3+ years of relevant experience for certain occupations
- **Official source:** [Department of Enterprise - Critical Skills Employment Permit](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/critical-skills-employment-permit/)

### Salary threshold detail

The €38,000 threshold applies to the majority of eligible roles. For roles specifically listed on the Critical Skills Occupations List - which includes ICT professionals, engineers, healthcare workers and certain financial roles - the threshold is €32,000. Your employer will confirm which threshold applies to your role.

### Path to stamp 4

After 2 years on a Critical Skills Employment Permit, you can apply for stamp 4 permission. Stamp 4 allows you to:

- Work for any employer without a permit
- Be self-employed
- Access most public services

Stamp 4 is not permanent residency, but it is the step immediately before it. After 5 years of legal residence in Ireland (including your permit years), you can apply for long-term residency.

### What this permit does not cover

- General Employment Permits (for roles not on the Critical Skills list, with a salary below €38,000)
- Intra-company transfers
- Self-employment routes

## Study Visa (Third Level)

Ireland requires a study visa (D visa) for any course longer than 3 months at a recognised Irish institution.

### Requirements

- **Acceptance letter:** From a recognised Irish higher-education institution
- **Living funds:** Approximately €7,000/year (indicative - no single fixed threshold is published; the visa officer assesses whether funds are sufficient for the full course duration)
- **Tuition fees:** Separate - typically €9,000-€25,000/year for international students at Irish universities
- **Health insurance:** Required
- **Official source:** [Immigration Service Delivery - Study](https://www.irishimmigration.ie/coming-to-study-in-ireland/)

### Third Level Graduate Scheme

After completing a degree at an Irish institution, you can apply for the Third Level Graduate Scheme, which gives you:

- **24 months** to remain in Ireland and seek employment (for degree-level graduates)
- **12 months** for higher diploma or postgraduate diploma graduates
- Permission to work full-time during this period

If you secure a job that qualifies for a Critical Skills Employment Permit during this period, you can transition directly to the permit without leaving Ireland.

## Key barriers for Nigerian applicants

These are documented process barriers - not approval rate statistics:

1. **Financial proof:** Bank statements must clearly show funds are genuinely accessible. Statements showing sudden large deposits close to the application date are scrutinised.
2. **Genuine student/worker intent:** The visa officer assesses whether your primary purpose is study or work, and whether you intend to comply with visa conditions.
3. **Employer registration:** For the Critical Skills permit, your employer must be registered with the Department of Enterprise. Not all employers are registered - confirm this before accepting a job offer.
4. **Document authentication:** Transcripts and certificates must be authenticated. The Irish Naturalisation and Immigration Service (INIS) may request additional verification.
