# USA Visa from Nigeria: F-1 Student and Work Visa Guide 2026

## Quick Summary

- **F-1 student visa:** Proof of sufficient funds for full programme cost (tuition + living) required - no fixed federal threshold; each institution sets its own cost of attendance
- **H-1B work visa:** Employer-sponsored, subject to annual lottery cap (85,000 visas/year)
- **O-1 extraordinary ability:** No cap, but requires documented extraordinary achievement
- **Official source:** [US Department of State - Travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas.html)

**Evidence note:** The US State Department does not publish visa approval rates by nationality. This guide covers documented requirements only.

---

## F-1 Student Visa

The F-1 visa is the primary route for international students at US universities and colleges.

### Requirements

- **I-20 form:** Issued by a SEVP-certified school confirming your admission and programme details - required before applying for the F-1 visa
- **Proof of funds:** You must demonstrate sufficient funds to cover your full cost of attendance (tuition + living expenses) for the first year, and evidence of continued funding for subsequent years. The required amount is set by each institution in the I-20 - there is no single federal minimum.
- **Typical cost of attendance:** USD 30,000-80,000/year at US universities (tuition + room + board + fees) - varies enormously by institution and location
- **Non-immigrant intent:** You must demonstrate ties to Nigeria and intent to return after completing your studies
- **SEVIS fee:** USD 350 paid before the visa interview

### Scholarships

US universities offer merit and need-based scholarships for international students. Fully-funded options include:
- **Fulbright Foreign Student Program** - covers tuition, living, travel
- **MasterCard Foundation Scholars Program** - at select US universities
- **Individual university scholarships** - vary by institution

Apply for scholarships before or alongside your university application - deadlines are typically 12-18 months before the programme start.

### Post-study work rights

- **OPT (Optional Practical Training):** 12 months of work authorisation after graduation in a field related to your degree
- **STEM OPT extension:** Additional 24 months (total 36 months) for STEM degree holders
- **H-1B:** After OPT, many graduates apply for H-1B employer sponsorship

## H-1B Work Visa

The H-1B is the primary work visa for professionals in specialty occupations (typically requiring a bachelor's degree or higher).

### Requirements

- **Employer sponsorship:** A US employer must file a petition on your behalf - you cannot self-petition
- **Specialty occupation:** The role must require at least a bachelor's degree in a specific field
- **Annual cap:** 85,000 H-1B visas per year (65,000 regular cap + 20,000 for US master's degree holders). Applications are subject to a lottery when demand exceeds the cap - which it has every year since 2014.
- **Prevailing wage:** The employer must pay at least the prevailing wage for the occupation and location

### H-1B lottery reality

The H-1B lottery means that even a qualified applicant with a valid job offer may not receive a visa in a given year. Multiple lottery entries are possible in subsequent years. This is a structural constraint, not a reflection of individual qualifications.

---

## O-1 Visa (Extraordinary Ability)

The O-1 visa is for individuals with extraordinary ability in science, arts, education, business or athletics.

### Requirements

- **Employer or agent sponsorship:** Required
- **Evidence of extraordinary ability:** Must meet at least 3 of 8 criteria (awards, published material, high salary, critical role, original contributions, etc.)
- **No annual cap:** Unlike H-1B, O-1 is not subject to a lottery

The O-1 is a viable route for accomplished Nigerian professionals - researchers, artists, tech founders - who can document their achievements.

---

## Key barriers for Nigerian applicants

1. **Non-immigrant intent:** The F-1 visa requires demonstrating strong ties to Nigeria. Weak ties (no property, no family, no employment history) increase refusal risk.
2. **Funds documentation:** Bank statements must clearly show the required funds are genuinely accessible and have been held for a reasonable period.
3. **H-1B lottery:** The cap and lottery system mean employer sponsorship alone does not guarantee a visa.
4. **Visa interview:** F-1 interviews at the US Embassy in Abuja are required - book early, slots fill weeks in advance.
