Qatar is one of the most active Gulf work destinations for African professionals, particularly in construction, healthcare, hospitality and domestic services. The 2020 Kafala reform changed some of the rules - but not all of them. Here is what official sources confirm.

## How the Qatar work permit works

Qatar's work permit system is employer-initiated. Your employer applies to the Ministry of Labour for a work permit before you can apply for a visa.

**Step-by-step process:**
1. Employer applies for work permit from Qatar Ministry of Labour
2. Ministry approves (or rejects) the work permit application
3. You apply for an employment visa at the Qatar embassy in Nigeria
4. You enter Qatar and complete a medical examination
5. You receive your Residence Permit (RP) - valid 1-2 years, renewable

**Processing time:** 2-6 weeks after employer application. Claims of "visa out in 3-5 days" are not consistent with official Ministry of Labour processing timelines.

**Primary source:** `https://www.mol.gov.qa/`

## The Kafala reform - what actually changed in 2020

Qatar's 2020 labour reforms were significant but partial. Here is what changed and what did not:

| What changed | What did not change |
|---|---|
| Workers can change jobs without employer permission after 1 year of employment | Employer still initiates the work permit |
| Workers can leave Qatar without exit permit (abolished) | Visa status still tied to employer |
| Minimum wage introduced (QAR 1,000/month) | Kafala sponsorship system still exists |
| Workers can file complaints with Ministry of Labour | Enforcement varies in practice |

**The honest picture:** The reforms are real and meaningful. But the system is still employer-led. Your legal status in Qatar is still linked to your employer. If you leave a job before your contract ends, your visa status changes.

## Minimum wage - what it means in practice

Qatar's minimum wage of QAR 1,000/month (approximately USD 275) is the legal floor. It applies to all workers regardless of nationality.

**What this means for African workers:**
- Any employer offering less than QAR 1,000/month is violating Qatar law
- Most skilled positions (healthcare, engineering, IT, finance) pay significantly above the minimum
- Domestic workers and construction workers are more likely to be at or near the minimum
- Food and accommodation provided by the employer are separate from the cash wage

**When an agent quotes you "salary 650k NGN":** QAR 1,000/month converts to approximately NGN 412,000/month at current rates. A salary of NGN 650,000/month would be approximately QAR 1,580/month - above the minimum but at the lower end for skilled work. Verify the actual QAR figure in the employment contract.

## What to verify before accepting a Qatar job offer

> Get the employment contract in writing before paying any fees. The contract must specify: salary in QAR, working hours, accommodation arrangements, and what happens if the employer terminates early.

**Documents required:**
- Valid passport (minimum 6 months validity)
- Passport photographs
- Educational certificates (attested)
- Medical fitness certificate
- Police clearance certificate
