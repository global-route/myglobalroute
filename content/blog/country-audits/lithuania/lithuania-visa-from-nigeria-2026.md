Lithuania is a Baltic EU and Schengen member state with a growing economy and documented labour shortages in IT, engineering and healthcare. For Nigerian applicants, it offers three publishable pathways: a study residence permit, a work residence permit and the EU Blue Card for highly qualified professionals.

## Study residence permit

**Who it is for:** Nigerian students accepted at a recognised Lithuanian higher education institution.

**Key requirements:**
- Acceptance letter from a Lithuanian university or college recognised by the Ministry of Education, Science and Sport
- Valid Nigerian passport (minimum 15 months validity beyond intended stay)
- Proof of sufficient financial means — EUR 500/month is the practical benchmark used by consulates
- Health insurance covering the full period of study
- Proof of accommodation in Lithuania

**Tuition fees:** EUR 1,500 to EUR 5,000/year for non-EU students at public universities. English-taught programmes are available at the University of Vilnius, Kaunas University of Technology, Vilnius Gediminas Technical University and others.

**Cost of living:**
- Vilnius: EUR 500-700/month (accommodation, food, transport)
- Kaunas, Klaipeda: EUR 400-600/month

**Work rights:** Up to 20 hours per week during term time without a separate work permit. Full-time during holidays.

**Processing time:** 1-2 months via the Migration Department online portal (migracija.lrv.lt). Apply after arriving on a national visa (D-type) issued by the Lithuanian consulate.

**Path to PR:** 5 years of continuous legal residence → permanent residence permit.

Primary source: [migracija.lrv.lt/en/services-for-foreigners/temporary-residence-permit/for-studies](https://migracija.lrv.lt/en/services-for-foreigners/temporary-residence-permit/for-studies/)

## Work residence permit

**Who it is for:** Nigerian professionals with a confirmed job offer from a Lithuanian employer.

**Key requirements:**
- Valid employment contract from a Lithuanian employer
- The employer must obtain a work permit on your behalf from the Lithuanian Labour Exchange before you apply for the residence permit
- Labour market test: the employer must demonstrate no suitable Lithuanian or EU candidate was available (exceptions apply for shortage occupations)

**Salary:** Minimum wage EUR 1,038/month gross. Skilled roles typically EUR 1,500-3,000/month gross.

**Processing time:** 2-3 months. The employer applies for the work permit first; you then apply for the national visa and residence permit.

Primary source: [migracija.lrv.lt/en/services-for-foreigners/temporary-residence-permit/for-work](https://migracija.lrv.lt/en/services-for-foreigners/temporary-residence-permit/for-work/)

## EU Blue Card

**Who it is for:** Highly qualified Nigerian professionals with a job offer meeting the salary threshold.

**Salary threshold:** Gross annual salary of at least EUR 19,572 (1.5 times the Lithuanian average gross annual salary, 2026 figure). This is approximately EUR 1,631/month gross.

**Qualification requirement:** University degree (bachelor's or higher) or at least 5 years of equivalent professional experience.

**Labour market test:** Exempt — no labour market test required for EU Blue Card applications.

**Validity:** 2 years, renewable. After 18 months, you can move to another EU member state under the EU Blue Card mobility rules.

**Path to PR:** 5 years of legal residence in Lithuania (or 2 years with EU Blue Card + 3 years in another EU state).

Primary source: [migracija.lrv.lt/en/services-for-foreigners/temporary-residence-permit/eu-blue-card](https://migracija.lrv.lt/en/services-for-foreigners/temporary-residence-permit/eu-blue-card/)

## Honest assessment

Lithuania is a low-profile but genuinely accessible EU destination. The costs are among the lowest in the Schengen area and the EU Blue Card salary threshold (EUR 19,572/year) is the lowest in the EU — significantly below Germany (EUR 45,300) or the Netherlands (EUR 46,107). Key practical considerations:

- **Language:** Most public university programmes are in Lithuanian. English-taught programmes exist but are fewer than in Poland or the Czech Republic. Verify the language of instruction before applying.
- **IT and tech demand:** Lithuania has a growing fintech and tech sector (Vilnius is home to several major bank tech hubs). Nigerian software engineers and data professionals are well-positioned for the EU Blue Card route.
- **Consulate access:** Lithuania does not have a consulate in Nigeria. Nigerian applicants apply at the Lithuanian embassy in Accra (Ghana) or through a Schengen visa centre. Factor in travel or courier costs.
- **Recognition of qualifications:** Lithuanian employers may require ENIC/NARIC equivalency for Nigerian degrees. Budget 4-8 weeks.

## Common rejection reasons

- Insufficient proof of financial means
- Acceptance letter from an unrecognised institution
- Failure to demonstrate ties to Nigeria
- Incomplete employer documentation for work permit applications
