Mauritius is the most developed financial centre in Africa and one of the most accessible destinations for African professionals. It has three distinct routes depending on your profile: remote worker, employed professional, or investor/business owner.

## Entry - visa-free for Nigerians

Nigerian nationals can enter Mauritius without a visa for up to 90 days. This is for tourism and short business visits. Working or residing long-term requires a formal authorisation.

## Route 1 - Premium Visa (remote workers)

The Premium Visa allows remote workers and freelancers to live in Mauritius for up to 1 year, renewable. No fixed minimum income threshold is published by the EDB.

**Requirements:**
- Valid passport
- Proof of remote employment or self-employment with income sourced outside Mauritius
- Proof of accommodation in Mauritius
- Health insurance
- Clean criminal record

**What it allows:** Living and working remotely in Mauritius. You cannot work for a Mauritius-based employer on this visa.

**Tax:** Mauritius operates a territorial tax system. Foreign-sourced income is not taxed. The 15% income tax rate applies only to Mauritius-sourced income.

**Primary source:** `https://www.edbmauritius.org/`

## Route 2 - Occupation Permit (employed professionals and investors)

The Occupation Permit is for foreign nationals employed by a Mauritian company or running a Mauritian business.

**Professional category:**
- Job offer from a Mauritian employer
- Minimum monthly salary: MUR 60,000 (approximately USD 1,300)
- Valid for 3 years, renewable

**Investor category:**
- Minimum investment: USD 50,000
- Minimum annual turnover: USD 100,000 from year 2
- Valid for 3 years, renewable

**Path to permanent residence:** After 3 years on an Occupation Permit, you can apply for a 10-year residence permit.

## Route 3 - Global Business Licence (investors and holding companies)

The GBL is Mauritius's holding company structure for investments into Africa and Asia. It is not a personal visa - it is a corporate structure.

**Key features:**
- Approximately 3% effective corporate tax rate on foreign-sourced income (partial exemption on 80% of specified foreign income)
- 46 double taxation treaties including India, China, France, UK, South Africa
- Requires genuine substance: 2 resident directors, principal bank account in Mauritius, board meetings in Mauritius

**Who uses it:** Private equity funds, family offices, multinational companies using Mauritius as a regional holding structure.

**Annual cost:** Approximately USD 9,000-23,000 including licence fee, management company fees, and audit.

For a full breakdown, see the [Mauritius Global Business Licence guide](/blog/playbooks/business/mauritius-global-business-licence-2026/).

## Why Mauritius for African professionals

**Strongest African passport:** The Mauritian passport provides visa-free access to approximately 156 countries including the EU Schengen area - the strongest passport in Africa.

**Financial centre:** Mauritius has a well-developed banking sector, stock exchange, and financial services industry. It is the preferred jurisdiction for holding companies investing into Sub-Saharan Africa.

**Connectivity:** Reliable island-wide 4G and fibre broadband. Co-working spaces available in Port Louis and Grand Baie.

**Time zone:** UTC+4 - good overlap with European morning hours and East African business hours.
