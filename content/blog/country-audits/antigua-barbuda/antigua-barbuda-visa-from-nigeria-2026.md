Antigua and Barbuda is a small twin-island nation in the Eastern Caribbean. It has a population of around 100,000, a tourism-dependent economy, and a legitimate hospitality and construction sector that does employ foreign workers. It also appears regularly in Nigerian Telegram groups and Facebook pages with offers that do not match how the official immigration system works.

This post covers what the official process looks like, what the job-on-arrival pattern actually means in practice, and the specific questions to ask before engaging any agent offering Antigua placements.

Primary source: Antigua and Barbuda Immigration Department, immigration.gov.ag; Department of Labour, labour.gov.ag

## The official work permit process

Antigua and Barbuda requires foreign nationals to hold a valid work permit before beginning employment. The process works as follows:

1. A prospective employer in Antigua identifies a foreign worker they want to hire
2. The employer applies to the Department of Labour for a work permit on behalf of the worker
3. The Department of Labour assesses whether a suitably qualified Antiguan national is available for the role
4. If approved, the work permit is issued to the employer, who then provides documentation to the worker
5. The worker applies for the appropriate entry visa using the work permit documentation

The employer pays the work permit fee. The worker does not pay upfront fees to a third-party agent for a work permit.

Official processing time is several weeks minimum. Any agent claiming 3-5 day processing is not using the official process.

## What "job on arrival" actually means

The phrase "job on arrival" is not an official Antigua and Barbuda visa category. It does not appear in the Immigration Act or the Department of Labour's published procedures.

In practice, agents using this phrase are typically describing one of two things:

**Pattern 1 — Visitor visa with informal job search.** The agent arranges a visitor visa. The worker travels to Antigua on a visitor visa, which does not permit work. The agent claims the worker will find a job once there. This is illegal — working on a visitor visa is a violation of Antiguan immigration law and can result in deportation.

**Pattern 2 — Unverified employer placement.** The agent claims to have a relationship with an Antiguan employer who will hire the worker on arrival. No written contract is provided before travel. The worker pays upfront fees. On arrival, the employer either does not exist, the terms have changed, or the worker is placed in conditions that differ significantly from what was described.

Both patterns have been documented in the Nigerian diaspora community. Neither represents a legitimate pathway.

## The hospitality sector — what is real

Antigua does have genuine demand for hospitality workers, particularly in hotels and resorts. The tourism sector is the primary driver of the economy. Some employers do recruit internationally.

The legitimate version of this looks like:

- A named employer (a specific hotel or resort, not a generic "hospitality company")
- A written job offer on company letterhead before any fees are paid
- The employer initiating the work permit process through the Department of Labour
- No upfront fees to a third-party agent

If an agent cannot name the specific employer and provide written documentation before you pay anything, the offer is not verified.

## Visa requirements for Nigerian passport holders

Nigerian passport holders require a visa to enter Antigua and Barbuda. A standard visitor visa:

- Does not permit employment
- Is typically issued for 30 days, extendable
- Requires proof of accommodation, return ticket, and sufficient funds

A work permit is separate from the entry visa and must be arranged by the employer before travel.

## Questions to ask any agent offering Antigua placements

Before engaging any agent offering work in Antigua and Barbuda:

1. What is the name of the specific employer?
2. Can you provide the employer's business registration number?
3. Has the employer applied for a work permit through the Antigua Department of Labour?
4. Can you provide a written job offer on the employer's letterhead before I pay any fees?
5. What is the written service agreement between me and your agency?

If any of these questions cannot be answered with documentation, do not pay.

## Summary

Antigua and Barbuda has a legitimate hospitality and construction sector that employs foreign workers through a formal work permit process. The job-on-arrival offers circulating in Nigerian agent networks do not reflect how that process works. The legitimate route requires a named employer, a work permit applied for by that employer, and no upfront fees to a third-party agent.
