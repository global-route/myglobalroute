No-tuition and low-cost sweet spots

Germany — Study visa

Public universities in Germany charge no tuition for most programmes. The main financial barrier is the blocked account.

  • Blocked account required: €11,208 (living funds proof — source: Federal Foreign Office)
  • Tuition: €0 at public universities (semester admin fee ~€150–€350 applies)
  • Admission offer required: Yes — from a recognised German university
  • Post-study: 18-month job search visa after graduation
  • Part-time work: 120 full days or 240 half days per year permitted

Italy — Higher-education study visa + DSU scholarship

Italy's DSU (Diritto allo Studio Universitario) scholarship covers tuition, accommodation and a living stipend for eligible low-income students.

  • Admission offer required: Yes — from a recognised Italian university via Universitaly portal
  • Minimum funds (self-funded): Approximately €6,167/year living costs — source: Italian MFA
  • DSU scholarship: Covers tuition, housing and provides a stipend (~€6,000–€7,000/year) for eligible applicants — eligibility is income-tested using ISEE/ISEEU calculation
  • Key requirement: Family income documentation must be legalised at the Italian embassy in Nigeria
View Italy pathway →

Hungary — Stipendium Hungaricum scholarship

Hungary's government scholarship programme covers tuition, accommodation and a monthly stipend for African students.

  • Admission offer required: Yes — from a recognised Hungarian university
  • Scholarship coverage: Tuition, dormitory accommodation, health insurance, monthly stipend
  • Application route: Via the Stipendium Hungaricum portal — applications open annually, typically October–January
  • Self-funded route: Residence permit for study requires admission offer and demonstrable funds
View Hungary pathway →

Canada — Study permit

Canada requires a study permit for courses longer than 6 months. Living funds are required in addition to tuition.

  • Admission offer required: Yes — from a Designated Learning Institution (DLI)
  • Minimum living funds (family size 1, outside Quebec): CAD 23,448/year — source: IRCC
  • Tuition: Separate — varies by institution and programme (typically CAD 15,000–30,000/year for international students)
  • Post-study: Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) up to 3 years after graduation
View Canada pathway →

Japan — Student status of residence

Japan has a large number of English-taught programmes and government scholarships (MEXT) for international students.

  • Admission offer required: Yes — from a qualifying Japanese educational institution
  • MEXT scholarship: Covers tuition, living allowance (~¥117,000/month) and travel — competitive, apply via Japanese embassy
  • Self-funded: Funds proof required; living costs approximately ¥100,000–¥150,000/month depending on city
  • Part-time work: Up to 28 hours/week permitted with permission
View Japan pathway →

South Korea — D-2 student visa

South Korea offers government scholarships (KGSP) and has a growing number of English-taught programmes.

  • Admission offer required: Yes — from a Korean university accredited by the Ministry of Education
  • KGSP scholarship: Covers tuition, living allowance, Korean language training — apply via Korean embassy
  • Self-funded minimum funds: Approximately USD 9,000/year living costs
  • Part-time work: Up to 20 hours/week during semester with permission

Budget planning guide

These are planning ranges only. Actual costs depend on your specific institution, city and circumstances. All figures are approximate.

Under $5,000 available

Best options: Italy DSU scholarship or Hungary Stipendium Hungaricum. Your cash covers application fees, document legalisation and travel. The scholarship covers everything else. These routes require strong academic records and early applications (12+ months ahead).

$5,000–$12,000 available

Best option: Germany blocked account route. €11,208 (~$12,000) covers the blocked account. Zero tuition means your ongoing costs are living expenses, which part-time work (up to 20 hours/week) can cover. Apply to 5–8 universities to maximise admission chances.

$12,000–$20,000 available

Options open: Germany, Finland (€800/month living funds), Norway (NOK 170,368/year), Sweden (SEK 10,656/month). Nordic countries have low or no tuition at public universities but higher living costs than Germany.

$20,000+ available

All study pathways open. Canada, Australia, New Zealand, UK, Ireland become viable. Focus on academic fit, post-study work rights and long-term residency pathways rather than just upfront cost.

Key steps for any study application

  1. Choose your destination and confirm the official requirements on the government portal
  2. Research universities and programmes — apply to multiple institutions
  3. Prepare language test (IELTS, TOEFL, TestDaF, or equivalent)
  4. Gather and authenticate documents (transcripts, certificates, passport)
  5. Secure funds or scholarship confirmation before applying for a visa
  6. Book embassy appointment early — slots fill 6–8 weeks out in Lagos and Abuja
  7. Apply for visa with complete documentation

Start at least 12 months before your intended start date. Document authentication (apostille, sworn translation, APS for Germany) takes time.
