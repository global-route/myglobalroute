Partnerships

Work With Us

We are the evidence layer. You are the service layer. Together we serve the client honestly.

The opportunity

The African migration services market has a trust problem. Unverified claims circulate in Telegram groups and WhatsApp channels - approval rates that authorities never published, processing times that are not official, fees that are agent pricing presented as government fees. Applicants cannot tell the difference between a legitimate agency and a fraudulent one.

MyPathway2Global is the reference point that does not exist yet. Not a directory of agents. Not a comparison site. A primary-source intelligence layer that anyone - applicant or agency - can cite.

A legitimate agency that can say "our pathway data is verified by MyPathway2Global" has a genuine differentiator in a market where no one else can say that. The partnership is worth more than any commission structure.

Three partnership types

Tier A - Verified Practitioner

Who: Licensed immigration lawyers, regulated immigration advisers, accredited consultants operating in the African market.

What you contribute: Primary-source sign-off on pathway records for countries within your practice area. Your name and firm appear on the pathway record as the verified reviewer. You replace the current manual source sign-off process.

What you receive: A Verified Practitioner badge on the platform, a practitioner profile page linked from relevant pathway records, and referral traffic from users who need legal advice beyond what the platform provides.

Requirements: Current regulatory authorisation in your jurisdiction. Willingness to review pathway records against primary sources. Disclosure of any conflicts of interest.

Tier B - Agency Partner

Who: Travel agencies, relocation firms, HR platforms and migration service providers operating in the African market.

What you contribute: Distribution - you route clients to the platform as the verified source of pathway information. You may co-brand content and embed platform pathway data in your client communications.

What you receive: An Agency Partner badge and listing in the partner directory, co-branding rights subject to accuracy requirements, and access to structured pathway data for the countries you serve.

Requirements: You must not make claims about pathways that contradict the platform's published data. You must not use the platform's name to imply endorsement of your specific services. You must maintain a written agreement with clients before commencing services.

Tier C - Content Partner

Who: Research organisations, universities, diaspora organisations, professional associations and media organisations.

What you contribute: Country audits, playbooks, case studies or policy updates co-produced with the platform. All content goes through the same evidence gate as platform-generated content.

What you receive: Co-authorship attribution on published content, brand visibility and SEO attribution.

The trust firewall

All partnerships are subject to the trust firewall. These rules are non-negotiable:

  • No commercial influence on pathway rankings. No partner payment, referral fee or commission arrangement may influence which pathways are recommended or how they are ranked.
  • No fabricated claims. The platform does not publish approval rates by nationality, processing time guarantees, success rate statistics, or "zero deposit" claims where a deposit is required. Partners may not publish these claims and attribute them to MyPathway2Global.
  • No suppression of adverse evidence. If a pathway has a documented blocker, that information is published. It is not removed because a partner sells that pathway.
  • No PII in the platform. The platform does not collect, store or transmit passport copies, CVs, bank statements or any personally identifiable information.
  • Clear disclosure. Any partner whose content appears on the platform must be disclosed as such.

Partnerships that violate any of these rules are terminated immediately.

The due diligence process

Before any partnership is formalised, we review:

  • Regulatory status and authorisation (for practitioners)
  • Marketing materials for fabricated claims
  • History of unresolved client complaints
  • Data protection practices

A written partnership agreement is required before any co-branding or data access. The agreement includes explicit acceptance of the trust firewall and an immediate termination clause for violations.
